# How Evidence Attaches to a Relationship

As a graph grows, accurate Nodes stop being enough on their own. The relationship between two Nodes needs to be trustworthy too.

```text
A ── ATTACHES_TO ──> B
```

This one line, by itself, doesn't say why the relationship exists. So relationships need evidence too.

## Edge and Evidence Are Different Information

A relationship is structure.

```text
A ──relation──> B
```

Evidence is the material that supports that structure.

```text
A ──relation──> B
       │
       └── evidence → source text
```

A relationship can be thought of as at least two layers:

```text
Relation
   +
Evidence
```

A relationship existing and a relationship being verified are not the same state.

## Evidence Can Attach to a Sentence

VINC can store evidence and confidence on a relationship.

```text
Concept
   │
   └── ATTACHES_TO
          ├─ evidence
          └─ confidence
```

With evidence in place, the graph moves past just saying "these are connected" — it can trace which sentence actually backs that connection.

## A Relationship Has Provenance Too

When a Concept gets extracted from a Document, a link forms between the two.

```text
Document
   │
   └── MENTIONS
          │
        Concept
```

If that Concept then connects to a Record, another relationship forms.

```text
Concept
   │
   └── ATTACHES_TO
          │
        Record
```

At that point, it should be possible to trace backward from a final piece of material to where it came from.

```text
Answer Material
      ↓
Record
      ↓
Concept
      ↓
Document
      ↓
Source Text
```

This is the core of provenance.

## Unsupported Relationships Need Different Handling

Not every relationship carries the same certainty. Some have direct textual support. Some get generated by system structure. Some remain unverified.

So a relationship's existence has to be kept separate from its evidence.

```text
Relation exists
      ≠
Relation is evidenced
```

This distinction matters when reading a graph.

## Confidence Is Not Evidence

If Evidence explains why a connection was made, Confidence expresses how much trust that connection has earned.

```text
evidence
= what supports it

confidence
= how much trust is placed in that support
```

Merge the two, and support gets tangled with judgment. So relationship data keeps them as separate fields on purpose.

## Automatically Generated Relationships Still Need Verification

Extracting Concepts from documents, or building graph relationships automatically, is convenient — but automation alone doesn't make a relationship true. A long document title or a vague phrase can end up linked to the wrong Concept.

So generation and verification need to stay separate stages.

```text
Source
  ↓
Extraction
  ↓
Candidate Relation
  ↓
Evidence Check
  ↓
Usable Relation
```

This split matters more, not less, as the graph gets more automated.

## Provenance Is a Navigation Path

Evidence isn't just a list of sources. It should be possible to trace backward through how a piece of answer material got built.

```text
Question
   ↓
Resolved Node
   ↓
Relation
   ↓
Record
   ↓
Evidence
   ↓
Source Document
```

With this path, an Agent's answer can be reviewed after the fact. Without it — relationships with no evidence — there's no easy way to check the result again.

## Evidence Becomes Part of the Graph

Evidence, in the end, doesn't only live in documents. Because a relationship itself can carry evidence, the graph expands from a plain list of connections into a knowledge structure that carries its own support.

```text
Node
   +
Edge
   +
Evidence
   +
Confidence
```

This matters again later, once time and View enter the picture. Connect when a relationship was valid, and where it's visible, and the Graph stops being a static structure — it becomes a data structure with time and space built in.
