# african-aesthetics

## 0. Declaration of Self-Critique — *the strongest obligation*

This note falls in the area of the **strongest self-critique obligation**:
- **Africa ≠ a single entity — the most diverse continent** (54 countries, 1,500+ languages, multilayered religious, formal, and oral traditions). This note is *only a partial* treatment, centered on *West African Yoruba, Akan, Mali, and Senegal*
- **The direct traces of colonial violence**: the slave trade (1500–1888, roughly 12.5 million forcibly transported), colonial partition (the Berlin Conference, 1884), colonial museum plunder (5,000+ Benin Bronzes, Egyptian artifacts, Congolese masks — the British Museum, the Louvre, Berlin, etc. — *restitution movements currently* underway)
- **The violence of Western absorption**: Picasso's *Demoiselles d'Avignon* (1907) borrowing African masks → grounding Cubism — *without acknowledgment of the source* (critiqued by Said's *Orientalism* and Hal Foster's *primitive trace*)
- **Translation limits**: orisha, àshẹ, sankofa, ubuntu, duende — essential loss in translation
- **The diaspora = colonialism + resistance, survival, re-creation**: candomblé, santería, vodou, samba, jazz, blues, hip-hop = *cultural recovery and re-creation* in the face of violence
- **This note is a *beginning stage*** — an obligation for additional notes (Dohun's decision)

## ① Definition and Core Concepts

### Sub-Saharan Africa — *the separation of fields is essentially absent*

**Core proposition** (the consensus of Robert Farris Thompson, Senghor, Achebe): African aesthetics does *not separate art, religion, the everyday, politics, and society* — integration is both the starting point and the essence.

### Major Traditions

1. **Yoruba (Nigeria, Benin, Togo)**:
   - The **Orisha** faith — 400+ deities (Oxalá, Yemoja, Ogun, Shango, Eshu, Oxum, etc.), each with its own color, music, rhythm, dance, food, and divination
   - The integrated mode: music (the batá drums, shekere) + dance + mask (egungun) + sculpture (orisha representations) + poetry (oríkì praise poetry) + ritual + food + costume
   - **àshẹ** — *life force, the power to make things happen* — the power residing in every thing, word, and act. Parallel to, and different from, the Chinese qi (jingju)
   - The **Egungun** masquerade — the masked dance of ancestral spirits
   - The Benin Bronzes (1300s–1897) — refined brass sculpture, the punitive-expedition plunder, the restitution movement

2. **Akan (Ghana, Côte d'Ivoire)**:
   - **Sankofa** — "go back and fetch it" — the aesthetics of learning from the past; the bird (an Adinkra symbol)
   - The **Adinkra** symbols — 60+ visual signs (textiles, stamps, ceramics) — visualizing proverbs and philosophy
   - **Kente** cloth — color and pattern conveying meaning (the Asante royal tradition)

3. **Dogon (Mali)**:
   - 78 kinds of masks (the sigi ritual)
   - The integration of cosmology + music + dance + mask + sculpture
   - Marcel Griaule's 1930s anthropology (though itself a target of the *Western reduction* critique)

4. **Mali, Senegal, Guinea — the Griot** (the oral-tradition poet-musician):
   - The Mande *jeli* — oral history and genealogy + the kora (a 21-string harp) + song + dance + social mediation
   - 30+ generations of hereditary families — parallel to, and different from, the Japanese iemoto and the Indian caste
   - Preserving the history of the **Mali Empire** (1235–1670) — the Sundiata epic (oral)
   - The contemporary: Salif Keita, Toumani Diabaté, Youssou N'Dour — global spread

5. **Polyrhythm** — full scale among the Ewe (Ghana, Togo), Ashanti, and Yoruba:
   - 2–5+ different meters simultaneously (3 + 2 + 4, etc.) — an essential difference from the West's single meter
   - The master drum + supporting drums + handclaps + dance — field integration
   - Applied in Aaron Copland's *Music for the Theatre* (1934) / Steve Reich's *Drumming* (1971 — after 6 months of study in Ghana)
   - **The nervous system of jazz, blues, rock, funk, and hip-hop**

6. **Mbira and kalimba** (Zimbabwe, Malawi) — Shona ritual music

7. **Southern Africa — Zulu, Xhosa, Sotho** — choral song, warrior dance, myth

### The Diaspora (colonialism and slavery + resistance and re-creation)

#### Latin America

1. **Brazilian Candomblé** (1500s–) — Yoruba + Bantu + Catholic syncretism:
   - Orisha → Saints (Yemoja = Maria, Oxalá = Jesus, Ogun = St. George, etc.)
   - The terreiro temple, percussion, dance, song, ritual, food, costume
   - The Mãe-de-santo (mother-priestess) — female spiritual authority
   - **Candomblé centered in Bahia (Salvador) — direct African → Brazilian preservation** (the recovery of religion, art, and identity within colonial violence)

2. **Cuban Santería** (Lukumí) — Yoruba + Catholicism — an input to Wifredo Lam's painting

3. **Haitian Vodou** (Vodun) — Fon (Benin) + Catholicism — the spiritual engine of the Haitian Revolution (1791–1804)

4. **The musical diaspora**:
   - **Samba** (Brazil) — Africa + Portugal — established from 1916, the 1928 carnival
   - **Bossa nova** (1958– — Tom Jobim, João Gilberto)
   - **Tango** (Argentina, Uruguay) — Africa + Europe + the gaucho — late 19th century
   - **Salsa** (Cuba, Puerto Rico, New York) — 1960s–
   - **Reggae** (Jamaica) — Bob Marley
   - **Capoeira** (the Brazilian martial art–dance–music–game integration) — a tool of enslaved African resistance; legalized after the 1888 abolition

#### The United States (North America)

5. **The blues** (1860s–) — the Mississippi Delta, after emancipation
6. **Jazz** (1900s–) — New Orleans — polyrhythm + improvisation + European harmony — the decisive influence on 20th-century music
7. **Gospel, R&B, soul, funk, hip-hop** — accumulating
8. **Hip-hop** (1973– — the Bronx — DJ Kool Herc) — music + poetry (rap) + the visual (graffiti) + dance (breakdancing) = **the modern integrated medium** + global (1980s–)

### Core Aesthetic Concepts

1. **àshẹ** (Yoruba — life force, the power of realization)
2. **ubuntu** (Bantu — the communal aesthetic of "I am because we are")
3. **sankofa** (Akan — learning by returning)
4. **duende** (Spain and Latin America — the deep-soul aesthetic sense — Lorca's 1933 *Theory and Play of the Duende*) — African influence acknowledged
5. **negritude** (Senghor, 1930s) — the Black identity and aesthetics movement
6. **call and response** — shared across the blues, jazz, gospel, and African ritual
7. **groove and swing** — micro-rhythmic variation (jazz, funk)

## ③ 🔴 Simultaneous 4-Axis Analysis (R-11 — N=13)

### Axis 1 — Cognition (the cognition of àshẹ, call-response, polyrhythm)

- **àshẹ cognition** = cognition of life force — parallel to, and different from, the Chinese qi (jingju), the Indian prāṇa, and the Japanese yūgen (an emphasis on realization and the power of the word)
- **Call-and-response** = social cognition — the integration of *I* and *we* (ubuntu) — the apex of mirror neurons + ToM (neuroaesthetics)
- **Polyrhythm cognition** = *simultaneous multi-meter processing* — challenging Patel's single-meter hypothesis (neuroaesthetics, music-foundations). Neurologically: bilateral simultaneity, cerebellar synchronization — unresolved in full scale
- **Sankofa** = cognition of temporal return — the integration of past-present-future (different from Western linear time)
- **Hip-hop's integrated cognition** = music (beat) + poetry (rap) + the visual (graffiti) + dance (b-boying) simultaneously — mirror neurons + DMN + ToM + the reward circuit, 4 at once

> **🧠 Axis decision memo (cognition)**: àshẹ + call-response + polyrhythm + sankofa + the hip-hop integration = 5 dimensions. Non-Western integrated cognitive dimensions accumulating (India 4 + Japan 5 + East Asia 5 + Africa 5 = 19 non-Western cognitive dimensions). The 19 non-Western dimensions integrated into the full-scale answer to Q1.

### Axis 2 — Evolution (the *most careful* application of evolution)

- **Humanity's origin = Africa** (200,000+ years — Homo sapiens) — the starting point of evolutionary aesthetics is Africa
- But 19th-century evolutionism was a *tool for justifying colonial violence* (Spencer's social Darwinism, racism) — the application is *most cautious*
- **Modern evolutionary aesthetics (Dutton, Pinker)**: humanity's evolutionary environment = the Pleistocene savanna — *Africa* — the *physical home ground* of the hypothesis of aesthetic evolution
- But the aesthetic-universality hypothesis lacks *full-scale African verification*
