## ② Music Theory Foundations (concise summary)

### Harmony
- **Intervals**: 1st, 3rd, 5th, 7th (major, minor, perfect, augmented, diminished)
- **Tonality**: major (Ionian), minor (Aeolian) + the church modes (Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Locrian)
- **Chords**: triads (I, ii, iii, IV, V, vi, vii°) + seventh chords + 9th/11th/13th tensions
- **Functional harmony**: T (tonic) → S (subdominant) → D (dominant) → T (Rameau's *Traité de l'harmonie*, 1722)
- **Chromatic harmony and modulation**: late 19th century (Wagner's *Tristan*) → the limits of tonality → Schoenberg's atonality (1908) → the twelve-tone technique (1921)
- **Jazz harmony**: ii-V-I, blues variants, modal (Davis's *Kind of Blue*, 1959)

### Rhythm
- **Meter**: simple (2, 3, 4 beats), compound (6, 9, 12), mixed (5, 7)
- **Syncopation**: emphasizing the weak beat — the essence of Latin, jazz, and rock
- **Polyrhythm**: two or more simultaneous meters — the essence of Africa (3+3+2, etc.) + absorbed by Stravinsky and Bartók
- **Metric modulation**: Carter, Reich — meter conversion

### Melody, Timbre, Form
- **Melody**: intervals + rhythm + contour
- **Timbre**: the distribution of the harmonic series — an instrument's identity
- **Form**: ABA (ternary) / sonata (exposition–development–recapitulation) / rondo / fugue / variations / strophic (song)

### Organology
- **Classification** (Hornbostel-Sachs, 1914): idiophone, membranophone, chordophone, aerophone + the later electric and electronic
- Orchestra = strings, woodwinds, brass, percussion (4 families)
- Electronic/digital (1948 Schaeffer's musique concrète, 1956 Stockhausen, 1980s synthesizers, DAWs)

## ③ Styles by Era (concise — from Plato to the 21st century)

| Period | Style | Core |
|---|---|---|
| to 1450, Medieval | Gregorian chant, organum, ars nova (Machaut) | development from monophony to polyphony |
| 1450–1600, Renaissance | polyphony, imitative counterpoint, Palestrina, Lassus | humanism, full polyphony |
| 1600–1750, Baroque | basso continuo, fugue, concerto, opera (Monteverdi's *Orfeo*, 1607), Bach, Handel, Vivaldi | the doctrine of affections, integration |
| 1750–1820, Classical | sonata form, symphony, string quartet, Haydn, Mozart, early-to-middle Beethoven | balance, clarity |
| 1820–1900, Romantic | program music, orchestral expansion, nationalist schools, late Beethoven, Schubert, Schumann, Chopin, Liszt, Wagner, Brahms, early-to-middle Mahler | expressivity, nationality |
| 1900–, Modernism | atonality (Schoenberg), twelve-tone (1921), neoclassicism (Stravinsky), impressionism (Debussy), Bartók, Hindemith, Shostakovich, Stravinsky | the crisis of tonality, experiment |
| 1945–, Postwar modernism | total serialism (Boulez, Stockhausen), electronic music (Schaeffer, Stockhausen), chance (Cage's *4'33"*, 1952), spectralism (Grisey), minimalism (Reich, Glass, Riley, Adams) | pluralization |
| 1980–, Contemporary | neoromanticism (Penderecki, Pärt), new complexity (Ferneyhough), DJ, electronic, EDM, generative-AI music (2020s) | fusion, digital |

- 1607 Monteverdi's *Orfeo* — the founding of opera (a direct precursor to Wagner's Gesamtkunstwerk)

## ④ 🔴 Simultaneous 4-Axis Analysis (R-11 candidate — N=6)

### Axis 1 — Cognition (the neural and gestalt circuits of music cognition)

**Music cognitive psychology in full scale** (Levitin, Patel, Krumhansl, Huron):

- **Levitin**, *This Is Your Brain on Music* (2006): the musical reward circuit (NAcc, VTA, dopamine) — music is a *primal reward* like drugs and food. fMRI-verified.
- **Patel**, *Music, Language, and the Brain* (2008): **music ↔ language share neural resources** — sharing Broca's area and syntactic circuits. A *shared-evolution hypothesis* for musical style and linguistic style (the OPERA hypothesis — Patel 2011).
- **Krumhansl**, *Cognitive Foundations of Musical Pitch* (1990): the cognition of tonal hierarchy — quantifying the learning of major/minor cognition with the *probe-tone* experiment.
- **Huron**, *Sweet Anticipation* (2006): music = *expectation–prediction–response* (the ITPRA model) — pattern learning is aesthetic reward.
- **Vessel's DMN (recursion to neuroaesthetics)**: intense musical experience = DMN activation (self-integration).
- **Mirror neurons**: listening to music = motor simulation (especially rhythm and dance).

**Bourdieu's habitus (recursion to sociology-of-art)**: music cognition is *socially learned* — the cognitive difference between classical and pop varies by class.

> **🧠 Axis decision memo (cognition)**: This note is full scale on music cognition — integrating the neural, the gestalt, and the social. **Patel's OPERA hypothesis** grounds music–language co-evolution. The full-scale answer to Q1: music cognition = NAcc reward + Broca syntax + DMN self + mirror motor + Bourdieu habitus = an **integration of 5 circuits**. **Unresolved**: the difference in neural processing of non-Western scales (India's 22 śruti, the Arabic 24 quartertones) — absent from this note.

### Axis 2 — Evolution (the evolutionary origin of music)

**The Pinker vs. Dutton split (recursion to neuroaesthetics), applied to music**:
- **Pinker (cheesecake)**: music = a *by-product* of language + mating. Not an adaptation.
- **Dutton, Mithen, Cross (music as adaptation)**: music = an adaptation for *group bonding*, *mother–infant bonding*, and *mating* — *The Singing Neanderthals* (Mithen, 2005)
- **Dissanayake**: mother–infant baby-talk = the starting point of the musical instinct (the essence of melody and rhythm lies in mother–infant interaction)
- **Patel's OPERA**: music–language co-evolution

**Darwin's sexual selection**: birdsong and human music are both mating signals — Miller's *The Mating Mind* (2000), the music-adaptation hypothesis

**Evolutionary timescales**:
- Human musical capacity: 50,000+ years (instruments — the Divje Babe bear-bone flute in Slovenia, 40,000 years old)
- Styles by era: decades to centuries — *cultural evolution*
- Digital music: 70 years (Schaeffer, 1948–) — *accelerating technological evolution*

> **🧠 Axis decision memo (evolution)**: 4 branches of the music-evolution hypothesis (Pinker's by-product / Dutton's adaptation / Dissanayake's mother–infant / Miller's mating). **This note integrates music evolution in full scale.** Patel's OPERA = the most refined hypothesis of music–language co-evolution. **Unresolved**: evolutionary verification of non-Western music (Indian, African, Indigenous American) — absent from this note.

### Axis 3 — Culture (the cultural differences of music) — **this note's strongest self-critique**

**🚨 The richness of non-Western music (an area absent from this note — full-scale self-critique)**:
- **Indian music**: raga (melodic mode), tala (rhythmic cycle), 22 śruti (microtones) — the Bhatkhande and Karnatak traditions; field integration a millennium ahead of Wagner (music + dance + poetry — the Natya Shastra, 200 BC – AD 200; recursion to comparative-arts §③)
- **Arabic/Turkish maqam**: 24 quartertones, complex rhythms (10/8, 9/8, 7/8)
- **Chinese wusheng (五声) and qisheng (七声)**: Daoist and Confucian philosophy of music (music as cosmic order)
- **Japanese gagaku, noh, kabuki**: music–drama integration (a precursor to Wagner)
- **Korean jeongak, pansori, folk music**: nongak, sijo, changgeuk — field integration
- **African polyrhythm**: simultaneous multiple meters (3+3+2, etc.) — absorbed into jazz and rock
- **Indonesian gamelan**: circular ensemble — influencing Debussy, Reich, Glass
- **Jazz, blues, rock, EDM**: an African + European + Latin fusion

**Patel's and Krumhansl's universality hypothesis (cognition)** vs. **ethnomusicology's (Merriam, Nettl) relativism**:
- Universal: the octave, rhythm cognition, part of emotional response
- Relative: scales, rhythmic patterns, styles of emotional expression

**Born's global music**: the globalization of music in the digital age (K-pop, Latin, Afrobeats) + critiques of colonialism and appropriation

**Bourdieu's musical taste (France)**: tastes for classical, jazz, pop, and rock differ by class — partially verified outside the West

### Axis 4 — Society (the sociology of music)

**Adorno — the apex of the sociology of music**:
- *Philosophy of New Music* (1949): **Schoenberg vs. Stravinsky** — Schoenberg = genuine modernism (a negative expression of social contradiction) / Stravinsky = commodity, restoration
- *Introduction to the Sociology of Music* (1962): **the 5 listening types** — expert, good listener, culture consumer, emotional listener, jazz fan, entertainment consumer, the unmusical
- The *culture industry* critique of jazz and pop — homogenization, commodification
- **Critique**: his jazz critique is *Eurocentric* + ignorant of Black music

**Bourdieu**: musical taste = class *distinction*. Classical = bourgeois capital / rock and pop = workers / jazz = the new bourgeoisie

**Becker (the sociology of jazz)**: *Outsiders* (1963) — the society of jazz musicians; *Art Worlds* (1982), the jazz world

**Born — the IRCAM ethnography**: the power structure of the computer-music institute — Boulez's authority, the hierarchy of engineers, state patronage

**The sociology of the music industry**: record labels, streaming (the Spotify/Apple Music algorithms), music labor (Banks), the live industry, festivals

> **🧠 Axis decision memo (society)**: This note is full scale on the sociology of music — recursion to F5. Adorno's *Schoenberg vs. Stravinsky* is the *dialectical split* between musical autonomy and the commodity. In the 21st century, digital platforms constitute a new social power through *algorithmic determination*.
