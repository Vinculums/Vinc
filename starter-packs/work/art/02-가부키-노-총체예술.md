## ① Definition and Core Concepts

### Noh (能, nōgaku 能楽) — 14th century
- **Authors and founders**: **Kan'ami Kiyotsugu** (観阿弥 清次, 1333–1384) + his son **Zeami Motokiyo** (世阿弥 元清, 1363–1443)
- **Period**: the late 14th century under the Muromachi shogunate — patronage of shogun Ashikaga Yoshimitsu → establishment
- **Essence**: masked drama + voice, dance, and poetry + a minimal stage + the aesthetics of *yūgen*
- **5 categories**: god plays (神能 *kami*), warrior plays (修羅 *shura*), woman plays (女 *katsura*), madness plays (狂 *kyō*), demon plays (鬼 *kiri*) — one program cycles through the 5 kinds
- **The stage**: roughly a 6×6 m square, a bridge (hashigakari), the painted pine (kagami-ita), the audience to the left — minimal, symbolic

### Kabuki (歌舞伎) — early 17th century
- **Founder**: **Izumo no Okuni** (出雲阿国, 1572?–1613?) — first performance in 1603 on the Kamo riverbed in Kyoto. A female dancer
- **Transformation**: 1629 ban on women's kabuki (public morals) → wakashū (若衆 — beautiful youths) kabuki → 1652 establishment of yarō (野郎 — adult male) kabuki
- **Essence**: music (shamisen, drums, flute) + song + dance + drama + spectacular costume and makeup (kumadori) + a dynamic stage (revolving, lifts, the hanamichi walkway stage)
- **Three genres**: jidaimono (period pieces — samurai) / sewamono (domestic pieces — commoners) / shosagoto (dance plays)
- **Famous playwright**: Chikamatsu Monzaemon (近松門左衛門, 1653–1725) — *Sonezaki Shinjū* (1703 — a love-suicide tragedy)

### Core Aesthetic Concepts

1. **yūgen** (幽玄 — "profound, mysterious depth"): the core of Zeami's noh aesthetics. Not direct expression but suggestion, lingering resonance, shadow. A depth *not graspable in itself yet felt*. Parallel to, and different from, the Indian dhvani (natya-shastra).

2. **mono no aware** (もののあはれ — "the deep pathos of things"): the Heian-era aesthetic (*The Tale of Genji* (源氏物語), Murasaki Shikibu, 1010). Deep sentiment in the *transience* of things and the flow of time — an integration of sorrow, beauty, and compassion. Formalized by Motoori Norinaga (本居宣長) in the 18th century.

3. **wabi-sabi** (侘寂 — "simplicity, imperfection, the trace of time"): the aesthetics of the tea ceremony (Sen no Rikyū 千利休, 1522–1591), ceramics, and gardens. Rejecting splendor and perfection + deep beauty in the *imperfect, unfinished, time-worn*.

4. **iki** (いき — the Edo urban aesthetic): 18th–19th century — refinement, chic, an aesthetics of subtraction. Formalized in Kuki Shūzō's *The Structure of Iki* (Iki no Kōzō, 「いき」の構造, 1930).

5. **ma** (間 — "interval, negative space, pause"): the *emptiness* of time and space is itself beauty. The *silence* of noh, the *blank space* of the garden, the *space between lines* of haiku.

6. **kata** (型 — the fixed form): both noh and kabuki have *prescribed forms* — not free expression, but depth within form. Parallel to, and different from, the Indian mudra and rasa-bhāva.

7. **jo-ha-kyū** (序破急 — "beginning, development, climax"): Zeami's theory of rhythm — the temporal structure of all art. Applied symmetrically to music, dance, theatre, poetry, gardens, and the tea ceremony. A field-integrating aesthetics.

## ③ 🔴 Simultaneous 4-Axis Analysis (R-11 — N=11)

### Axis 1 — Cognition (the cognition of yūgen, ma, kata)

- **Yūgen cognition** = *cognition of suggestion* — direct meaning < shadow, resonance, incompleteness. Parallel to the Indian dhvani (natya-shastra). Neurologically: the hypothesis of DMN activation (neuroaesthetics) (self-integration)
- **Mono no aware** = cognition of temporality — converting the *transience* of things into deep sentiment. Potentially consistent with evolutionary aesthetics (Dutton's environmental adaptation) and Vessel's DMN
- **Ma cognition** = *cognition of emptiness* — the pause of time and space as beauty. A gestalt figure–ground reversal (background = figure)
- **Kata cognition** = cognition of form — freedom < depth within form. An affinity with Aristotle's form (aesthetics) and Greenberg's medium autonomy (sociology-of-art), yet *non-reducible*
- **jo-ha-kyū** = cognition of rhythm and time — parallel to Huron's *ITPRA expectation–prediction–response* (music-foundations)

> **🧠 Axis decision memo (cognition)**: Japanese aesthetic cognition = the 5 dimensions of *suggestion + temporality + negative space + form + rhythm*. A *completely different system* from India's 8–9 rasas + the Western 5 dimensions. The Japanese dimension added to the answer to Q1 — *suggestion, negative space, and transience* are absent or weak in India and the West.

### Axis 3 — Culture (Japan + global influence)

**The multiple layers of Japanese aesthetics in full scale**:
- Heian (794–1185): the *Genji*'s mono no aware + waka and calligraphy
- Kamakura and Muromachi (1185–1573): Zen Buddhism, noh, the tea ceremony, gardens → wabi-sabi, yūgen
- Edo (1603–1868): kabuki, ukiyo-e, haiku, iki
- Meiji (1868–1912): the Western shock, modernization + the traditional revival
- Contemporary: film, manga, anime, games, J-pop — global spread

**🚨 Japan's internal diversity**:
- The mainland vs. Okinawa (Ryukyu) vs. the Ainu (indigenous Hokkaido) — this note is confined to the *mainland (Yamato)*
- Colonialism (Korea, Taiwan, Manchuria) — the dimension of Japanese aesthetics as a *colonial instrument* (the Said perspective) — only partially in this note

**The history of Japan's influence on the West (the strongest non-Western influence)**:
- **Japonisme** (late 19th century): the ukiyo-e of Hokusai, Hiroshige, Utamaro → a directly decisive influence on Impressionism and Post-Impressionism (Van Gogh, Manet, Monet, Toulouse-Lautrec, Whistler) (recursion to visual-art-foundations)
- **W.B. Yeats**, *At the Hawk's Well* (1916) — noh influence, via Pound through the Fenollosa texts
- **Ezra Pound**, *Cathay* (1915 — translations of Chinese and Japanese poetry), *Imagism* — haiku influence
- **Britten**, *Curlew River* (1964) — influenced by the noh play *Sumidagawa*
- **Cage**, **Cunningham** — influenced by Zen and Japanese aesthetics
- **Tarkovsky** — influenced by Kurosawa and Ozu
- **Wes Anderson**, *Isle of Dogs* (2018) — Japanese aesthetics directly

**The West → Japan**:
- 1868, the Meiji Restoration — full-scale introduction of Western aesthetics
- Taishō and Shōwa modernism — bidirectional
- Murakami Haruki, Banana Yoshimoto — global Japanese literature
- Miyazaki (Ghibli) — a Japan + West integration

> **🧠 Axis decision memo (culture)**: Japan exerted *the strongest influence on the West* among non-Western cultures — Japonisme (grounding Impressionism), Yeats, Pound, Cage. This note is a *full-scale recovery* from the 9-note self-critique — full-scale recognition of the *Japanese dimension* of Western art history.
