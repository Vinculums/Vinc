# Caravaggio

## ① One-line essence

**Painting in which a single shaft of light cuts an event out of the darkness** — a rejection of idealization · using real people from the street as models for sacred images + extreme chiaroscuro (tenebrism) = the opening of Baroque painting + a naturalist declaration to *paint only what is seen* — a life that was itself a flight (murder · dying in exile just before his pardon).

## ② Life and historical context (4 axes)

- **Life**: born 1571 in Milan (his family's ancestral seat, the town of Caravaggio) — died 1610 in exile at Porto Ercole (age 38). Apprenticed in Milan to Simone Peterzano (in the lineage of Titian's pupils) → moved to Rome c.1592 — poverty · subcontracted still lifes → patronage of Cardinal Del Monte → the 1599–1600 Contarelli Chapel cycle brought him instantly to the summit of Rome → **the 1606 murder of Ranuccio Tomassoni** (a duel · brawl) → death sentence · flight from Rome → Naples → Malta (1608 knighted → imprisoned · escaped · expelled) → Sicily → Naples (1609 assaulted · facial wound) → died of fever in 1610 while heading north in hope of a papal pardon.
- **Cognition**: *cognition of the seized instant* — cutting the decisive moment of a narrative (a calling · a beheading · astonishment) like stage lighting. Omitting the underdrawing (disegno) — painting *directly alla prima* onto the canvas + leaving only traces of incised contours (incisioni)
- **Evolution**: a frontal rejection of late Mannerism (artificial gracefulness) → **the opening of Baroque naturalism** — one of two great axes alongside Annibale Carracci's classical line. A radical interpretation of the *directly appealing image* that the church of the Counter-Reformation (after the Council of Trent) demanded
- **Culture**: the Roman church commission system + cardinal patronage (Del Monte · Giustiniani) — yet certain commissioned works were rejected and had to be redone (*Death of the Virgin* 1601–06 — rumors that the model for the Virgin was a drowned woman / a woman of the street → rejection by the religious order · purchased by Gonzaga through Rubens's mediation)
- **Society**: fame coexisting with a criminal record (numerous records of assault · carrying weapons) — the prototype of the Romantic myth of the *cursed artist*. After his death he spread across 17th-century Europe → *denigration · oblivion* in the 18th–19th centuries (Poussin's hostility: "born to destroy painting") → **rediscovery by Roberto Longhi in the 20th century** (the 1951 Milan exhibition) — a canonical case of T8 rediscovery

## ③ Major works and methodology

**Representative works**:
- *The Calling of Saint Matthew* (1599–1600, San Luigi dei Francesi, Contarelli Chapel, Rome) — a tavern-like room · a diagonal beam of light = the moment of the calling. His breakthrough work
- *The Crucifixion of Saint Peter* · *The Conversion of Saint Paul* (1600–01, Cerasi Chapel) — the fallen Saul = the embodiment of mystical experience
- *Judith Beheading Holofernes* (c.1598–99) — the beheading as an ongoing instant
- *Supper at Emmaus* (1601, London) — the theatricalization of the moment of recognition · combined with still life
- *Death of the Virgin* (1601–06, Louvre) — swollen feet · a plebeian Virgin = a naturalist scandal
- *The Seven Works of Mercy* (1607, Naples) — the summit of his Neapolitan period of flight
- *The Beheading of Saint John the Baptist* (1608, Valletta, Malta) — his largest canvas + **his only signed work** (signed in the Baptist's blood, f. michelang.o)
- *David with the Head of Goliath* (c.1610, Borghese) — Goliath = a self-portrait — the summit of the penitent self-portrait

**Distinctive methodology**:
- **Tenebrism**: silencing the background into darkness so that a single light source cuts out only the figures — the *radicalization* of chiaroscuro (in Leonardo's lineage). Light = the visual grammar of divine intervention
- **Naturalism**: a rejection of idealization — down to dirty feet · wrinkles · fruit on the verge of rot. The attitude that "there is no master but nature" (recorded by the biographers Bellori · Baglione, hostile accounts included)
- **Street models**: rendering saints and the Virgin as real people from the street (day laborers · prostitutes — Fillide Melandroni and others) — subverting the *grammar of social rank* in sacred imagery. The rejection of *Death of the Virgin* is the evidence
- **Direct execution**: almost no preparatory drawings (surviving drawings near zero) — painting directly over a dark ground + reproducing model poses under lamplight (contemporary records)
- **Staging the instant**: choosing the *most unstable moment* of a narrative — theatricalizing pictorial time (the Baroque grammar of the affections)

## ④ 🔴 Cross-field influence and creation (MM-13 acceleration)

**Influence within art** — the Caravaggisti (the Caravaggio school) spread across Europe: Orazio · Artemisia Gentileschi (Rome) → Ribera (Naples · Spain) → the Utrecht school (Honthorst · Terbrugghen · Baburen, returning home after study in Rome in the 1610s) → Northern Europe · France (Georges de La Tour) · Spain (Zurbarán · the early Velázquez).

#### Caravaggio → Rembrandt (T2)
- **What**: tenebrism — a single light source in the darkness · psychological lighting → Rembrandt's psychology of chiaroscuro
- **Path**: Caravaggio in Rome (1600s) → **the Utrecht Caravaggisti** (Honthorst · Terbrugghen — returning to the Netherlands after residing in Rome in the 1610s) → Rembrandt (who never visited Italy — mediated through Utrecht + tutelage under Lastman) → the early dramatization of chiaroscuro (*The Blinding of Samson*, 1636) → late inner illumination (*Return of the Prodigal Son*, c.1668)
- **Timing**: 1600s Rome → 1610s–20s Utrecht mediation → 1630s~ Rembrandt
- **Transformation**: **T2 stylistic inheritance (indirect mediation)** — Caravaggio's *light of the external event* → deepened into Rembrandt's *light of the inner life · the soul*. A canonical case of stylistic transmission without a direct encounter
- **Basis**: records of the Utrecht school's residence in Rome / analysis of chiaroscuro in Rembrandt's early work / Longhi's genealogy (1951)

#### Caravaggio → Gentileschi (Artemisia) (T2)
- **What**: tenebrism + the bodily character of a violent narrative → appropriation from a female painter's viewpoint
- **Path**: her father Orazio Gentileschi (Caravaggio's Roman colleague · co-defendant in a 1603 defamation suit) → Artemisia (1593–c.1656) learning in the workshop → *Judith Slaying Holofernes* (the c.1612–13 Naples version · the c.1620 Uffizi version) — reconstructing Caravaggio's same subject with *physical vividness · female agency* (an interpretation strongly linking it to the 1611 Tassi sexual-assault case · trial, though reduction is to be guarded against)
- **Timing**: 1600s Rome → 1610s–20s
- **Transformation**: **T2 stylistic inheritance** + a shift of viewpoint — Judith becomes not an object of observation but an *agent of action*. A central case in 20th-century feminist art history (Mary Garrard's research following Linda Nochlin's 1971 "Why Have There Been No Great Women Artists?") — directly bound up with the obligation to *reinforce female nodes* in the influence graph
- **Basis**: the two versions of *Judith Slaying Holofernes* / the 1612 trial records / Garrard's *Artemisia Gentileschi* (1989)

#### Caravaggio → 19th-century rediscovery of realism (T8)
- **What**: naturalism · plebeian models · rejection of idealization → the retroactive coherence with the lineage of realism (Courbet · Manet) + 20th-century canonical rehabilitation
- **Path**: 18th–19th-century denigration (disparaged as a "nature-imitator" · the transmitted Poussin hostility) → the influx into France of 19th-century Spanish painting (Ribera · Velázquez — in Caravaggio's lineage) → Courbet's *The Stonebreakers* (1849, not *The Angelus*) · Manet's flat chiaroscuro as an *indirect lineage* → canonical rehabilitation through **Roberto Longhi**'s research (1920s~) + **the great 1951 Milan exhibition** → his contemporary status at the popular summit
- **Timing**: 1610 after his death → about 250 years of denigration → 1951 rehabilitation (341 years later)
- **Transformation**: **T8 reinterpretation · rediscovery** — Bach's 79 years · Hildegard's 791 years · Caravaggio's roughly 250–340 years of dormancy. Longhi's thesis: "Without Caravaggio there would be no Ribera · Vermeer · La Tour · Rembrandt, and Delacroix · Courbet · Manet would have painted differently"
- **Basis**: the catalogue of Longhi's 1951 exhibition / Longhi's *Caravaggio* (1952) / the reception history of the Spanish manner in 19th-century Salon criticism

**Art → other fields**:
- **Film lighting**: a single light source · low-key chiaroscuro = the prototype of **chiaroscuro / low-key lighting** — film noir · Martin Scorsese ("we owe a debt to Caravaggio," to that effect) · Derek Jarman's *Caravaggio* (1986) biopic
- **Photography**: the upstream source of what is called Rembrandt lighting — the grammar of portrait-photography lighting
- **Literature · popular narrative**: the myth of the cursed genius — biographies · novels · crime narratives (Peter Robb's *M*, 1998, etc.)
- **Theater · the stage**: the Baroque staging of the instant = a parallel to the aesthetics of stage lighting (returning to comparative-arts)

## ⑤ Answers to the meta-questions

- **Q1 (why define beauty?)**: *the directness of the real* — beauty is not idealization but the actual body · event revealed out of the darkness. Anti-Platonic: not the Idea but *this body, now* (a radicalization of the mimesis theory of aesthetics)
- **Q2 (by what criterion?)**: *the cutting power of the light source + the instability of the instant + the reality of the model*. A frontal rejection of the criterion of gracefulness (grazia) — the very subversion of criteria as method
- **Q3 (cross-field influence and creation)**: within art (Caravaggisti → Rembrandt · Gentileschi) + crossing fields (film lighting · photography) — *the single technical element of light* as the cross-field mediator (Bach's form · Leonardo's drawing · Caravaggio's light)

## ⑥ Connections and successors

- Precursors: Leonardo (the chiaroscuro lineage — Leonardo da Vinci) · Lombard naturalism (Moretto · Savoldo) · Peterzano (T1 apprenticeship · Titian's lineage)
- Contemporaries: Annibale Carracci (rival of the classical line — responsible for the central painting of the Cerasi Chapel) · Del Monte (patron) · Orazio Gentileschi (colleague · lawsuit)
- Direct successors: Artemisia Gentileschi · Ribera · the Utrecht school · La Tour · Zurbarán
- Distant successors: Rembrandt · Velázquez → 19th-century realism (T8) → film lighting
- Criticism · controversy: Poussin · Bellori (17th-century classicist hostility) ↔ Longhi (20th-century rehabilitation) — the sociology of canon formation (sociology-of-art)
