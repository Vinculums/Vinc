# Chinua Achebe

## ① One-line essence

***Things Fall Apart* (1958)** — the first full-fledged novel written in English from an *internal African viewpoint*. **A full-scale critique of Joseph Conrad's *Heart of Darkness* (1899)** — the 1975 lecture "*An Image of Africa*" — a frontal challenge to Africa's representation in Western literary history. **Transforming the English language itself for African literature** — a strategy different from Ngũgĩ's rejection of English.

## ② Life and historical context (4 axes)

- **Life**: 1930 Ogidi (Igbo, eastern Nigeria) — 2013 Boston. Both sides of Christian parents and a traditional Igbo community. Ibadan University · BBC radio · 1958 *Things Fall Apart* (age 28). Directly experienced the Igbo mass killings of the Biafran War (1967–70) · diplomat · poet · professor. 1990 traffic accident — wheelchair. Later years in the United States (Bard · Brown).
- **Cognition**: Igbo oral tradition + English integrated — *simultaneous cognition of two languages and two cultures*
- **Evolution**: the *decisive inflection point* of postcolonial literature — after *Things Fall Apart*, 50+ language translations · 20 million+ copies — entering the canon of world literary history
- **Culture**: Igbo society (pre-colonial · colonial-in-progress · post-independence) in full. With the arrival of colonialism and Christianity, the title *Things Fall Apart* (= quoting Yeats's *The Second Coming*, 1919) — the cultural disintegration brought by colonialism
- **Society**: Nigerian colonization and independence (1960) · civil war · diaspora — the *politically engaged aesthetics* of the African writer

## ③ Major works and methodology

**Representative works**:
- *Things Fall Apart* (1958) — Okonkwo · Igbo village · arrival of colonialism, in three parts. Africa's first *world-canon* novel
- *No Longer at Ease* (1960) — Okonkwo's grandson · corruption in Nigeria just before independence
- *Arrow of God* (1964) — clash of colonial administration and an Igbo priest
- *A Man of the People* (1966) — satire of post-independence corruption
- *Anthills of the Savannah* (1987) — critique of military dictatorship
- *Hopes and Impediments* (1988 — essays) — criticism of colonialism, literature, politics
- **"*An Image of Africa: Racism in Conrad's Heart of Darkness*"** (1975 lecture / 1977 published) — a full-scale critique of Conrad (forcing self-reflection on the *racism* of the Western literary canon)

**Distinctive methodology**:
- **Fusion of Igbo proverbs into English** — "Proverbs are the palm-oil with which words are eaten" (*Things Fall Apart*). English grammar + Igbo proverbs · metaphor · worldview
- **A three-generation family narrative** — Okonkwo → Obi → Beatrice (three works integrated)
- **Integration of internal and external viewpoints** — colonialism seen from within the Igbo
- **Integration of essay + novel + poetry** — crossing fields
- **A strategy of *transforming* English** (different from Ngũgĩ's rejection of English) — "Let no one be fooled by the fact that we may write in English, for we intend to do unheard of things with it"

## ④ 🔴 Cross-field influence and creation (MM-13)

**Within literature**:
- **Founding the *entry of African literature into the world canon*** — Soyinka (1986 Nobel) · Ngũgĩ · Bessie Head · Mariama Bâ · Tsitsi Dangarembga · Adichie · Yaa Gyasi all follow directly
- **Caribbean diaspora literature**: V.S. Naipaul · Derek Walcott (1992 Nobel) · Jamaica Kincaid
- **Black American literature**: Toni Morrison (1993 Nobel — praised by Achebe) · James Baldwin
- **Founding postcolonial theory**: coherence with Said's *Orientalism* (1978) — the Conrad critique is a direct input. Bhabha · Spivak · Hall follow in earnest.
- ***Things Fall Apart*, 50+ language translations · 20 million+ copies** — the global pinnacle of African literature

**Literature → other fields**:
- **Film**: *Things Fall Apart* TV films (1971 · 1987) + Sembène's *Camp de Thiaroye* (1988) — following Achebe's spirit
- **Music**: Fela Kuti (Achebe's contemporary in Nigeria) — integration of politics + music (Afrobeat) · followed by Burna Boy · Femi Kuti
- **Politics**: African political discourse · the *Achebe Forum* — memory of the Igbo · Biafra
- **Education**: canon of the English-literature curriculum — placed *alongside Conrad* in Western and worldwide English-literature classes (an intended dialectic)

**The Conrad → Achebe critique in earnest**:
- Conrad's *Heart of Darkness* (1899) — portrays Africa as the *dark unconscious · outside of civilization*
- Achebe 1975: "Conrad was a bloody racist" — a frontal challenge to the *racism* of the Western literary canon itself
- Yet the Conrad critique does *not* negate Conrad's value — a dialectical challenge

## ⑤ Answers to the meta-questions

- **Q1**: Humans perceive and define beauty from *community + self-identity + historical memory*. A parallel to ubuntu (African and Latin American aesthetics), plus political meaning.
- **Q2**: The criterion of beauty = *communal truth + political honesty + linguistic transformation + fusion of proverb and oral tradition + colonial self-critique*. Rejects Western autonomy (Kant) — an *aesthetics of engagement*.
- **Q3**: English + Igbo integration + Conrad critique + coherence with Said = a bidirectional formalization of *Western → African influence + African → Western influence*. **Transforming the Western literary canon itself**.
