# jingju

## 0. Declaration of Self-Critique

- **Translation limits**: qiyun shengdong (氣韻生動), shenyun (神韻), jing (境), han (恨), meot, jeong (情), yeobaek (餘白), etc. — essential loss in English or Korean translation
- **China ≠ a single entity**: Han ≠ Tibetan ≠ Uyghur ≠ Mongol ≠ Manchu / this note is *Han-centered* only
- **Korea ≠ a single entity**: jeongak (the ruling class) ≠ folk music (the common people) / yangban ≠ commoner ≠ gwangdae performer / the mainland ≠ Jeju
- **China vs. Korea vs. Japan: *East Asia ≠ a single entity***: sharing the Chinese-character sphere + Confucianism, Buddhism, and Daoism, yet *each civilization developed independently*
- **60–700 years ahead of Wagner**: jingju in the 19th century vs. Wagner 1849 — in fact nearly contemporaneous. But the *Yuan zaju of the 13th–14th centuries and the Tang song-and-dance drama of the 7th–10th centuries* are 1,100–500 years ahead of Wagner — *the priority over Wagner is not temporal but a matter of *different systems**

## ① Definition and Core Concepts

### Chinese Jingju (京剧, jīngjù — Beijing opera) — established in the 1790s
- **History (concise)**: Yuan zaju (13th–14th centuries — *The Story of the Western Wing* (西廂記), *The Orphan of Zhao* (趙氏孤兒)) → Ming *kunqu* (崑曲, 16th century) → 1790, the Four Great Anhui Troupes (四大徽班) enter Beijing → 1840s, jingju established
- **Essence**: music (the jīnghú 京胡, drums, gong) + song (chàng 唱 — the 4 vocal styles) + speech (niàn 念) + acting (zuò 做) + martial arts (dǎ 打) + dance + spectacular makeup (liǎnpǔ 脸谱 — color = character: red for loyalty, black for integrity, white for treachery) + costume (xíngtou 行头) + a minimal stage (a horse = a whip / a boat = an oar)
- **The 4 role types** (jiǎosè 角色): shēng 生 (male) / dàn 旦 (female) / jìng 净 (the strongly painted-face male) / chǒu 丑 (comic)
- **Masterworks**: *Farewell My Concubine* (霸王別姬), *The Drunken Concubine* (貴妃醉酒), *Crossroads* (三岔口), the *Sun Wukong* (孫悟空) series

### Masters
- **Mei Lanfang** (梅兰芳, 1894–1961) — the apex of the dàn (旦 — female roles). The *Mei school* (梅派). **His 1935 Moscow performances → a direct inspiration for Brecht's *Verfremdungseffekt* (alienation effect)** (Brecht's *Alienation Effects in Chinese Acting*, 1936). The *decisive case* of direct non-Western → Western theoretical influence.
- **Cheng Yanqiu** (程砚秋), **Shang Xiaoyun**, **Xun Huisheng** — the Four Great Dan (四大名旦)

### Korean Pansori (sori, changgeuk) — established in the 17th century
- **History**: the 17th-century Honam gwangdae (changwoo 倡優) performer tradition → the 18th–19th-century golden age (Shin Jae-hyo 申在孝, 1812–1884, its compiler and theorist) → the Japanese occupation, liberation, UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage 2008
- **Essence**: **one-person sung drama** — the gwangdae (singer) + the gosu (drummer) — only 2 people. Solo performances of up to 8 hours.
- **3 elements**:
  - **Chang** (唱 — song): 5 tones + 6 rhythmic cycles (jinyang, jungmori, jungjungmori, jajinmori, hwimori, eotmori)
  - **Aniri** (narrative speech)
  - **Neoreumsae** (gesture and expression — expressing every character and object with a single fan)
- **The 5 transmitted madang**: *Chunhyangga*, *Simcheongga*, *Heungbuga*, *Sugungga*, *Jeokbyeokga* (originally 12 madang, 7 lost)
- **Schools**: Dongpyeonje (eastern Honam), Seopyeonje (western Honam), Junggoje — regional styles
- **The contemporary**: Im Kwon-taek's *Seopyeonje* (1993), *Chunhyang* (2000), Lee Ja-ram's *Sacheon-ga* (2007 — a Brechtian adaptation in pansori), the singers Han Seung-seok and Park Ae-ri

### Shared East Asian Aesthetic Concepts

1. **Qiyun shengdong** (氣韻生動 — "spirit resonance, living movement"): the first of the 6 laws (六法) in **Xie He**'s (謝赫, 6th century) *Guhua Pinlu* (古畫品錄) — the supreme criterion of painting. *Vitality and qi* over form. Parallel to, and different from, the Indian *prāṇa* + the Japanese *yūgen*.

2. **Shenyun** (神韻 — "numinous resonance"): Wang Shizhen (王士禛), 18th century — the *depth beyond direct expression* in poetry and painting. Parallel to the Japanese yūgen.

3. **Jingjie** (境界 — "boundary, realm"): Wang Guowei (王國維), 1908, *Renjian Cihua* (人間詞話) — the *worldmaking* of poetry. Parallel to Goodman's *Ways of Worldmaking* (philosophy-of-art).

4. **Sanyuan** (三遠 — "the three distances"): Guo Xi (郭熙, 1020–1090), *Linquan Gaozhi* (林泉高致) — the 3 viewpoints of painting:
   - Gaoyuan (高遠 — looking up from the foot of the mountain to its peak)
   - Shenyuan (深遠 — the depth seen beyond the mountain)
   - Pingyuan (平遠 — looking levelly from a near mountain to distant ones)
   - → **Multiple viewpoints** (an essential difference from the West's single-viewpoint perspective)

5. **The unity of calligraphy, painting, and poetry** (書畫詩一體) — the *field integration* of poetry, calligraphy, and painting (the apex in Yuan-era literati painting (文人畫) — Zhao Mengfu (趙孟頫), Huang Gongwang (黃公望), Wang Meng (王蒙))

6. **Korean aesthetics**:
   - **Han** (恨) — deep sorrow, grievance, lingering resonance. Not mere sadness (release + sublimation)
   - **Heung** (興) — exhilaration, joy, vitality
   - **Jeong** (情) — deep affection (toward human relations and things)
   - **Meot** — form + freedom + naturalness (analyzed by Choi Chun-Sik, 1972)
   - **Yeobaek / yeoun** (餘白·餘韻) — emptiness, lingering resonance (parallel to, and different from, the Japanese ma)

## ③ 🔴 Simultaneous 4-Axis Analysis (R-11 — N=12)

### Axis 1 — Cognition (the cognition of qi, shenyun, jingjie)

- **Qiyun shengdong (氣韻生動)** = *cognition of vitality* beyond form — essentially different from Aristotle's form (aesthetics). Neurologically: the DMN (neuroaesthetics) + a mirror-neuron hypothesis (vitality = activation of action circuits)
- **Sanyuan (三遠, multiple viewpoints)** = *integrated cognition* — single-viewpoint perspective (the Western Renaissance) vs. the *simultaneous cognition* of multiple viewpoints. A multilayering of the gestalt figure-ground
- **Jingjie (境界)** = *worldmaking cognition* — parallel to Goodman (philosophy-of-art)
- **The one-person multi-role cognition of pansori** = the apex of Theory of Mind (neuroaesthetics) — one gwangdae representing all characters and objects → the apex of the listener's ToM simulation
- **The unity of calligraphy and painting (書畫一體)** = the integration of field cognition — poetry, script, and picture in the *same cognitive circuit* (the Chinese character itself is visual + semantic)

> **🧠 Axis decision memo (cognition)**: East Asian cognition = the 4 dimensions of qi (vitality) + multiple viewpoints + integration + the ToM apex. India (rasa, dhvani) + Japan (yūgen, ma) + East Asia (qi, jing) = **roughly 12 new non-Western cognitive dimensions**. The non-Western dimension added to the answer to Q1 in full scale.

### Axis 3 — Culture — this note's accelerating recovery

**China, Korea, and Japan — East Asia = sharing Chinese characters + Confucianism + Buddhism + Daoism**:
- Landscape painting (grounded in China → spread to Korea, Japan, Vietnam)
- Calligraphy (the Chinese character = a visual + semantic field integration)
- Gardens (China → a different evolution in Japan)
- The tea ceremony (China → evolved in Japan)

**Differences between Korea, China, and Japan**:
- China: vast landscapes, multiple viewpoints, qi
- Korea: han, emptiness, naturalism (Joseon white porcelain, the moon jar), the folk (talchum mask dance, pansori)
- Japan: precision, negative space, yūgen, iemoto

**The history of influence on the West (most decisive)**:
- **Mei Lanfang, Moscow 1935 → Brecht's *Verfremdungseffekt*** (the 1936 essay) — the most decisive case of *non-Western → Western theoretical influence*. A full-scale influence on Brecht's epic theatre + modern theatre.
- **Pound**, *Cathay* (1915) — Chinese poetry in translation + Imagism
- **Cage**, the *I Ching* — chance music (1951, *Music of Changes*)
- **Wong Kar-wai, Zhang Yimou, Hou Hsiao-hsien, Park Chan-wook, Bong Joon-ho** — East Asian cinema gone global
- **K-pop, K-drama, Japanese manga, Chinese games** — the accelerating 21st-century *East Asia → global* flow

**The West → East Asia**:
- Late 19th to early 20th century — the introduction of Western modernism
- The *new Western-influenced drama* — Chinese new theatre influenced by Western drama
- Korea: the introduction of Western music and art + the traditional revival (gugak, traditional dance, cultural heritage)
- Japan: the integration of the Western and the traditional (Kawabata's 1968 Nobel, Murakami, Miyazaki)

> **🧠 Axis decision memo (culture)**: **The *3rd-stage recovery* from the 9-note self-critique** — India, Japan, East Asia (China, Korea). **Mei → Brecht is the first decisive case of non-Western → Western theory** (Yeats, Pound, Cage, Britten, Brecht accumulating). The first full-scale integration of Korean aesthetics (han, heung, jeong, meot). **This note is the East Asian integrated answer** + **the obligation of self-recognition of Korean aesthetics (Dohun's country of residence)**.
