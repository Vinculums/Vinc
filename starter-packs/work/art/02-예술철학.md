# philosophy-of-art

## ① Definition and Core Concepts

**Philosophy of art** is the discipline in the late-20th-century analytic-philosophy tradition that seeks to answer *"what is art"*. This note is the direct successor to meta-question 2 of aesthetics (by what criterion do we call something beauty·art) — the *analytic answer* after Adorno.

The key forks:
- **Semiotic theory**: art = a symbol system (Goodman)
- **Historicism**: art = the conferral of meaning within an art-historical context (Danto)
- **Institutional theory**: art = an artifact granted recognition by the artworld (Dickie)
- **Expression theory**: art = the expression of a mental state (Wollheim)
- **Narrative theory**: art = a position within a historical narrative (Carroll)

**The return to aesthetics (U-04 — redefinition of meaning)**:
- Plato's imitation theory → Danto rejects imitation "after the end of art" (Warhol's *Brillo Box*)
- Aristotle's form → Goodman redefines form as *density·syntacticity*
- Kant's universality → Dickie dismantles universality into *institutional practice* (not universal; a social convention)
- **Hegel's end of art → Danto inherits it directly (1984 *The End of Art*)** — the strongest return
- Adorno's autonomy → Dickie's institutional theory redefines autonomy as *autonomy within the institution*

→ This note *translates·redefines* the 6 positions of aesthetics in analytic-philosophical terms. The return to the history of aesthetics in earnest.

## ③ 🔴 Simultaneous 4-Axis Analysis (R-11 candidate N=2)

### Axis 1 — Cognition (why do humans perceive beauty?)

**Goodman**: cognition = the capacity to *decode symbols*. The cognitive operation of identifying the 5 symptoms. Humans cognize by converting *reference* into *exemplification* — a color swatch *possesses the color while simultaneously pointing to it*. → **cognition is itself the symbolic capacity**.

**Danto**: cognition = the capacity for *interpretation*. The *Brillo Box* is perceptually identical to the everyday box — the cognitive difference is the *higher-order cognition* of applying *artworld theory*. "An eye that has not seen cannot see art" — theory-dependent cognition. → **the *theory-dependence* of cognition**.

**Dickie**: cognition = *institutional cognition* — cognition trained in the practices of the artworld. One who has not learned the institution cannot cognize. Cognition as *learned social cognition*.

**Wollheim**: cognition = *seeing-in* — a twofold cognition seeing the painting's surface (physical marks) + the depicted object *simultaneously*. A hypothesis of a neurological basis (pictorial cognition is a capacity distinct from ordinary visual perception). → a direct input to subsequent neuroaesthetics (Foundations 3).

**Carroll**: cognition = *cognition of narrative position* — placing a work within the *historical narrative* of "this work is the next stage after abstract expressionism". Cognition as *historical cognition*.

> **🧠 Axis decision memo (cognition)**: Kant's *subjective universality* in aesthetics → *forks* into Goodman's symbolic capacity + Wollheim's seeing-in + Danto's theory-dependent cognition. Kant's single universal differentiates into 5 different cognitive forms (U-04 redefinition of meaning). **A direct input to neuroaesthetics**: Wollheim's seeing-in is a direct precursor to Zeki's dual visual-circuit hypothesis. In earnest in Foundations 3.

### Axis 2 — Evolution (the evolutionary origin of the sense of beauty)

This note is analytic philosophy — the evolutionary dimension is weaker than in aesthetics. Yet there are *implicit* evolutionary assumptions in Goodman·Wollheim.

**Goodman**: does not treat the *evolution* of the symbolic capacity. Yet the fact that humans develop symbol systems *in every culture* suggests evolutionary universality. → **an implicit evolutionary assumption**.

**Danto**: treats art's *history* but not evolution. Art *ended* in 1964 — a rupture, not evolution.

**Dickie**: treats the sociological *history* of the institution. No evolution.

**Wollheim**: *seeing-in* derives from the *general capacity of human visual perception* — a hypothesis of a byproduct of evolutionary adaptation (not explicit; later evolutionary aesthetics makes it explicit).

**Carroll**: narrative cognition = the human *narrative instinct* (the story-telling animal — Gottschall as a successor). An evolutionary assumption.

→ Full evolutionary aesthetics (Darwin·Pinker·Dutton·Dissanayake) comes in earnest in neuroaesthetics. **This note is the note where the evolution axis is weakest** — self-critique.

> **🧠 Axis decision memo (evolution)**: Analytic philosophy is *systematically indifferent* to the evolutionary dimension. Yet implicit evolutionary assumptions are scattered through Goodman·Wollheim·Carroll. **Full reinforcement obligatory in the next note (Foundations 3) — the evolution axis grows weaker in this note (accelerating self-critique)**.

### Axis 3 — Culture (the definition of beauty by cultural sphere)

**Goodman**: *Ways of Worldmaking* — the making of multiple worlds. Culture·science·art·the everyday each constitute their own *world*. → **compatible with cultural relativism** — though a universal symbolic capacity is assumed.

**Danto**: confined to Western modernism — the Brillo Box is art only inside the *Western museum*. Non-Western art (African masks etc.) is treated only in the form *absorbed by the Western artworld*. → **a Euro-American limit** (inheriting the limits of Kant·Hegel in aesthetics).

**Dickie**: the very definition of the *artworld* is a *modern Western institution*. Non-Western *traditional craft·religious art* belongs to separate institutions (Dickie's text is inadequate on non-Western application). → the target of the critique of **the cultural confinement of institutional theory** (Wilfried van Damme as a successor).

**Wollheim**: an analysis of *painting* — almost confined to Western painting (da Vinci·Manet·Caravaggio). Seeing-in is assumed to be a universal capacity.

**Carroll**: narrative theory — the *art-history narrative* is Western-centered (a critique of Greenberg's modernism narrative). Yet the *universality of narrative cognition* is universal.

> **🧠 Axis decision memo (culture)**: Analytic philosophy is *even weaker on the cultural dimension* than aesthetics. From Plato to Adorno there was at least the Greece↔Germany↔20th-century comparison, but analytic philosophy is confined to *Anglo-American analysis + modernist America*. **Non-Western philosophy of art (Mohanty's *Indian Aesthetics*·Munro's *Oriental Aesthetics*) is absent from this note — a separate domain**. Yet Goodman's multiple worlds is a candidate for the possibility of non-Western integration.

### Axis 4 — Society (class·institution·market as determinants of beauty)

This note is **the note richest on the society axis** — Dickie's institutional theory takes the social dimension in earnest.

**Goodman**: the social dimension is weak. Centered on the *individual cognition* of the symbolic capacity. Yet *exemplification* depends on social convention.

**Danto**: presents the concept of the *artworld* (the 1964 essay) — the direct input to Dickie. Warhol's *Brillo Box* is art only within the social network of *the Stable Gallery + critics + museums*. → **the first explicit statement of social determination in earnest**.

**Dickie's institutional theory (the most social)**:
- **Artifactuality**: made by humans (not natural objects)
- **Conferral of status**: a *representative of the artworld (some person acting on behalf of the artworld)* confers the *status of candidate for appreciation*
- **The artworld = an informal institution**: the *loose institutional network* of painters·galleries·critics·viewers·museums·schools·magazines, etc.
- → **art = the product of a social institution**. The direct answer to Q2: "what criterion?" → **an artifact recognized by the artworld**
- **Critique** (Carroll·Beardsley): circularity (the artworld = the people who deal with art = the people who recognized art). Sociological reduction.

**Wollheim**: the social dimension is weak. Centered on painting's *individual expression*.

**Carroll**: the *narrative community* — the identification of art occurs within the *historical narrative community*. The social dimension is absorbed into the *historical narrative*.

→ **The direct precursor of the subsequent sociology of Bourdieu·Becker**: Dickie 1974 → Becker's *Art Worlds* (1982) → Bourdieu's *The Rules of Art* (1992). In earnest in sociology-of-art.

> **🧠 Axis decision memo (society)**: Dickie's institutional theory is the strongest *social answer* to Q2 of aesthetics. Adorno's autonomy → *redefined* as autonomy within Dickie's institution (U-04 redefinition of meaning). Yet the critiques of institutional theory's *cultural confinement* (Western institutions) and *circularity* remain unresolved — Bourdieu's sociological critique follows.

## ④ 🔴 Cross-Field Interaction (MM-13 candidate N=2)

This note is analytic aesthetics — *analysis by field* is refined, but the *field-integration* dimension is weaker than in aesthetics.

### Goodman — the most cross-field
- The **5 symptoms** apply *symmetrically* to music·visual art·literature·theater·dance. Painting is dense + syntactically replete / music is syntactically replete / literature has syntactic notationality / dance is multi-medium / theater is complex reference.
- **Allographic vs Autographic**: music·literature (identity preserved through score·text) vs painting·sculpture (original = identity)
- → quantifying the *differences of symbolic structure* by field. A tool of *precise classification of fields* rather than field integration.
- **Creation**: symbol-system *worldmaking* — each field produces a different world

### Danto — centered on painting·visual art
- Principal cases in painting (Warhol·Pollock·Rauschenberg)
- Application to film·literature made explicit (*The Philosophical Disenfranchisement of Art* 1986)
- **Cross-field**: the modernism → postmodernism *art-history narrative* — the hypothesis that 1960s visual art applies to other fields as well
- **Creation**: a new *interpretive community* confers new meaning — novelty = novelty of meaning

### Dickie — the cross-field institution
- Institutional theory *applies regardless of field* — music·visual art·literature·theater each have their own *disciplinary artworld*
- **Cross-field**: the music world·art world·literary world are separate institutions but influence each other (music criticism influencing art criticism, etc.)
- **Creation**: occurs when the institution *confers status* on a new work — novelty at the level of social convention

### Wollheim — centered on painting
- *Painting as an Art* — a precise analysis of painting
- Only suggests the *possibility of applying seeing-in* to other fields (music·literature)
- **Field confinement self-stated**

### Carroll — film·mass art in earnest
- *A defense of mass art* — film·TV as *genuine art*
- Cross-field narrative — film absorbing painting·literature, painting influenced by film
- **Creation**: the crossing of cross-field narratives — each field's *historical narrative* influences other fields

### 🔴 Integrated insight (progress on Dohun's Q3 answer — this note's cross-field answer)

**Additions to Dohun's Q3 answer (field-integration mechanisms)**:
1. **Difference of symbolic structure** (Goodman): fields = different symbol systems — influence = the *translation* of symbols
2. **The artworld = per-field institutions** (Dickie): field influence = the human network between institutions (e.g., music critics ↔ art critics)
3. **Narrative crossing** (Carroll): cross-field influence = the *crossing points* of historical narratives (e.g., film ↔ painting in 1920s–30s modernism)
4. **Ontological difference** (Wollheim's allographic vs autographic): fields = different ontologies — influence = ontological transformation (score → performance → recording)

**Comparison with the integration in aesthetics** (U-04 return):
- The integration in aesthetics: Aristotle's tragedy·Hegel's 5-field evolution·Nietzsche's Apollonian-Dionysian·Wagner's Gesamtkunstwerk — *metaphysical·historical-philosophical* integration
- **This note's integration**: Goodman's symbols / Dickie's institution / Carroll's narrative — *analytic-philosophical separation·translation·crossing*
- → the two integrations are *complementary*. Metaphysical integration (the whole) ↔ analytic integration (translation across fields)

**Creation mechanisms (analytic philosophy)**:
- Goodman: founding a new symbol system → making a new world
- Danto: a change of artworld theory → a new interpretation
- Dickie: the institution confers new status → a new work
- Wollheim: a new mode of expression
- Carroll: advancing a narrative stage

→ Adding *5 analytic forms* to the 6 forms of aesthetics. **The creation mechanisms expand to 11 forms** (the accumulation of Dohun's Q3 answer).

## ⑤ Chronological Appendix (R-03 4-act retrofit)

This section is *appendix*. The R-11 main text is the 4 axes of ③.

### Act 1 — The emergence of analytic aesthetics (1950s–1960s)
- 1956 — Morris Weitz, "art cannot be defined" (Wittgenstein's family resemblance) — the era of rejecting definitional attempts
- 1964 — **Danto** "The Artworld" (essay) — the first appearance of the artworld concept + the analysis of Warhol's *Brillo Box*
- 1968 — **Goodman** *Languages of Art* — the formalization of symbol theory

**Decisive turn:** Weitz's rejection of definition → Goodman·Danto's re-attempt at definition — *when* is art? + the renewed possibility of definition through artworld theory.

### Act 2 — Formalization (1968–1984)
- 1968 — Wollheim *Art and Its Objects* — expression·seeing-in
- 1974 — **Dickie** *Art and the Aesthetic* — institutional theory v1
- 1981 — Danto *Transfiguration of the Commonplace*
- 1984 — Dickie *The Art Circle* (the refinement of institutional theory v2)
- 1984 — Danto *The End of Art*

**Decisive turn:** Dickie's institutional theory + Danto's end of art — the formalization of definition at the social·historical level.

### Act 3 — Critique·Expansion (1990s–2000s)
- 1992 — Bourdieu *The Rules of Art* — the sociological critique
- 1997 — Danto *After the End of Art* — pluralism after the end
- 1999 — Carroll *Philosophy of Art* — the formalization of narrative theory
- 2000s — the integration of analytic aesthetics·neuroaesthetics·cognitive science (Currie·Levinson)

**Decisive turn:** Danto·Dickie's analysis → Bourdieu's sociology + Carroll's narrative — from *internal definition* to *external critique·narrative position*.

### Act 4 — Pluralization (2010–present)
- Digital·NFT art — the debate on the applicability of Goodman's *identity*·Dickie's *institution*
- Generative-AI art — under Dickie's institution, the conferrer of status = AI? Human? The artworld?
- Dohun's Q3 in earnest: cross-field *digital integration* (film·games·VR·multimedia)

**Decisive turn:** Digital·AI → the *retrial* of Goodman·Dickie. How analytic aesthetics adapts to the 21st century.
