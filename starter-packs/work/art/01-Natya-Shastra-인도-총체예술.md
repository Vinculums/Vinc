- **India's internal diversity**: the North (Hindustani) ≠ the South (Carnatic) / Veda ≠ Upanishad ≠ Purana ≠ Tantra ≠ Bhakti — this note is confined to the *classical Sanskrit tradition*
- **The non-Western ≠ "pre-Western"**: that the Natya Shastra is *2,200 years ahead of* Wagner is a temporal priority, but it is NOT a *primitive stage of Western development*. It is an essentially *different system*.

## ① Definition and Core Concepts

The ***Natya Shastra*** (नाट्यशास्त्र) = "the science of drama and total art"

- Author: **Bharata Muni** (a legendary figure — the multiple-redaction hypothesis rather than a single author)
- Period: **200 BC – AD 200** (the consensus of most scholars — roughly *2,050–2,200 years ahead of* Wagner's *Das Kunstwerk der Zukunft*, 1849)
- Length: **6,000+ ślokas (verses)**, 36–37 chapters
- Language: Sanskrit
- Subject: **nāṭya** = the integration of the 5 fields of drama + dance + music + the visual + literature, plus stage, acting, audience, aesthetic sentiment, direction, costume, makeup, convention — *everything*

**Core concepts**:

1. **nāṭya** (नाट्य): the integrated performing art. A *single, unseparated art*. Different from Western *theatre* — music, dance, poetry, and the visual are *originally one*.

2. **rasa** (रस — "taste, essence"): the *completed experience* of aesthetic sentiment. The emotion the spectator *tastes* in the performance. **8 basic rasas** (Bharata) + **a 9th added** (Abhinavagupta, 11th century):
   - **śṛṅgāra** (शृङ्गार) — love, romance, beauty (rose, spring)
   - **hāsya** (हास्य) — laughter, comedy (white)
   - **karuṇa** (करुण) — compassion, sorrow (gray)
   - **raudra** (रौद्र) — anger, fury (red)
   - **vīra** (वीर) — courage, the heroic (golden)
   - **bhayānaka** (भयानक) — fear, terror (black)
   - **bībhatsa** (बीभत्स) — disgust, revulsion (blue)
   - **adbhuta** (अद्भुत) — wonder, amazement (yellow)
   - **śānta** (शान्त) — peace, stillness (white, jasmine) — added by Abhinavagupta, the highest rasa (leading to liberation, mokṣa)

3. **bhāva** (भाव — "emotional state"): the *raw material* of rasa. The *primal emotion* of characters and situations. Bhāvas combine and sublimate into rasa.
   - **sthāyibhāva** (8–9 *durable emotions*): rati (love), hāsa (laughter), śoka (sorrow), krodha (anger), utsāha (energy), bhaya (fear), jugupsā (disgust), vismaya (wonder), śama (peace)
   - **vyabhicāribhāva** (33 *transitory emotions*): shame, longing, arrogance, etc.
   - **sāttvikabhāva** (8 *bodily signs*): trembling, tears, cold sweat, excitement, etc. — bodily mimesis

4. **abhinaya** (अभिनय — "expression, acting"): the 4 instruments for conveying rasa:
   - **āṅgika** — the body (gesture, posture, the mudra hand signs)
   - **vācika** — language (dialogue, song)
   - **āhārya** — the visual (costume, makeup, stage, props)
   - **sāttvika** — the inner (emotional authenticity, bodily signs)
   - → The 4 abhinayas are the *mechanism of field integration* — no Western-style separation.

5. **dhvani** (ध्वनि — "resonance, suggestion"): the 9th-century Ānandavardhana's *Dhvanyāloka* — the *suggested meaning* of poetry and art is the essence. Direct meaning < suggested meaning. (An affinity with Western ekphrasis, free indirect discourse, and modernist *suggestion*.)

## ③ 🔴 Simultaneous 4-Axis Analysis (R-11 — the first application in the non-Western area)

### Axis 1 — Cognition (the cognitive mechanism of rasa)

- **Rasa cognition** = the conversion from bhāva (raw material) to *tasting (āsvāda)*. NOT *direct emotion*, but a *tasting* accompanied by *aesthetic distance*.
- **Abhinavagupta's theory of aesthetic distance** — rasa is *generalization (sādhāraṇīkaraṇa)* — personal emotion → universal emotion. Parallel to Kant's *subjective universality* (but 1,000 years earlier, with a *completely different grounding* — Kant via cognitive faculties / Abhinavagupta via the dissolution of self reaching śānta)
- **Dhvani cognition** — the *leap* from direct meaning to suggested meaning. Poet–reader collaborative cognition.
- **Potential consistency with neuroaesthetics (recursion to neuroaesthetics)** — is Abhinavagupta's dissolution of self consistent with Vessel's DMN deactivation? (DMN = self-related) But unresolved.

> **🧠 Axis decision memo (cognition)**: Rasa theory has an *affinity* with Aristotle's *katharsis* (purification) + Kant's *subjective universal* (aesthetics) + Wollheim's *seeing-in* (philosophy-of-art), yet is a *completely different system*. **Translation limits**: rasa = "taste" + "sentiment" + "essence" + "quintessence" — no single English word. **Indian aesthetic cognition = the 4 dimensions of distance + generalization + suggestion + bodily mimesis** — a *non-reductive answer* to the Western 5 dimensions (Plato, Aristotle, Kant, Hegel, etc.).

### Axis 2 — Evolution (applied with caution — evolutionary theory is itself of Western origin)

This note's self-critique: *evolutionary theory* is a 19th-century Western product — applying it *retroactively* to Indian aesthetics risks reduction.

- **India's native conception of time**: cyclical (yuga, kalpa) — different from Western linear evolution
- **The ageless assumption of rasa theory**: Bharata regards rasa as *eternal truth* — not an evolutionary product (though a modern interpretation is possible)
- **However**, applicability of Pinker's and Dutton's evolutionary aesthetics (recursion to neuroaesthetics):
  - The universality of rasa (8–9 human emotions) = compatible with the evolutionary-universality hypothesis
  - sāttvikabhāva (trembling, tears, cold sweat) = the autonomic nervous system — evolutionary emotional signs

> **🧠 Axis decision memo (evolution)**: The evolution axis is applied *with caution* (consistent with Dohun's explicit statement). India's native *cyclical time* — a risk of evolutionary reduction. But rasa universality and bodily mimesis are compatible with evolutionary aesthetics. **The evolution axis at a deliberately *low priority* within the 3.5/4 consistency** — the obligation to avoid Western reduction.

### Axis 4 — Society (the Indian art society)

- **Caste and nāṭya**: originally nāṭya was the *fifth Veda* — accessible to all castes (the 4 Vedas were restricted to Brahmins). But later caste separation (actors confined to the śūdra caste, etc.)
- **Patron relations**: royal, temple, and merchant patronage — parallel to Bourdieu's field
- **Temple dance** (devadāsī): temple-dedicated dancers (from BC to the 19th century) — the integration/contradiction of religion + art + society + sexuality/labor. Abolished by the British colonial government in 1947 (the *colonization* by colonialism — reduction to "Hindu morality")
- **Colonialism and nationalism**:
  - 19th-century British colonialism *classified and reduced* Indian art (Orientalism — recursion to Said, literature-foundations)
  - The 20th-century nationalist revival — Tagore (Nobel, 1913), Coomaraswamy, Rukmini Devi (the revival of Bharatanatyam)
- **The contemporary**: Indian cinema (Bollywood — music + dance + drama + the visual = the *modern nāṭya* hypothesis), the digital, the diaspora

> **🧠 Axis decision memo (society)**: the Indian art society = religion + caste + kingship + colonialism + nationalism + diaspora, 5+ dimensions. **Bourdieu's field theory** (sociology-of-art) is applicable to India — but without directly reducing it to the *Western bourgeois* model.
