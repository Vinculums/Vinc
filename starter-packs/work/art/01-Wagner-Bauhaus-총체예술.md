## ② Advanced Analysis of Wagner's Gesamtkunstwerk

### Founding texts
- **1849** *Die Kunst und die Revolution* (Art and Revolution) — Greek tragedy as the peak of field integration; modernity as separation and decay
- **1849** *Das Kunstwerk der Zukunft* (The Artwork of the Future) — the founding of the Gesamtkunstwerk
- **1851** *Oper und Drama* (Opera and Drama) — the theory of organic integration of music-poetry-drama

### Core proposition
> "Each artistic field must not be a *means* to another field but must *mutually interpenetrate*. Music, poetry, drama, stage, acting, and architecture form *a single organic unit*."

- **Return to Greek tragedy** — the separation of fields is decay since the Renaissance. Tragedy (music + drama + dance + poetry + mask) is the model.
- **Music at the peak** — Schopenhauer's influence (music = the direct expression of the will, aesthetics; Nietzsche's return)
- **Poetry = the womb of music** — poetry conceives music (a particular utterance demands a particular musical form)

### The advanced work — *Der Ring des Nibelungen* (1869–76)
- An integrated cycle of 4 operas: *Das Rheingold*·*Die Walküre*·*Siegfried*·*Götterdämmerung*
- 16 hours · 100+ Leitmotifs · Germanic mythology · philosophical allegory
- **Leitmotif** = a musical motif for each character, object, concept, and emotion — music functions as *narrative text* (a direct precursor to the film score, music-foundations; a return)
- Poetry (written directly by Wagner — *Stabreim* alliteration) + music (the peak of chromatic harmony) + stage (specially designed at Bayreuth) in organic integration

### Bayreuth Festspielhaus (1876)
- Wagner built his own theater — inventing the medium itself
- Audience darkening — concentration and immersion on the stage
- Hidden orchestra pit ("mystic abyss") — one does not know where the music comes from · mystery
- Symmetrical fan-shaped seating — the stage seen equally from every seat
- → **A direct precursor to the modern cinema and concert hall** (comparative-arts; the invention of media)

### *Tristan und Isolde* (composed 1859 · premiered 1865)
- **The Tristan chord** — F-B-D♯-G♯ — delayed resolution · the peak of chromaticism
- A *fracture* in the age of tonality — a direct precursor to Schoenberg's atonality 60 years later (music-foundations; a return)
- The most refined single work of music-poetry-drama integration (a 3-hour continuous dialectic of love and death)
- **Liebestod (Love-Death)** — music, poetry, and drama fuse at the peak of a single emotion

### History of influence
- **Music**: late Romanticism (Mahler·Strauss·Bruckner) → Schoenberg's atonality → the film score
- **Theater and opera**: the modern *age of the director* (Adolphe Appia·Edward Gordon Craig·Wieland Wagner)
- **Film**: Eisenstein·Hitchcock·Coppola·Lucas (*Star Wars* = a direct application of the Leitmotif — Williams)
- **Architecture**: fan-shaped seating + audience darkening = every modern theater
- **Ideology**: Wagner's anti-Semitism → absorbed by Nazism (1933–45) — Adorno's *In Search of Wagner* (1952) critique, aesthetics · sociology-of-art (a return)
- **Philosophy**: Nietzsche's *Birth of Tragedy* (1872) dedicated to Wagner → later break (*The Case of Wagner* 1888)

### Critique (Adorno + a return to Dohun's explicit U-21)
- **Totalitarian danger**: integration strips the audience of autonomy + imposes a single viewpoint
- **Racism and nationalism**: Germanic mythology + denigration of Jews — political implications
- **Risk of violating U-21 preservation of diversity**: Wagner's integration is a *single* integration — a tension between Dohun's explicit "diverse viewpoints" + Wagner's *totality*
- → The core dialectic of this note: **organic integration (Wagner)** vs **preservation of diversity (Dohun·Adorno)** — an unresolved dialectic

## ③ Advanced Analysis of the Bauhaus Integrated School (1919–1933)

### Founding
- **1919 Weimar launch** — Walter Gropius's *Bauhaus Manifesto* (1919): "Architects, sculptors, painters — we must all return to craft! The *total work of art (Gesamtkunstwerk)* made by free and self-respecting craftsmen — *the new building of the future*."
- A direct return to Wagner ("Gesamtkunstwerk") — but *expanded* from the *work unit* to the *educational·industrial·architectural* unit
- Over 14 years (Weimar 1919–25 → Dessau 1925–32 → Berlin 1932–33) — closed by the Nazis in 1933

### Curricular integration
- **Vorkurs (preliminary course)** — Itten·Albers·Moholy-Nagy: fundamentals of color·form·material·composition (field-independent)
- **Painting**: Kandinsky (1922–33) — point·line·plane·color·form / Klee (1920–31) — *Pedagogical Sketchbook* (1925) — the play and movement of the line / Feininger
- **Sculpture**: Schlemmer
- **Architecture**: Gropius·Hannes Meyer·Mies van der Rohe (the last director)
- **Design and furniture**: Marcel Breuer (Wassily chair 1925·Cesca chair 1928)·Mies (Barcelona chair 1929)
- **Textiles**: Gunta Stölzl·Anni Albers — abstract textiles
- **Printing and typography**: Herbert Bayer·László Moholy-Nagy — the *Universal* typeface·sans-serif
- **Photography**: Moholy-Nagy's *Vision in Motion* — the *new vision* movement
- **Theater**: Oskar Schlemmer's *Triadic Ballet* (1922) — geometric costumes + music (Hindemith) + dance + stage integration = the Bauhaus *Wagnerian* work
- **Stage workshop (Bühne)**: Schlemmer·Moholy-Nagy — integration of geometry + the human body + media

### Core principles
- **Form follows function** — Sullivan (1896) → the peak at Bauhaus
- **Field integration** — painting·sculpture·architecture·design·theater·photography in *equal* collaboration (no hierarchy)
- **Integration of craft and industry** — the influence of Arts and Crafts (Morris) + the acceptance of industrial mass production
- **Advanced use of new materials** — steel·glass·concrete·plastic·plywood
- **The founding of the International Style** — the late 1920s

### Decisive works and movements
- **Triadic Ballet** (Schlemmer 1922) — a field-integration work
- **The Dessau school building** (Gropius 1925–26) — a canon of modernist architecture
- **The Wassily chair** (Breuer 1925) — the founding of the tubular-steel chair
- **Barcelona Pavilion** (Mies 1929) — flowing space
- **Bauhausbücher** (14 volumes, 1925–30) — integrated publications

### The 1933 closure + exile + American influence
- The 1933 Nazi closure ("cultural Bolshevism"·"degeneracy")
- Exile: Gropius (Harvard GSD)·Mies (IIT Chicago)·Moholy-Nagy (New Bauhaus Chicago → IIT Institute of Design)·Albers (Black Mountain → Yale)·Breuer (Harvard·New York)
- A decisive influence on American modernist design and architecture — Knoll·Herman Miller·International Style skyscrapers
- **Black Mountain College** (NC, 1933–57): Albers (Bauhaus) + Cage·Cunningham·Rauschenberg·Kline·Olson — the *postwar American integrated school* (a direct descendant of the Bauhaus)

### History of influence
- **Architecture**: the International Style·MoMA·Brutalism·Mid-century modern
- **Design**: Dieter Rams (Braun) → Apple (Jonathan Ive) — minimalist design in direct descent from the Bauhaus
- **Graphics and typography**: Helvetica (1957) → digital typefaces → modern UI/UX
- **Education**: Black Mountain → Cal Arts → every modernist design school
- **Modern field integration**: *integrated design consultancies* such as IDEO·Frog Design — the industrial successors of the Bauhaus

### Cases compared with the Bauhaus
- **Vkhutemas** (Moscow, 1920–30) — the parallel Soviet integrated school (Lissitzky·Rodchenko·Tatlin) — mutual influence with the Bauhaus
- **Black Mountain College** (1933–57) — the American direct descendant of the Bauhaus
- **Cal Arts** (1961–) — Disney-sponsored·Cage·Baldessari — a successor in field-integrated education
- **Royal College of Art (London)·Rietveld Academy (Amsterdam)·HfG Ulm (a direct Bauhaus successor 1953–68)** — design integration

## ④ Wagner vs Bauhaus Comparison (Dialectic)

| Dimension | Wagner's Gesamtkunstwerk | Bauhaus |
|---|---|---|
| **Unit** | A single work (an opera cycle) | School·curriculum·movement + industry |
| **Goal** | Recovery of Greek tragedy (spirit·myth) | Integrated design for industrial society (function) |
| **Hierarchy** | Music at the peak + poetry·drama subordinate | Fields *equal* (no hierarchy) |
| **Time** | Late-19th-century Romanticism·synthesis | 20th-century modernism·function |
| **Medium** | An own theater (Bayreuth) | School·industry·architecture |
| **Ideology** | Germanic nationalism (Wagner) | Socialism·internationalism |
| **Outcome** | Late-Romantic music·the film score | Modernist design·the International Style |
| **Critique** | Totalitarianism·racism | Functionalist reduction·poverty of content (Tom Wolfe's *From Bauhaus to Our House* 1981) |

**Dialectic**: the two models are *two forms* of field integration:
- Wagner: **organic integration** — interpenetration of fields within a single work
- Bauhaus: **modular integration** — the autonomy of fields + collaboration + education

→ **Modern field integration = the dialectic of the two forms**:
- Diaghilev's *Ballets Russes* = the Wagner model + Bauhaus collaboration
- Black Mountain·Cal Arts = direct descendants of the Bauhaus model
- Film = a fusion of both models (director = Wagner / collaboration = Bauhaus)
- Games·VR·generative AI = the acceleration of modular integration
