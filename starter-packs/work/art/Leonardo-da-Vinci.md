# Leonardo da Vinci

## ① One-line essence

**"Painting is a science (la pittura è scienza)"** (the thesis of the *Trattato della pittura*) — observation · anatomy · optics · mathematics integrated within painting as *a single method of knowing* — sfumato (contourless tonal transition) + anatomical precision + engineering imagination = the Renaissance peak of the *uomo universale* (universal man).

## ② Life and historical context (4 axes)

- **Life**: 1452 Vinci (near Florence) — 1519 Amboise (Clos Lucé, France). Illegitimate son of the notary Ser Piero — excluded from university Latin education → the formative primacy of *empirical observation* ("I am not a man of letters but a disciple of experience"). From c.1466 apprenticed in Verrocchio's Florence workshop → 1482–99 the Milan court of Ludovico Sforza (his self-recommendation letter as military engineer — painter listed last) → moving between Florence and Rome → 1516 moved to France at the invitation of Francis I · died abroad.
- **Cognition**: *the extreme of visual observation* — decomposing bird flight · water vortices · muscle fiber through *slow observation + drawn record*. Mirror writing in reverse (left-handed). About 7,000+ notebook sheets surviving (Codex Atlanticus · Codex Leicester · the Windsor anatomical drawings) — the asymmetry of *very few finished works (about 15 securely attributed paintings)* ↔ *a vast record*.
- **Evolution**: the early Renaissance (Masaccio · Brunelleschi's linear perspective) → **the inaugurator of the High Renaissance** — first of the three peaks Leonardo · Michelangelo · Raphael. The decisive case of the status shift from medieval craftsman → modern *intellectual-artist*.
- **Culture**: Florentine Medici humanism + the Milan court's demand for science and engineering + the church commission system. Under church constraints, he personally dissected about 30 corpses at the hospital (Santa Maria Nuova) — expanding beyond artistic purpose into *science itself*.
- **Society**: a lifelong *artist in service* to court and church (a Bach parallel) — yet many commissions unfinished (*Adoration of the Magi* incomplete · the equestrian monument never cast). After his death the notebooks scattered → centuries of latency → the codices rediscovered and organized in the 19th–20th centuries. *Salvator Mundi* (attribution disputed), $450M at auction in 2017 — the highest price in art-market history.

## ③ Major works and methodology

**Representative works**:
- *Virgin of the Rocks* (c.1483–86 Louvre version · the later London version) — founding sfumato and the pyramidal composition
- *The Last Supper* (1495–98, Santa Maria delle Grazie, Milan) — capturing an instant of psychology (just after "one of you will betray me") + the perspectival vanishing point = the head of Christ
- *Mona Lisa* (c.1503–06/–19, Louvre) — the peak of sfumato · the gaze-tracking effect · the most famous painting in the world
- *Vitruvian Man* (c.1490, drawing) — human proportion + geometry (circle · square) = a diagram of microcosm thought
- *Lady with an Ermine* (c.1489–91) — a forerunner of the psychological portrait
- *Adoration of the Magi* (1481, unfinished) — the underdrawing stage as a laboratory of composition
- **The codices**: Codex Atlanticus (machines · engineering) · Codex Leicester (water · geology — purchased by Bill Gates in 1994) · the Windsor anatomical drawings (drawings of the heart valve · the fetus in the womb — anticipating the modern anatomical plate)

**Distinctive methodology**:
- **Sfumato**: removal of the contour line — a *smoke-like (fumo)* stepless transition of light and shade → volume and psychological ambiguity (the corners of the Mona Lisa's mouth). A head-on renewal of the line-centered Florentine tradition (Botticelli)
- **Anatomical observation**: about 30 personal dissections → precise drawings of muscles · vessels · heart · fetus — *anatomy for the sake of drawing* becoming *anatomy in its own right*. Yet unpublished → limited influence on the medicine of his day (a T8 later-rediscovery structure)
- **Science–art integration**: optics (aerial perspective — the farther, the bluer and hazier) · mathematical proportion (the illustrations for Luca Pacioli's *De divina proportione*, 1509) · geology · hydraulics · flight studies forming *a single system of knowing* with the method of painting
- **Engineering drawing**: the flying machine (ornithopter) · war chariot · canals · gears — founding *drawing = a language of design* (anticipating modern engineering drafting)
- **The method of the unfinished**: since observation never ends, the work never ends — incompleteness itself as the shadow of the methodology

## ④ 🔴 Cross-field influence and creation (MM-13 accelerated)

**Influence within art**:

#### Verrocchio → Leonardo (T1)
- **What**: workshop apprenticeship — transmission of the full range of skills: drawing · oil painting · sculpture · metalwork
- **Path**: entering Verrocchio's Florence workshop from c.1466 → assigned the left angel of the *Baptism of Christ* (c.1472–75) — the Vasari tradition that "Verrocchio, surpassed by his pupil, laid down his brush" (with exaggeration)
- **Timing**: c.1466–1478 (the workshop period)
- **Transformation**: **T1 direct apprenticeship** — yet oil painting (the influx of Flemish technique) and sfumato are Leonardo's own extension. The canonical case of the pupil surpassing the master
- **Basis**: Vasari's *Lives* (1550) / brushwork analysis of the *Baptism of Christ* (Uffizi)

#### Leonardo → Raphael (T2)
- **What**: the pyramidal composition · sfumato · the psychological-portrait format → the High Renaissance synthesis
- **Path**: Raphael's Florence sojourn 1504–08 — direct study of the *Mona Lisa* composition (Raphael's copy drawing survives) → adoption of the pose and composition in the *Maddalena Doni* portrait (c.1506) → the pyramidal composition of the Madonna series
- **Timing**: the shared Florence period, 1504–08
- **Transformation**: **T2 stylistic inheritance** — Raphael transforming Leonardo's ambiguity into *clarity and harmony* (synthesized with Michelangelo's figures)
- **Basis**: Raphael's copy drawing of the Mona Lisa (Louvre) / *Maddalena Doni* (Palazzo Pitti) / Vasari's *Lives*

#### Leonardo → the later anatomical and engineering-drawing tradition (T6 + T8)
- **What**: the format of anatomical and mechanical *drawing = a medium of knowledge* → the modern traditions of the anatomical plate and engineering drafting
- **Path**: the notebooks unpublished · scattered after his death (inherited by Melzi → dispersed) → the Windsor anatomical drawings and others organized and published in the 18th–19th centuries → confirmed to be a path separate from Vesalius's *Fabrica* (1543), hence re-evaluated as an *anticipation* → the symbolic ancestor of the lineage of modern scientific illustration and engineering CAD
- **Timing**: 1489–1513 (the anatomical drawings) → unpublished for centuries → codex scholarship in earnest in the 19th–20th centuries
- **Transformation**: **T6 medium translation** (body · machine → the language of drawing) + **T8 rediscovery** (limited contemporary influence → later re-evaluation). Parallel to the 1829 Bach revival and the 1970s Hildegard rediscovery — except that for Leonardo *the painterly fame was continuous; only the notebooks lay latent*
- **Basis**: the anatomical drawings of the Windsor Royal Collection / the Codex Atlanticus (Ambrosiana) / the scholarship of Kenneth Clark · Martin Kemp

**Art → other fields**:
- **Science**: the observation–record–hypothesis cycle = the art-side anticipation of the method of empirical science (Kemp, *Leonardo da Vinci: The Marvellous Works of Nature and Man*)
- **Optics · perception research**: aerial perspective · tonal transition → a classic case for the study of visual perception (returning to neuroaesthetics — research on the peripheral-vision effect of the Mona Lisa's smile)
- **Popular culture**: the 1911 theft of the *Mona Lisa* → its status as the world's most famous painting / Duchamp's *L.H.O.O.Q.* (1919) — the canon itself as an avant-garde target
- **Psychoanalysis**: Freud's *Leonardo da Vinci and a Memory of His Childhood* (1910) — the prototype of psychoanalytic criticism of an artist (with its methodological controversies)

## ⑤ Answers to the meta-questions

- **Q1 (why define beauty?)**: *grasping the laws of nature with the eye is itself beauty* — painting = science = knowledge of nature. Beauty = the visual verification of natural order (aesthetics — an empiricist transformation of the proportion-and-Idea tradition)
- **Q2 (by what criterion?)**: *precision of observation + proportion + naturalness of tonal transition + capture of psychology*. Closer to Aristotelian observation than to Plato's Idea — though the Vitruvian Man retains the proportional Idea
- **Q3 (cross-field influence and creation)**: within art (Raphael · the High Renaissance) + across fields (anatomy · engineering · optics · psychoanalysis) — *the medium of drawing itself* as the means of crossing fields. Bach's *form* · Leonardo's *drawing* — parallel mechanisms of crossing

## ⑥ Connections and successors

- Forerunners: Verrocchio (T1 apprenticeship) · Masaccio · Brunelleschi (linear perspective) · Alberti (*De pictura*, 1435, theory of painting) · Flemish oil painting (the influx of van Eyck's technique)
- Contemporaries: Michelangelo (rival — the planned 1504 Florence confrontation of *Battle of Anghiari* vs *Battle of Cascina*) · Botticelli · Bramante (Milan colleague) · Luca Pacioli (mathematical collaboration)
- Direct successors: Raphael (T2) · Melzi · Luini and the Leonardeschi · Giorgione (the Venetian reception of sfumato)
- Distant successors: the anatomical and engineering-drawing tradition (T6+T8) · Duchamp (overturning the canon) · Freud (psychoanalytic criticism)
- Crossings: the scholarship of Kemp · Clark / the *Salvator Mundi* market (returning to sociology-of-art)
