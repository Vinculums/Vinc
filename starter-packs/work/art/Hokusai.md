# Katsushika Hokusai

## ① One-line essence

**"If heaven would grant me but five more years, I could become a true painter (画工)"** (deathbed tradition, age 89) — his lifelong self-chosen name *Old Man Mad About Painting (画狂老人, Gakyō Rōjin)* — ukiyo-e woodblock prints + the Mount Fuji series + the sketch encyclopedia (北斎漫画) = the peak of Edo popular printmaking and the epicenter of **the decisive non-Western → Western (Japonisme) influence**.

## ② Life and historical context (4 axes)

- **Life**: 1760 Katsushika, Edo (present-day Sumida ward, Tokyo) — 1849 Edo (age 88–89; 90 by East Asian reckoning). 1778 entered the studio of the ukiyo-e master Katsukawa Shunshō (actor prints) → expelled · drifting among schools → **some 30 name changes** (春朗 · 宗理 · 北斎 · 戴斗 · 為一 · 画狂老人卍) + a tradition of **93 house moves** — a *ritual of self-renewal*, changing his name at each stylistic turn. His greatest series (*Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji*) was produced in his **seventies** — a canonical case of a late-life creative peak.
- **Cognition**: *a cognition of sketching all things* — accumulating direct sketches of waves · birds · yōkai · sumo wrestlers · daily customs, the whole of creation (about 4,000 plates in the Hokusai Manga) + a *selective absorption* of Western perspective and shading (Dutch copperplate engraving — the rangaku circuit via Nagasaki)
- **Evolution**: ukiyo-e centered on beauties (Utamaro) and actors (Sharaku) → Hokusai **elevated the landscape print into an independent genre** → carried on by Hiroshige (*Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō*, 1833–34) — the late golden age of Edo printmaking
- **Culture**: Edo chōnin (townsman) popular culture — the print = a commercial mass medium of the **four-party workshop division of labor** among publisher (版元) · painter · carver (彫師) · printer (摺師) (in contrast to the Western *solitary genius* model). The adoption of the imported pigment **Prussian blue (bero-ai)** was the material condition of the color innovation of the Fuji series
- **Society**: produced under national seclusion (sakoku) → after the opening of the ports (1853~), prints flowed out to Europe — the 1856 anecdote of Félix Bracquemond discovering the *Hokusai Manga* in ceramic packing paper (by tradition) → the 1867 Paris Exposition Universelle → the explosion of **Japonisme**. At home a *popular entertainment medium* ↔ after Western reception, *canonical art* — an asymmetry of evaluation circuits (sociology-of-art)

## ③ Major works and methodology

**Representative works**:
- ***Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji (富嶽三十六景)*** (c.1830–33 — 10 plates added on its success, 46 in fact) — a series of *viewpoint, season, and daily-life variations on the single motif* of Mount Fuji
  - *Under the Great Wave off Kanagawa (神奈川沖浪裏, The Great Wave off Kanagawa)* — among the most reproduced images in the world
  - *Fine Wind, Clear Morning (凱風快晴, Red Fuji)* · *Rainstorm Beneath the Summit (山下白雨)* — the so-called three masterworks
- ***Hokusai Manga (北斎漫画)*** (1814~, 15 volumes; the final volume posthumous, 1878) — an encyclopedia of about 4,000 sketches: figures · flora and fauna · yōkai · architecture · decomposition of movement
- *A Tour of Waterfalls in Various Provinces (諸国瀧廻り)* (c.1833) — the waterfall series · *Remarkable Views of Bridges in Various Provinces (諸国名橋奇覧)* — the bridge series
- *One Hundred Views of Mount Fuji (富嶽百景)* (1834–35, picture album) — 100 views of Fuji · the source of the deathbed tradition (the postscript)
- *One Hundred Ghost Tales (百物語)* (c.1831–32) — ghost-story yōkai prints
- Late brush paintings: *Sleeping Beauty* · the festival-cart ceiling paintings at Obuse (小布施) (wave designs, 1840s)

**Distinctive methodology**:
- **Ukiyo-e woodblock (a division-of-labor mass medium)**: painter's design → carver → printer, polychrome printing (nishiki-e 錦絵) — a medium of *multiple originals and popular circulation*. An ontology different from the aesthetics of the single-original painting (returning to comparative-arts)
- **The Thirty-six Views method**: a *viewpoint-variation series* on a single object (Fuji) — a double structure of foreground labor scenes and the sacred mountain in the distance. The direct forerunner of Monet's *Rouen Cathedral* and *Haystacks* series (1890s)
- **Manga (北斎漫画)**: 'manga (漫画)' = pictures drawn as the brush goes — *encyclopedization of the observational sketch*. A parallel to Leonardo's notebooks (T7 accidental convergence — the compulsion to draw and record all things)
- **Wave curves · geometric composition**: the claw-like wave of the *Great Wave* + arc-and-triangle composition (the wave's triangle ↔ Fuji's triangle in correspondence) — a synthesis of copperplate perspective and Japanese planar composition
- **Prussian blue**: full adoption of an imported chemical pigment — the innovation of indigo-gradation printing (藍摺, aizuri). A case of material–aesthetic coupling

## ④ 🔴 Cross-field influence and creation (MM-13 accelerated)

**Influence within Japan**:
- Hiroshige's inheritance of the landscape print (*Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō*, 1833–34 — a contemporary rivalry) → the canon of late-Edo landscape
- Pupils and school (the Hokusai school) + the Obuse brush-painting tradition

#### Hokusai → Monet · Van Gogh · Degas (Japonisme) (T9 + T6)
- **What**: flat color planes · bold cropping (trimming) · asymmetry · the series concept → the formal renewal of Impressionism and Post-Impressionism
- **Path**: the opening of the ports (1853~) → prints flowing to Europe → the 1856 Bracquemond *Hokusai Manga* discovery anecdote → the Japanese pavilion at the 1867 Paris Exposition Universelle → the Paris dealers (Siegfried Bing's *Le Japon Artistique*, 1888–91) →
  - **Monet**: collected about 250 ukiyo-e (extant at the Giverny house — 23 by Hokusai) → the *Thirty-six Views* series method → the *Haystacks* · *Rouen Cathedral* · *Water Lilies* series + the Japanese bridge at Giverny
  - **Van Gogh**: copies of ukiyo-e (1887 oil copies of Hiroshige's *Bridge in the Rain* and *Flowering Plum Orchard*) + in an 1888 letter to Theo, likening the *Great Wave*'s wave to "claws" with high praise → the color planes and contour lines of the Arles period
  - **Degas**: bird's-eye views · diagonals · daring cropping of the picture plane (the composition of the dancer series) — a parallel to the movement sketches of the Hokusai Manga
- **Timing**: made in the 1830s → discovered in Europe 1856–67 → absorbed in the 1870s–90s (about 30–60 years)
- **Transformation**: **T9 borrowing/appropriation + T6 medium translation** (transplanting the grammar of the woodblock mass medium → into the single-original oil medium). **The first major case of decisive non-Western → Western influence**, preceding Picasso ← African masks (1907) — yet Japonisme too must carry the critique of *stripping the source of its context* (an Edo popular medium → Western exoticism) (returning to east-west-aesthetics-comparison)
- **Basis**: the catalog of Monet's Giverny collection / Van Gogh's letters (1888) + the two 1887 oil copies / Bing's *Le Japon Artistique* / records of the 1867 Exposition

#### Hokusai → Debussy *La Mer* (T6)
- **What**: the *Great Wave* → orchestra — a painting → music medium translation
- **Path**: Debussy's immersion in Japonisme → *La Mer* (1905), **the first-edition score cover directly adopting the wave design of the *Great Wave*** (by tradition, Debussy kept the print in his home)
- **Transformation**: **T6 medium translation** — a parallel node to Mussorgsky's *Pictures at an Exhibition* (painting → music). The wave image → the acoustic structure of the three sea movements
- **Basis**: the 1905 Durand first-edition cover of *La Mer* / Debussy's letters and biographical sources

#### Hokusai → the modern manga lineage (T2 + T8, with the lineage debate noted)
- **What**: the sketches, movement decomposition, and humor of the *Hokusai Manga* → the name 'manga (漫画)' and the visual-encyclopedia tradition → modern manga
- **Path**: the *Hokusai Manga* (1814~) popularized the word 'manga' → Meiji satirical prints (Kiyochika and others) · the influx of Western cartoons (Wirgman's *Japan Punch*, 1862) → 20th-century panel comics → modern manga after Tezuka Osamu (1947 *Shin Takarajima*)
- **Timing**: 1814 → the 20th century (about 130 years)
- **Transformation**: **T2 stylistic inheritance (the name · sketch tradition) + T8 reinterpretation** — but **the scholarly debate must be noted**: the strong counterargument (Frederik Schodt and others) that the direct ancestors of modern manga are Western cartoons and film grammar — the *Hokusai Manga* is a sketchbook, not narrative panel comics. This note restricts itself to the *lineage of name and visual culture* and avoids asserting direct descent (an unfounded single-line lineage = a violation of this graph's principles)
- **Basis**: the 15 volumes of the *Hokusai Manga* / Schodt's *Manga! Manga!* (1983) — lineage caution / discussions of the Japan Society for Studies in Cartoons and Comics

**Prints → other fields**:
- **Design · graphics**: Art Nouveau (mediated by Bing) · the poster (Toulouse-Lautrec's flat color planes) → modern graphic design
- **Circulation of popular imagery**: the *Great Wave* — the emoji (source of the 🌊 design) · merchandise · parody = the global meme-ification of a single print — the print parallel of Murasaki's *Genji* millennium of medium transfer (T6 accelerated)
- **Representation of disaster and nature**: repeatedly summoned in tsunami reporting and climate-crisis visualization (with the scholarly note that the original is a great wave, not a tsunami)

## ⑤ Answers to the meta-questions

- **Q1 (why define beauty?)**: *the infinite discipline of the act of drawing all things itself* — "I have drawn since age six, yet nothing before seventy is worth taking; at one hundred I shall reach the marvelous" (the gist of the *One Hundred Views of Mount Fuji* postscript) — beauty = not completion but *an asymptote of practice*. In contrast with the Western genius-completion model (aesthetics · east-west-aesthetics-comparison)
- **Q2 (by what criterion?)**: *accumulated observation + compositional geometry (the wave–Fuji correspondence) + circulating power of a popular medium*. The criterion of a *multiply printed medium*, not the aura of a single original — a case anticipating Benjamin's reproduction argument by 150 years
- **Q3 (cross-field influence and creation)**: prints → Western painting (Japonisme) · music (Debussy) · design · manga — a key node correcting the West-centered influence graph with an **East → West arrow** (paired with Picasso 1907)
