## §1. Modernist criticism — Greenberg, Fried

### 1-α — Greenberg's formalist modernism

- **Greenberg** *Avant-Garde and Kitsch* 1939, *Modernist Painting* 1960 — the essence of painting = *flatness*
- Each medium must be reduced to *its own essence* (medium specificity)
- Canonization of abstract expressionism (Pollock, Rothko, Newman) — *American art hegemonic*
- Limit: the *kitsch vs avant-garde* dichotomy, medium essentialism unresolved

### 1-β — Michael Fried *Art and Objecthood* 1967

- a critique of minimalism — rejecting *theatricality*
- *absorption* vs *theatricality* — a revival of Diderot 1750
- Later work: Caravaggio, Manet in earnest (absorption in earnest)
- Limit: negating minimalism and conceptual art → later *deconstruction*

### 1-γ — Rosalind Krauss *The Originality of the Avant-Garde* 1985

- deconstructing Greenberg's formalism — *grid as myth*
- *expanded field of sculpture* — the *categorical expansion* of sculpture
- The formal beginning of the October school (Krauss, Bois, Buchloh, Foster)

## §2. The Frankfurt School — Adorno, Horkheimer, Benjamin

### 2-α — Walter Benjamin 1936, *The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction*

- **aura** (historical authenticity) — original vs reproduction
- photography and film dissolve the aura — *democratization + politicization*
- *flâneur*, *arcades project* — urban aesthetics in earnest
- Influence: media theory, visual culture, digital aesthetics

### 2-β — Adorno *Aesthetic Theory* 1970

- art = *the negativity of society*
- *culture industry* (co-authored with Horkheimer, 1947) — a critique of mass culture (jazz, Hollywood)
- *autonomous art* vs *committed art* — the Brecht–Sartre debate
- Music criticism: defending Schoenberg, criticizing jazz (contested)
- Limit: European-hegemonic, ignores non-Western aesthetics

### 2-c — Marcuse, Habermas — successors in critical theory

- Marcuse *One-Dimensional Man* 1964 — capitalist aesthetic homogenization
- Habermas *public sphere* — the role of the aesthetic public sphere
- Influence: political aesthetics, activist art

### 2-d — Mathematical parallels

- *negativity*, *non-identity* (Adorno) ↔ Math 01 undecidability (Gödel)
- *medium specificity* (Greenberg) ↔ the *forgetful* functor (category theory)

## §3. Post-structuralism — Foucault, Derrida, Lacan

### 3-α — Foucault, power and discourse

- *Les mots et les choses* 1966 — *episteme* (an era's framework of cognition)
- *Surveiller et punir* 1975 — *panopticon*, *biopower*
- Art application: the museum = a *power apparatus* (Tony Bennett's *exhibitionary complex*)
- *what is an author* 1969 — deconstructing the author function

### 3-β — Derrida, deconstruction

- *De la grammatologie* 1967 — *différance*, *supplement*
- *La vérité en peinture* 1978 — the painting's *parergon* (frame, outside)
- Aesthetic application: the *deconstruction* of style and canon (the boundary is the essence)

### 3-c — Lacan, psychoanalysis + visual art

- mirror stage, imaginary, symbolic, real
- *gaze* — the power of the look
- Influence: feminist art theory (Mulvey *Visual Pleasure* 1975)
- film theory: *suture*, *identification*

### 3-d — Roland Barthes

- *La mort de l'auteur* 1967 — the death of the author
- *S/Z* 1970 — readerly vs writerly text
- *Camera Lucida* 1980 — *studium* vs *punctum* (the essence of the photograph)

## §4. Baudrillard, the simulacrum

- *Simulacres et simulation* 1981 — *hyperreality*, *simulation*
- The 4 stages of the image: ① reflection ② masking ③ masking the absence of masking ④ pure simulacrum
- Influence: The Matrix (the Wachowskis' film), VR, NFT, AI-generated art
- The *absence of the original* in the digital age — the 21st-century form of Benjamin's aura
- Limit: pessimistic, insufficient on the *possibility of resistance*

## §5. Feminist, post-colonial criticism

### 5-α — Feminist art theory

- **Linda Nochlin** *Why Have There Been No Great Women Artists?* 1971 — a critique of canonical power
- **Laura Mulvey** *Visual Pleasure and Narrative Cinema* 1975 — the male gaze
- **Griselda Pollock** *Vision and Difference* 1988 — deconstructing modernism
- **Judith Butler** *Gender Trouble* 1990 — gender performativity

### 5-β — Post-colonial aesthetics

- **Edward Said** *Orientalism* 1978 — the power of Oriental representation
- **Gayatri Spivak** *Can the Subaltern Speak?* 1988 — the possibility of subaltern speech
- **Homi Bhabha** *The Location of Culture* 1994 — *hybridity*, *third space*
- **bell hooks** *Art on My Mind* 1995 — Black feminist aesthetics

## §6. A critique of canonical power

### 6-α — Mechanisms of canon formation

- museum, art market, academy, curriculum, archive — the *4 powers*
- *whose canon?* — Western, male, heterosexual, upper-middle-class dominant
- Nochlin 1971 → Pollock 1988 → contemporary intervention (Guerrilla Girls, 1985–)

### 6-β — Deconstructing, expanding the canon

- expanded canon (feminist, post-colonial, queer, indigenous)
- *digital canon* — the Internet, social media = a new power
- AI training data (LAION, CommonCrawl) = *21st-century canonical power*
- → directly consistent with "why beauty" U-22 (reputation vs individual)
