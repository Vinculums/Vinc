# sociology-of-art

## ① Definition and Core Concepts

**Sociology of art** analyzes the *social determination, reproduction, power, market, and institutions* of art. **In F3 Neuroaesthetics the social axis weakened** → this note has an *obligation to fully restore the social axis*. **A sociological refinement of F2 Dickie's institutional theory** + **the stage for the full-scale verification of the U-22 candidate (the separation of majority vs. individual perspectives)**.

Key branch points:
- **Modernist criticism (Greenberg)**: medium autonomy, avant-garde vs. kitsch
- **Field theory (Bourdieu)**: art = *cultural capital + a struggle for position in the field* — a sociological deconstruction of aesthetic judgment
- **The cooperative activity of the artworld (Becker)**: art = *collective action* — a network of artists, patrons, critics, audiences, and producers
- **Multiple domains (Heinich)**: art = a *regime of singularity* (scarcity, excellence, authenticity)
- **Sociology of music (Born)**: the power, identity, and technology of music production
- **Class, race, gender (Banks and others)**: the *reproduction* + *exclusion* of the art society

**Recursion to F1–F4 (U-04 in full scale)**:
- **Adorno's autonomy and culture industry** (aesthetics) → refined by Bourdieu's *relative autonomy* of the field (not absolute autonomy — socially dependent)
- **Dickie's institutional theory** (philosophy-of-art) → Becker's *Art Worlds* (1982 — the sociological successor to Dickie 1974) — an attempt to resolve the circularity critique
- **Vessel's DMN–social overlap** (neuroaesthetics) → the possibility of a neural grounding for Bourdieu's *habitus* (embodied society)
- **McLuhan's media determination** (comparative-arts) → Born's technology–society integration (the media–society dialectic of music production)
- **Manovich's algorithmic power** → the sociology of digital labor and platform capitalism (Banks, Born)

**Consistency with Dohun's explicit statement**:
- **U-22 (the separation of majority vs. individual perspectives)**: Dohun's "whichever holds a high percentage among these may well become its reputation" — this note gives a full-scale analysis of the *sociological determination of reputation*. Reputation = the *social-mechanistic accumulation* of majority opinion.
- **Preservation of diverse perspectives**: Bourdieu and Becker analyze the multilayered nature of perspectives *by field position* and *by artworld role*.

## ③ 🔴 Simultaneous 4-Axis Analysis (R-11 candidate — just short of N=5, qualified for formal registration)

### Axis 1 — Cognition (why do humans perceive beauty? — social cognition)

**Bourdieu — cognition is social (habitus)**:
- *habitus* = embodied social dispositions — birth, class, and education *form* aesthetic cognition
- "Taste is not nature but a social product"
- The empirical study in *Distinction* (a 1963–68 survey of 1,217 French respondents) — tastes in music, art, cuisine, and clothing differ strongly by class
- Bourgeois = formal beauty, abstraction / workers = function, practicality, content-focused
- → **Cognition is *programmed* by social position.** A rejection of Kant's universal (aesthetics) — universal cognition = the *naturalization* of bourgeois cognition.

**Becker — cognition is cooperative**:
- Cognition learns *cooperative practices* — the shared conventions of "art worlds" (musical harmony, artistic perspective, etc.)
- Cognition = learned conventions + shared resources
- → Cognition is *collective learning*.

**Heinich — singularity cognition**:
- Cognition of contemporary art = cognition of *scarcity and exceptionality* — differing from the average is aesthetic value
- A work close to the average ≠ art (only in the contemporary)
- → Cognition is *comparative/differential cognition*.

**Greenberg — medium cognition**:
- Modernist cognition = *cognition of the medium itself* — consciousness of painting's flatness, color, and composition
- Academic painting = forgetting the medium / modernism = awareness of the medium
- → Cognition is *medium consciousness*.

**Born — technologically mediated cognition**:
- At IRCAM, musical cognition is mediated by *algorithms and interfaces*
- Technology *constrains and expands* cognition
- → Cognition is *technology–society collaboration*.

> **🧠 Axis decision memo (cognition)**: This note *deconstructs* the Kantian universal (aesthetics) and the neural universal (neuroaesthetics) into *social determination*. **Bourdieu's habitus is central** — the *social grounding* of cognition. **Neural vs. social tension**: the DMN universal (neuroaesthetics) vs. habitus differentiation (Bourdieu) — unresolved (an integrated model of universal neural circuitry + social-learning modification is undetermined).

### Axis 2 — Evolution (the evolutionary origin of aesthetic sense — social evolution)

This note is on the *social evolution* dimension — distinct from human biological evolution.

**Bourdieu — social *reproduction* evolution**:
- Capital reproduction (economic, cultural, social) — the *transmission of position* across generations
- Aesthetic taste as a tool of social-position reproduction
- → **Social evolution = position reproduction + change**.

**Becker — cooperative evolution**:
- Art-world conventions *evolve* over time (the transformation of jazz conventions — New Orleans → bebop → free)
- New conventions = a new art world

**Greenberg — modernist evolution (a direct inheritance from Hegel)**:
- Modernism = the *historical progress* of medium autonomy (recursion to Hegel's spiritual evolution, aesthetics)
- Painting: Renaissance → impression → cubism → abstraction → color field (Pollock, Rothko, Newman) — the apex of medium autonomy
- → **Media evolution = a shift of social power**

**Heinich — the triple-game evolution**:
- Stage 1 (transgression): the avant-garde transgresses existing norms
- Stage 2 (integration): society, market, and museum absorb it
- Stage 3 (critique): a new transgression emerges
- → **Contemporary-art evolution = the triple-game cycle**.

**Born — technology–society co-evolution**:
- Music technology (electronic, digital) ↔ society (studio, internet, platform) co-evolve
- → The *sociological refinement* of McLuhan's media evolution (comparative-arts).

**Banks — labor evolution**:
- The *precaritization* of creative labor (gig economy, platforms, AI) — the evolutionary pressure of the 21st century
- Artist poverty (Abbing) + accelerating digital labor

> **🧠 Axis decision memo (evolution)**: This note's evolution dimension = *social evolution*. Human evolution (neuroaesthetics), media evolution (comparative-arts) + this note's *social evolution* = **3 evolutionary timescales**. **Unresolved**: quantifying the interaction of the three scales (human evolution — Pleistocene / media evolution — decades to centuries / social evolution — decades to a generation) — when the scales mismatch, which takes priority?

### Axis 4 — Society (the determination of beauty by class, institution, and market) — **the full-scale essence of this note**

This note is *full-scale* on the social axis — restoring the F3 weakening.

**Bourdieu — field theory (most refined)**:
- **The field** = an autonomous space of positions — the artistic field, political field, economic field, academic field, etc.
- **Capital**: economic capital / cultural capital (credentials, taste, embodied knowledge) / social capital (networks) / symbolic capital (authority)
- **habitus**: embodied social dispositions — regulating action within the field
- **The struggle for position**: actors within a field mobilize capital to change their position +/-
- **Relative autonomy**: the field is *relatively* autonomous from the outside (politics, economy) — not absolutely autonomous (Adorno's critique of autonomy, aesthetics)
- **Deconstructing aesthetic disinterestedness (Kant)**: disinterestedness = the *social capital* of the economically secure bourgeois (only those without livelihood worries can be disinterested)
- → **A direct answer to the U-22 candidate**: *reputation = the cumulative result of the struggle for position in the field*. Majority opinion = the distribution of power in the field.

**Becker — Art Worlds (most cooperative)**:
- The artwork = *collective cooperative activity* — a Wagner work = composer, performers, instrument makers, score publishers, orchestra, theatre, audience, critics, patrons, ...
- *integrated professionals* (the field's core) / *mavericks* (heretics) / *folk* / *naïve* — 4 positions
- An attempt to resolve the *circularity critique* of Dickie's institutional theory (philosophy-of-art): the artworld = a network of cooperative action (a sociological unit) — not a mere *circular definition*
- A change of conventions = a new art world

**Heinich — the regime of singularity**:
- The contemporary-art society ascribes value to *scarcity* — differing from the majority is aesthetic value
- A sociology of visibility — who is seen is power
- The triple game: transgression → integration → critique

**Greenberg — critical power**:
- The critic has the power to determine social position
- Grounding Abstract Expressionism through media such as *Partisan Review* and *Art News*
- **Sociological critique**: Greenberg's power = Bourdieu's field position (critical capital)

**Born — institutional power**:
- The IRCAM ethnography — state patronage + modernist power + the composer's authority + the hierarchy of computer engineers
- A musical work ≠ the composer alone — a collaborative product of *institutional power + engineers + composer*

**Banks — the politics of labor**:
- The *inequality* of creative labor — differences of entry, wage, and recognition by class, race, and gender
- The digital and platforms *accelerate reproduction* (applying Bourdieu's capital)

> **🧠 Axis decision memo (society)**: This note *fully restores* the social axis — the strongest note. **Bourdieu's field + Becker's cooperation + Heinich's singularity + Born's institution + Banks's labor** = an overlay of 5 social models. **A direct answer to U-22**: reputation = the distribution of field power + the consensus of the cooperative network + the accumulation of visibility. Dohun's explicit "diverse perspectives" = the multilayered nature of perspectives by field position.

## ④ 🔴 Cross-field Interaction (MM-13 candidate — just short of N=5)

This note is sociology — a *sociological analysis by field* + a *cross-field social network*.

**Bourdieu — per-field fields + inter-field relations**:
- The musical field, artistic field, and literary field are each *relatively autonomous fields*
- Mutual capital conversion between fields (cultural capital → authority in the musical field)
- Cross-field influence = capital conversion + movement of persons (Bourdieu himself compared literature, art, and music)

**Becker — per-field art worlds + collaboration**:
- Fields = different art worlds (the jazz world ↔ the classical world ↔ the art world)
- Cross-field collaboration: film = a *cooperative unit* of music + art + literature + theatre + engineers + capitalists — a full-scale sociology of field integration

**Heinich — contemporary art = a field-integrating market**:
- Contemporary museums and biennales *cross fields* (simultaneous exhibition of painting, sculpture, installation, video, performance)
- The value of singularity applies regardless of field

**Born — music-technology-visual integration**:
- IRCAM works integrate music + computer visuals + space
- Digital music = a medium integrating the visual and the aural

**🔴 Sociological mechanisms of cross-field integration (5 new in this note)**:
1. **Inter-field capital conversion** (Bourdieu) — cultural capital converts into cross-field authority

3. **The integrated market** (Heinich) — biennales, festivals, and platforms cross fields
4. **Institutional integration** (Born) — *integrated schools and institutes* like IRCAM, Bauhaus, and Black Mountain
5. **Labor-market integration** (Banks) — on the digital and platforms, creators perform *cross-field labor* (music, art, video, text simultaneously)
