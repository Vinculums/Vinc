# Quantum Mechanics II

## One-line essence
The angular-momentum algebra $[\hat{J}_i, \hat{J}_j] = i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}\hat{J}_k$ determines rotational symmetry and the spin representations, and perturbation theory, the variational method, and scattering theory form the three pillars of real calculation — if QM I is the abstract formalism, QM II is the user's manual for how that formalism meets atoms, many-body systems, and experiment.

## Physical picture
**The discovery of spin — the non-classicality of rotating the measurement axis**: measuring along the $z$-axis with Stern-Gerlach gives two points at ±1/2. Take only one side of that beam and measure again along the $x$-axis, and it splits once more into two points at ±1/2. If it were a classical magnet, an already-aligned beam would have no reason to split again. **Spin is not an external degree of freedom but an internal quantum degree of freedom.** Every qubit is essentially a spin-1/2 system.

**Angular momentum = the generator of rotational symmetry**: from Noether's theorem (classical), rotational symmetry → conservation of angular momentum. In the quantum realm this conserved quantity becomes an operator, and its algebra in turn determines the rotation representations. The reason every particle in the universe carries integer or half-integer spin is the representation theory of SU(2).

**The essential indistinguishability of many-body systems**: two electrons cannot be labeled. Upon exchanging particles 1 and 2:
- Bosons: $\psi(x_2, x_1) = +\psi(x_1, x_2)$ — they pile without limit into the same state. BEC, lasers, superfluids
- Fermions: $\psi(x_2, x_1) = -\psi(x_1, x_2)$ — the same quantum numbers are impossible (Pauli exclusion). **The root of the periodic table, the electronic structure of solids, and the stability of neutron stars**

**The fine structure of the hydrogen atom**: the Bohr model gave only $E_n = -13.6/n^2$, but the actual spectrum splits within the same $n$. The cause: spin-orbit coupling $\hat{H}_{LS} \propto \hat{L}\cdot\hat{S}$. The Lamb shift is a deeper effect — QED vacuum fluctuations → the decisive verification of QFT.

**The intuition of perturbation theory**: in $\hat{H} = \hat{H}_0 + \lambda\hat{V}$, $\hat{H}_0$ is exactly solvable and $\hat{V}$ is small. Expand in powers of $\lambda$. The first-order term is the average value, the second-order term is the influence of nearby states. **Eighty percent of physics calculations are perturbation theory** — because the exactly solvable problems number only three: the infinite well, the harmonic oscillator, and the hydrogen atom.

**Fermi's golden rule — the transition-rate formula**: when a weak external perturbation induces the $i \to f$ transition, the transition probability is proportional to time, with proportionality constant $|\langle f|V|i\rangle|^2 \cdot \rho(E_f)$. Spontaneous emission, beta-decay rates, optical-absorption cross sections, LED efficiency — all the same formula.

**Scattering = the skeleton of particle physics**: collide a beam with a target and look at the scattered angles and energies. The measured quantity is the differential cross section $d\sigma/d\Omega$. All the interactions between particles are encoded in this one quantity. LHC, CERN, KEK — the format of every accelerator experiment.

## Mathematical structure
**Angular-momentum algebra**:
$$[\hat{J}_i, \hat{J}_j] = i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}\hat{J}_k$$

Ladder operators: $\hat{J}_\pm = \hat{J}_x \pm i\hat{J}_y$, $[\hat{J}_z, \hat{J}_\pm] = \pm\hbar\hat{J}_\pm$

Eigenstates $|j, m\rangle$, $j = 0, 1/2, 1, 3/2, \ldots$, $m = -j, \ldots, +j$ ($2j+1$ values). **The existence of half-integer spin** comes from the true representations of SU(2).

**Pauli matrices** (spin 1/2):
$$\sigma_x = \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\ \sigma_y = \begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix},\ \sigma_z = \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}$$

$\hat{S}_i = \frac{\hbar}{2}\sigma_i$, $[\sigma_i, \sigma_j] = 2i\epsilon_{ijk}\sigma_k$, $\{\sigma_i, \sigma_j\} = 2\delta_{ij}\mathbb{I}$

**Rotation representation**:
$$\hat{U}(\vec{n}, \theta) = \exp\left(-\frac{i\theta}{\hbar}\vec{n}\cdot\hat{\vec{J}}\right)$$

Spin 1/2: $\hat{U} = \cos(\theta/2) - i\sin(\theta/2)\,\vec{n}\cdot\vec{\sigma}$. **−1 under a 2π rotation** (the hallmark of half-integer spin, a simple instance of the Berry phase).

**Clebsch-Gordan decomposition**:
$$|j_1\rangle \otimes |j_2\rangle = \bigoplus_{J = |j_1-j_2|}^{j_1+j_2} |J\rangle$$

**Many-body — fermion antisymmetrization** (the Slater determinant):
$$\Psi(x_1,\ldots,x_N) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{N!}}\det[\phi_i(x_j)]$$
With two identical $\phi$'s the determinant = 0 → Pauli exclusion.

**Time-independent perturbation theory** ($\hat{H} = \hat{H}_0 + \lambda\hat{V}$):
$$E_n = E_n^{(0)} + \lambda\langle n|\hat{V}|n\rangle + \lambda^2\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{|\langle m|\hat{V}|n\rangle|^2}{E_n^{(0)} - E_m^{(0)}} + \cdots$$

In the degenerate case, solved by diagonalizing within the subspace.

**Variational method** — a trial wave function $|\psi_{\text{trial}}\rangle$:
$$E_0 \leq \frac{\langle\psi_{\text{trial}}|\hat{H}|\psi_{\text{trial}}\rangle}{\langle\psi_{\text{trial}}|\psi_{\text{trial}}\rangle}$$

Vary the parameters → find the minimum. **The foundation of DFT, quantum chemistry, and materials science.**

**Fermi's golden rule** (first-order time-dependent perturbation):
$$\Gamma_{i\to f} = \frac{2\pi}{\hbar}|\langle f|\hat{V}|i\rangle|^2\rho(E_f)$$

A harmonic external field + energy conservation $E_f = E_i + \hbar\omega$.

**Scattering — the differential cross section**:
$$\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = |f(\theta, \phi)|^2$$

Two ways to compute the scattering amplitude:
- **Born approximation**: $f_B(\vec{q}) = -\frac{m}{2\pi\hbar^2}\int e^{-i\vec{q}\cdot\vec{r}}V(\vec{r})d^3r$ — a Fourier transform
- **Partial-wave decomposition**: $f(\theta) = \frac{1}{k}\sum_l (2l+1)e^{i\delta_l}\sin\delta_l\, P_l(\cos\theta)$ — encoded in the phase shifts $\delta_l$

**Optical theorem** (probability conservation, unitarity): $\sigma_{\text{tot}} = \frac{4\pi}{k}\text{Im}\,f(0)$

**WKB approximation** (the semiclassical limit $\hbar \to 0$):
$$\psi(x) \approx \frac{1}{\sqrt{p(x)}}\exp\left(\frac{i}{\hbar}\int p(x')dx'\right)$$
Tunneling probability $T \approx e^{-2\int|p|dx/\hbar}$ — alpha decay, STM, nuclear fusion.

## Why it matters
**Quantitative understanding of atoms and molecules**:
- Hydrogen fine structure, hyperfine structure, the Lamb shift
- Helium many-body (the variational method)
- Molecular bonding (LCAO, the molecular-orbital method) — Pauling 1939
- **The periodic table = the combination of Pauli exclusion + spin + angular-momentum quantum numbers**

**Magnetism and resonance**:
- Curie's law, Pauli paramagnetism (fermion statistics)
- NMR / MRI — nuclear-spin Larmor precession + RF pulses
- ESR/EPR — analysis of radicals and defects

**The fermion/boson dichotomy** — the root of all quantum statistics:
- BEC (1995 Cornell-Wieman, 2001 Nobel)
- The stability of white dwarfs and neutron stars (fermion pressure)
- Superconductivity and superfluidity (the bosonization of fermion pairs — Cooper pairs)

**Perturbation theory = the universal tool of physics**:
- QED perturbation series: 12-digit agreement of $g-2$
- Decay rates in condensed matter and particle physics
- Lippmann-Schwinger → the S-matrix → QFT scattering

**Variational method → quantum chemistry, materials science**:
- DFT (Hohenberg-Kohn 1964, Kohn-Sham 1965, 1998 Nobel)
- The standard tool of new materials, catalysis, and drug design

**Scattering theory = the format of particle physics**:
- The LSZ reduction formula → QFT scattering
- The discoveries of the Standard Model particles (W, Z, Higgs) were all scattering cross sections

**Bridges to the next stage**:
- Spin + SR → Dirac → QED → the Standard Model (the entrance to Quantum Field Theory)
- Many-body + statistics → BCS, the quantum Hall effect, topological matter (condensed matter)
- Qubit = spin 1/2 → quantum information and computing

## Historical context
**The discovery of spin (1922–1928)**:
- 1922 Stern-Gerlach — the two-point splitting of a silver-atom beam (1944 Nobel)
- 1925 Goudsmit-Uhlenbeck — 21- and 24-year-old graduate students' "electron self-rotation" hypothesis. Pauli scoffed at first but soon accepted it
- 1925 Pauli — the exclusion principle (1945 Nobel)
- 1927 Pauli — the non-relativistic spin-matrix formalization
- **1928 Dirac** — the Dirac equation. **Spin is automatically derived from SR+QM** — not a hypothesis but a necessity. The positron was also automatically predicted (1933 Nobel)

**The birth of perturbation theory and scattering (1925–1948)**:
- 1925 Schrödinger, Born, Heisenberg — stationary perturbation theory
- 1928 Gamow / Condon-Gurney — WKB alpha decay (the first application of quantum tunneling)
- 1933 Bethe-Salpeter — many-body variational methods
- 1948– Tomonaga-Schwinger-Feynman — renormalization of the QED divergences (1965 Nobel)
- 1950 Lippmann-Schwinger — the formalization of scattering theory. The S-matrix

**Many-body, magnetism, solids (1927–)**:
- 1927 Heitler-London — the hydrogen molecule (the beginning of quantum chemistry)
- 1928 Bloch — electrons in a crystal, band theory
- 1929 Slater — the many-body determinant
- 1939 Pauling — *Nature of the Chemical Bond* (1954 Nobel)
- 1957 BCS — superconductivity. Fermion pairs → boson condensation (1972 Nobel)
- 1964 Hohenberg-Kohn / 1965 Kohn-Sham — DFT (1998 Nobel)

**The rise of quantum information (1980–)**:
- 1980 Manin / 1982 Feynman — the idea of quantum computing
- 1984 BB84 — Bennett-Brassard quantum key distribution
- 1994 Shor — the quantum factoring algorithm (a threat to RSA)
- 1996 Grover — the search algorithm
- Qubit = spin 1/2 = the simplest rotatable quantum system

**The key insight**: nearly all of the QM II tools (spin, perturbation, scattering, variational methods) **appeared within 5–10 years right after QM I**. 1925–1935 was the second golden age of quantum mechanics. Without these tools, no quantitative result in atomic, molecular, solid-state, or particle physics would be possible.
