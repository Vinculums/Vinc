# Thermodynamics

## One-line essence
Energy is conserved but its usefulness decreases — the universe flows from order to disorder.

## Physical picture
Pour cold milk into hot coffee and it becomes lukewarm. Conversely, a lukewarm drink does not spontaneously become hot again on its own. This is the everyday face of the second law of thermodynamics.

The intuition behind the laws:
- **Zeroth law**: the concept of temperature is meaningful — if two bodies are at the same temperature, they are in equilibrium with each other
- **First law**: energy does not disappear — only its form changes (heat, work, chemical energy...)
- **Second law**: **disorder (entropy) always increases** — it creates the arrow of time
- **Third law**: absolute zero ($T=0$) cannot be reached — perfect order is impossible

The key picture: a broken cup does not reassemble on its own, and the scent of perfume, once it has spread through the room, does not go back into the bottle. The universe has an asymmetry that makes time flow in only one direction, and the identity of that asymmetry is entropy.

The statistical meaning of entropy: **the logarithm of the number of possible microstates**. The arrangements in which the shards of a broken cup are scattered are astronomically many, while the arrangement in which they are gathered is close to a single one. Nature simply goes toward the more probable state.

## Mathematical structure
**First law (conservation of energy)**:
$$dU = \delta Q - \delta W$$
- $U$: internal energy
- $\delta Q$: heat entering the system, $\delta W$: work done by the system

**Second law (increase of entropy)**:
$$dS \geq \frac{\delta Q}{T}, \quad \Delta S_{\text{universe}} \geq 0$$
- $S$: entropy, $T$: temperature
- equality for a reversible process, inequality for an irreversible process

**Boltzmann's formula (statistical definition)**:
$$S = k_B \ln \Omega$$
- $\Omega$: the number of microstates corresponding to a macrostate
- $k_B \approx 1.38 \times 10^{-23}$ J/K (the Boltzmann constant)
- the equation engraved on his tombstone — the bridge connecting statistical mechanics and thermodynamics

**Free energy (a practical tool)**:
$$F = U - TS \quad \text{(Helmholtz free energy: isothermal, constant-volume work)}$$
$$G = U - TS + pV = H - TS \quad \text{(Gibbs free energy: isothermal, isobaric)}$$
- at equilibrium $F$ or $G$ is minimized
- spontaneity of a chemical reaction: $\Delta G < 0$ ⟹ spontaneous

**Third law (Nernst's theorem)**:
$$\lim_{T \to 0} S = 0 \text{ (in the case of a perfect crystal)}$$

**Heat-engine efficiency (the Carnot limit)**:
$$\eta_{\text{Carnot}} = 1 - \frac{T_c}{T_h}$$
No heat engine can exceed this efficiency.

## Why it matters
Thermodynamics is **one of the most universal theories in physics**. It holds regardless of the identity of particles or the kind of interactions.

Deeper positioning:
- **The arrow of time**: the microscopic laws (Newton, quantum) are time-symmetric, but the macroscopic world is asymmetric. The sole source of that asymmetry is entropy
- **A connection to information theory**: Shannon entropy and Boltzmann entropy are the same mathematics — information = negative entropy
- **Black hole entropy**: $S_{BH} = \frac{A}{4G\hbar}$ — Hawking's discovery. The intersection of general relativity, quantum mechanics, and thermodynamics
- **A core of cosmology**: the increase of the universe's entropy = the cosmic clock from the Big Bang to the heat death
- **Life and evolution**: living things locally lower entropy but pour out more of it into their surroundings (a net increase)
- **The foundation of all engineering**: power plants, engines, refrigerators, semiconductors — it sets the limit of every energy conversion

Einstein: "Classical thermodynamics is the only universal physical theory of which I am convinced that it will never be overthrown."

## Historical context
**A product of the Industrial Revolution**:
- In the 18th–19th centuries, improving the efficiency of the steam engine was a pressing engineering problem
- Carnot (1824, *Réflexions sur la puissance motrice du feu*): discovered the efficiency limit of the ideal heat engine — "no engine can exceed it"
- Joule (1840s): experimental measurement of the equivalence of work and heat → establishing the first law
- Clausius (1850, 1865): introduced the term "entropy," formalized the second law
  - "The energy of the universe is constant. The entropy of the universe tends toward a maximum."

**Boltzmann's statistical interpretation (1870s)**:
- The startling insight that entropy is not merely a macroscopic variable but **the logarithm of the number of microstates**
- It met with strong opposition from the physicists of the day (Mach, Ostwald), and Boltzmann took his own life in 1906
- However, Einstein's 1905 paper on Brownian motion proved the legitimacy of statistical mechanics

**Twentieth-century expansion**:
- Shannon (1948): entropy of the same form appeared in information theory → information = a physical quantity
- Hawking (1974): black holes emit thermal radiation → the unification of gravity, the quantum, and thermodynamics
- Nonequilibrium thermodynamics (Prigogine) and the fluctuation theorems (Jarzynski, Crooks) keep it active into the 21st century
