# Maxwell Electromagnetism

## One-line essence
Electricity and magnetism create each other and ripple together, and that rippling is light itself.

## Physical picture
Shake a magnet near a coil and a current arises. Conversely, a coil carrying a current acts like a magnet. Electricity and magnetism seem to live apart, but in fact they are one body.

Maxwell's central insight is deeper still — **a changing electric field creates a magnetic field, and a changing magnetic field creates an electric field.** The two, forever giving birth to each other, spread out across space. This is the electromagnetic wave.

An intuitive picture: throw a stone into a lake and ripples spread. The water itself does not go far, but the rippling spreads. The electromagnetic wave is just the same — the electric field and the magnetic field pump each other and race through the vacuum at the speed of light. When that speed was calculated, it was exactly the speed of light. It was the moment it was first revealed that light is an electromagnetic wave.

Radio, Wi-Fi, X-rays, visible light, gamma rays — all are merely different frequencies of the same electromagnetic wave.

## Mathematical structure
**The four Maxwell equations (vacuum, SI units)**:

$$\nabla \cdot \vec{E} = \frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0} \quad \text{(Gauss's law: charge is the source of the electric field)}$$

$$\nabla \cdot \vec{B} = 0 \quad \text{(absence of magnetic monopoles: the magnetic field is always a closed loop)}$$

$$\nabla \times \vec{E} = -\frac{\partial \vec{B}}{\partial t} \quad \text{(Faraday's law: a changing magnetic field wraps around an electric field)}$$

$$\nabla \times \vec{B} = \mu_0 \vec{J} + \mu_0\epsilon_0 \frac{\partial \vec{E}}{\partial t} \quad \text{(Ampère-Maxwell law)}$$

**The key result — the wave equation**: in vacuum ($\rho=0, \vec{J}=0$), combining the above equations gives
$$\nabla^2 \vec{E} - \mu_0\epsilon_0 \frac{\partial^2 \vec{E}}{\partial t^2} = 0$$
$$c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \epsilon_0}} \approx 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$$

**The Lorentz force**: the force the electromagnetic field exerts on a charge.
$$\vec{F} = q(\vec{E} + \vec{v} \times \vec{B})$$

**The 4-tensor form (relativity-friendly)**:
$$\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \mu_0 J^\nu, \quad \partial_\mu \tilde{F}^{\mu\nu} = 0$$
- $F^{\mu\nu}$: the electromagnetic field tensor (unifying E and B into one object)
- the four equations are compressed into the two lines above → revealing that electromagnetism is essentially relativistic

## Why it matters
Maxwell's equations are **the most successful unified theory in the history of physics**.

- **The first great unification**: electricity + magnetism + optics → one theory. Faraday's experiments compressed into a single line
- **The midwife of relativity**: the requirement that Maxwell's equations have the same form in all inertial frames gave birth to special relativity
- **The womb of quantum field theory**: quantization of the electromagnetic field → the photon (QED) → the prototype of the Standard Model's gauge theory
- **The beginning of gauge theory**: the gauge freedom $A_\mu \to A_\mu + \partial_\mu \Lambda$ is the core structure of all modern particle physics
- **The foundation of all communication technology**: radio, TV, mobile phones, the internet, GPS — applications of the electromagnetic wave

The simplicity of the equations and the vast reach of their consequences gave physicists the belief that "Maxwell's equations are one of the equations closest to truth."

## Historical context
**Piecemeal discoveries (1820s–1860s)**:
- Ørsted (1820): a current moves a compass needle → electricity and magnetism connected
- Ampère (1820s): quantification of the magnetic force between currents
- Faraday (1831): electromagnetic induction — a changing magnetic field induces a current
- Gauss: the laws of electrostatics and magnetostatics

The problem: the four laws stood separately, and Ampère's law produced a contradiction (violation of charge conservation) in the presence of a time-varying electric field.

**Maxwell's unification (1865, *A Dynamical Theory of the Electromagnetic Field*)**:
- Added the **displacement current** term $\mu_0\epsilon_0 \partial_t \vec{E}$ to Ampère's law → resolving the contradiction
- Combining the four laws automatically derived the wave equation
- The speed of that wave coincided with the speed of light → the conclusion "light = electromagnetic wave"
- In 1888 Hertz experimentally confirmed radio waves → the whole world acknowledged it

**A bridge to relativity**: at the end of the 19th century, the fact that Maxwell's equations "must have the same form in whatever inertial frame they are viewed" collided with the Galilean transformation. This collision gave birth to Einstein's special relativity in 1905. Maxwell's equations were already relativistic, and in a sense relativity was needed to reveal that truth.
