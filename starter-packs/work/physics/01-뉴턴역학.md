# Newtonian Mechanics

## One-line essence
Force is the cause that changes momentum over time, and every motion is determined as the integral of this causal relation.

## Physical picture
Imagine a cup at rest on a table. It stays still. If someone pushes it with a finger, it slides. When the finger is removed, the cup tries to maintain its own motion until friction brings it to a stop. These three scenes are the entirety of Newton's three laws.

- **Inertia**: In the absence of external interference, the state (whether at rest or in uniform straight-line motion) is maintained. Motion is a "property of maintaining itself."
- **F=ma**: What changes that state is force. The heavier the cup, the less quickly it changes under the same force.
- **Action-reaction**: When the finger pushes the cup, the cup pushes the finger back with exactly the same magnitude. Forces always exist in pairs.

From these three pictures, everything from planetary orbits to car collisions is solved. Newton was the first to see that the falling of an apple and the revolution of the Moon obey the same principle.

## Mathematical structure
$$\vec{F} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt} = m\vec{a}$$

- $\vec{p} = m\vec{v}$ : momentum. The "amount of inertia," an essential conserved quantity.
- $\vec{F}$ : external force. The cause of state change.
- $m$ : inertial mass. The "degree of resistance to change."

**Conservation laws (consequences arising from symmetry)**:
$$\frac{d\vec{p}}{dt} = 0 \iff \vec{F}_{ext} = 0 \quad \text{(spatial translation symmetry → conservation of momentum)}$$
$$\frac{dE}{dt} = 0 \iff \partial_t V = 0 \quad \text{(time translation symmetry → conservation of energy)}$$
$$\frac{d\vec{L}}{dt} = \vec{\tau} \quad \text{(spatial rotation symmetry → conservation of angular momentum)}$$

Energy: $E = T + V = \frac{1}{2}m v^2 + V(\vec{r})$ — the sum of kinetic energy and potential energy.

## Why it matters
Newtonian mechanics is not merely a formula for the movement of objects. It is **the beginning of a deterministic view of the universe**. Laplace's demon — the idea that if you give only the initial conditions, the entire future can be solved — was born here.

Deeper positioning:
- **The starting point of all classical theories**: Lagrangian and Hamiltonian mechanics are Newton rewritten in a different language
- **A bridge to quantum mechanics**: the Ehrenfest theorem — quantum expectation values follow the classical equations of motion
- **Its limit against relativity**: valid only when $v \ll c$. It breaks down as $v \to c$
- **Extension into field theory**: generalizing from particle → field leads to electromagnetism and general relativity

Most important is that modern physics begins where Newtonian mechanics breaks down.

## Historical context
In the 17th century, Kepler organized the empirical laws that planetary orbits are ellipses, but did not know **why** they were so. Galileo discovered the laws of terrestrial motion (falling bodies, inertia), but could not connect them to the heavens.

Newton (1687, *Principia*) combined two things:
1. Kepler's celestial motion = Galileo's terrestrial motion (universal gravitation)
2. Solving motion as a function of time with the new mathematical tool of differentiation and integration

Background puzzles:
- "Why does the Moon not fall?" → in fact it is falling (free fall), only it also moves sideways so fast that it keeps missing the Earth
- "How is action at a distance possible?" → Newton himself was not satisfied with it ("Hypotheses non fingo")

Two hundred years later, Faraday and Maxwell's concept of the **field** filled this philosophical void, and a hundred years after that Einstein reinterpreted gravity as the curvature of spacetime.
