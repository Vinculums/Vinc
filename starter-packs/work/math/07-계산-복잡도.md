# Computability

## One-line essence
The *formal limits* proved by Gödel and Cohen were converted by Turing and Cook into *algorithmic limits*, and the very fact that these two limits mirror each other is the foundation of the integration of mathematics, CS, and AI.

## Intuitive picture

This field is *the bridge between mathematics and computer science* — a direct successor to the 1936 starting point where Gödel, Church, and Turing appeared in Foundations 1 §Act 3.

Three scenes:

- **Hilbert 1928 → Turing 1936**: Hilbert asked "*does there exist an algorithm that decides the truth of every mathematical statement?*" Turing defined the *Turing machine* and proved that the *Halting Problem* is undecidable — *No* to Hilbert's question. At the same time it was discovered that Church's $\lambda$-calculus and Gödel's recursive functions are all *equivalent* models of computation (the **Church-Turing thesis**).
- **Cook 1971 → P vs NP**: the question after computability is *efficiency*. Problems solvable in *polynomial time* (P) vs. problems *verifiable in polynomial time* (NP). Cook-Levin 1971/1973 proved that *SAT is NP-complete* — every problem in NP reduces to SAT. *Is P = NP?* is the most intuitive yet hardest open problem of the Millennium list.
- **Quantum computation**: Feynman 1982 proposed the quantum computer from the fact that quantum mechanics *cannot be simulated by a classical computer*. Shor 1994 gave a *polynomial-time factorization* algorithm on a quantum computer (RSA breaks). Google 2019 and Jiuzhang 2020 experimentally verified *quantum supremacy*. **Physics determines computation.**

The meta-insight of this field: **computability is the *physical realization* of formal limits — the Gödel-Cohen formal limits converted into algorithmic limits**. Church-Turing 1936 of Foundations of Mathematics §Act 3 is both the *starting point* and the meta-mechanism of this field.

## Formal structure

### Turing machine

**Turing machine** $(Q, \Sigma, \Gamma, \delta, q_0, F)$:
- $Q$ the set of states, $\Sigma$ the input alphabet, $\Gamma$ the tape alphabet
- $\delta : Q \times \Gamma \to Q \times \Gamma \times \{L, R\}$ the transition function
- $q_0$ the start state, $F$ the accepting states

**Church-Turing thesis**: *intuitively computable = computable by a Turing machine*.

→ Every reasonable model of computation (λ-calculus, recursive functions, Markov algorithms, μ-recursive, register machines) is *equivalent*. Not a mathematical theorem (a claim of equivalence with an intuitive definition).

### Halting Problem

**Theorem**: $H = \{(M, w) : \text{TM } M \text{ halts on } w\}$ is undecidable.

**Proof** (diagonalization):
1. Assume a TM $D$ deciding the Halting Problem
2. Define a new TM $E(M)$ = "if $D(M, M)$ is 'no' halt, if 'yes' loop forever"
3. Does $E(E)$ halt? — *contradiction*

→ The *same mechanism* as Gödel incompleteness (Gödel numbering + self-reference + diagonalization).

### Complexity classes

**P**: the class of problems solvable in polynomial time.

**NP**: the class of problems *verifiable* in polynomial time. (E.g., SAT — whether a variable assignment satisfies can be checked in polynomial time.)

**NP-complete**: the hardest problems in NP. Every NP problem reduces to them in polynomial time.

**Cook-Levin theorem** (1971, 1973):
$$\text{SAT} \in \text{NP-complete}$$

→ Every NP problem reduces to SAT. Solving SAT in polynomial time solves every NP problem in polynomial time = $P = NP$.

**Karp 1972**: 21 other problems are also NP-complete (3-SAT, CLIQUE, VERTEX COVER, HAMILTON, TSP, …).

### Hierarchy theorems, reductions, oracles

**Time hierarchy theorem** (Hartmanis-Stearns 1965): $\text{DTIME}(f(n)) \subsetneq \text{DTIME}(f(n)^2)$.

**Space hierarchy theorem**: space classes are also hierarchically separated.

**Polynomial hierarchy** (Stockmeyer 1976): $\Sigma_k^P, \Pi_k^P, \Delta_k^P$ — quantifier alternation.

**Oracle separation** (Baker-Gill-Solovay 1975): for an oracle $A$, $P^A = NP^A$; for an oracle $B$, $P^B \neq NP^B$. → P vs NP cannot be *relativized* (the relativization barrier).

### Probabilistic algorithms and complexity classes

**BPP**: polynomial-time probabilistic algorithms (error < 1/3).

**RP**: one-sided error (correct on yes instances with probability ≥ 1/2).

**ZPP**: zero error (Las Vegas algorithms).

**BQP**: quantum polynomial time (error < 1/3).

→ $P \subseteq ZPP \subseteq RP \subseteq BPP \subseteq P/poly$ + $BQP \supseteq P, BPP$.

### Quantum computation

**Qubit**: $|\psi\rangle = \alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle$, $|\alpha|^2 + |\beta|^2 = 1$.

**Quantum circuits**: unitary gates (Hadamard, CNOT, T gates are universal).

**Shor 1994**: factorization $\in BQP$ — a polynomial-time quantum algorithm. RSA cryptography breaks.

**Grover 1996**: unsorted search in $\sqrt{N}$ — a quadratic speedup.

**Quantum supremacy**: Google Sycamore 2019, USTC Jiuzhang 2020 — quantum experimental verification of a task impossible for classical computers.

### The PCP theorem and hardness of approximation

**PCP theorem** (Arora-Lund-Motwani-Sudan-Szegedy 1992 + Arora-Safra 1992):
$$NP = PCP(O(\log n), O(1))$$

→ An NP proof can be verified with arbitrarily small error *by checking only a constant number of bits*.

**Khot's Unique Games Conjecture (UGC)** (2002): some hardness of approximation (the exact ratios for MAX-CUT, VERTEX-COVER) is determined under the UGC.

**Reductions and hardness of approximation**: the *limits of approximation algorithms* for many NP-hard problems are precisely classified via PCP and UGC.

## Why it matters

- **The mathematics ↔ CS bridge**: this field is the *algorithmic dimension* of the *formal limits* of Foundations of Mathematics §Act 3. The same meta-mechanism.
- **The foundation of cryptography**: RSA, ECC, post-quantum (lattice, CSIDH), zero-knowledge, blockchain. If P = NP, all cryptography breaks.
- **AI/ML direct**: deep-learning convergence (PAC-learning, VC dimension, Rademacher complexity), tractable inference, quantum ML.
- **A PHYSICS bridge**: Feynman 1982 proposed the quantum computer from *quantum simulation*. The Shor and Grover quantum algorithms. Quantum supremacy verification. A direct bridge to Quantum Information and non-Abelian anyon.
- **Meta-recognition of computational limits**: the very question of *why* P vs NP, UGC, and circuit lower bounds are hard (the relativization, natural proofs, and algebrization barriers) is itself a meta-open-problem.
- **Lean and Coq formal proof**: HoTT, Lean 4, Coq, and Isabelle *verify mathematical proofs by computer* — a direct application of Foundations of Mathematics §Act 4 + Scholze's Liquid tensor experiment.

## Historical context — R-03 four acts (mathematics variant)

### Act 1 — Birth: Hilbert, Gödel, Church, Turing (1928–1936)

- **1928**: **Hilbert-Ackermann**, explicit statement of the **Entscheidungsproblem** — does an algorithm exist that decides the truth of every mathematical statement? (an exact match with Foundations of Mathematics §Act 2 — the starting point of this field)
- **1931**: **Gödel**, the *incompleteness theorems* — the proof of the limits of formal systems (Foundations of Mathematics §Act 3)
- **1934**: **Gödel, Herbrand, Kleene**, the definition of *recursive functions*
- **1935**: **Church**, the **λ-calculus** — a new model of computation
- **1936**: **Turing**, *On Computable Numbers* — the **Turing machine** + the **undecidability of the Halting Problem** + the **Church-Turing thesis** (the intuitive-equivalence claim)
- **1936**: **Church**, the proof of the **undecidability of the Entscheidungsproblem** — *No* to Hilbert's 1928 question
- **1936**: **Kleene**, the proof of the equivalence of the *μ-recursive function*

→ Within the 8 years 1928–1936: the start of this field + formal limits + the Church-Turing thesis. The 4 models (Turing, λ, recursive, μ-recursive) are all equivalent.

**Decisive turn**: Turing 1936 — the *abstract formal limit* (Gödel) *converted* into a *physical algorithmic limit* (the Turing machine, the Halting Problem) → the bridge of mathematics + CS formed.

### Act 2 — Formalization: computability classes, hierarchy theorems, NP (1936–1971)

- **1937**: **Turing**, the *universal Turing machine* — simulating other TMs (the prototype of the modern computer)
- **1944**: **von Neumann**, the *EDVAC report* — the stored-program computer (the prototype of the modern CPU)
- **1948**: **Shannon**, *information theory* — information = negative entropy
- **1956**: **Cobham**, the definition of the *P class* (published 1965) — *polynomial time = efficient*
- **1965**: **Hartmanis-Stearns**, the **Time hierarchy theorem** — more time lets you solve more
- **1965**: **Edmonds**, the *seed of the definition of NP* — nondeterministic polynomial
- **1971**: **Cook**, **SAT is NP-complete** (Cook 1971, Levin 1973 independently)
- **1972**: **Karp**, the *21 NP-complete problems* — all natural NP problems are NP-complete
- **1973**: **Levin**, *SAT NP-complete* (independently of Cook, not disclosed outside the Soviet Union in 1971 — recognized late)

→ Turing's definition → computability classes → the formalization of P, NP, NP-complete. Cook-Levin 1971 is the *starting point of complexity theory*.

**Decisive turn**: Cook-Levin 1971 + Karp 1972 — the *NP-complete class* was formally enrolled → the open problem P vs NP appeared as a candidate for *the hardest problem in mathematics*.

### Act 3 — Verification/extension: quantum computation, probabilistic algorithms, PCP (1973–2002)

- **1973**: **Adleman**, *primality-test undecidability* (a different dimension from Turing's general decision)
- **1975**: **Baker-Gill-Solovay**, the *oracle separation* — P vs NP faces the *relativization barrier*
- **1977**: **Solovay-Strassen**, *Miller-Rabin* — probabilistic primality tests
- **1977**: **Rivest-Shamir-Adleman**, **RSA cryptography** — based on the hardness of factorization
- **1979**: **Garey-Johnson**, *Computers and Intractability* — the standard NP-complete reference
- **1982**: **Feynman**, *Simulating Physics with Computers* — the proposal of the **quantum computer** (quantum mechanics is classically unsimulable)
- **1985**: **Deutsch**, the *quantum Turing machine* — the model of quantum computation
- **1985**: **Goldwasser-Micali-Rackoff**, the **zero-knowledge proof**
- **1992**: **Arora-Lund-Motwani-Sudan-Szegedy + Arora-Safra**, the **PCP theorem**
- **1994**: **Shor**, the **Shor algorithm** — quantum polynomial-time factorization (the future risk of RSA breaking)
- **1996**: **Grover**, the **Grover algorithm** — quantum search in $\sqrt{N}$
- **1997**: **Razborov-Rudich**, the *natural proofs barrier* — the meta of the hardness of circuit lower bounds (a second barrier for P vs NP)
- **2002**: **Khot**, the **Unique Games Conjecture** — a classification tool for hardness of approximation

→ The emergence of quantum computation → the formalization of probabilistic algorithms → the PCP theorem → the classification of hardness of approximation. The *why is it hard* of P vs NP (relativization, natural proofs) becomes meta-recognized.

**Decisive turn**: Shor 1994 + PCP 1992 — *quantum computation breaks RSA, and hardness of approximation is precisely classified* → both the *physical realization* and the *mathematical precision* of computational limits begin in earnest.

### Act 4 — Reinterpretation/application: quantum supremacy, circuit lower bounds, Lean formal proof (2010–present)

- **2002**: **Agrawal-Kayal-Saxena (AKS)**, *primality testing in P* — *deterministic* polynomial time (not probabilistic)
- **2010s**: **Aaronson**, *quantum complexity* — the refinement of the BQP and QMA classes
- **2014**: **Ryan Williams**, *NEXP $\not\subset$ ACC^0* — the first *nontrivial* circuit-lower-bound result (the first progress after 40 years of stagnation)
- **2019**: **Google Sycamore**, the first **quantum supremacy** experiment (random circuit sampling, 53 qubits, 200 seconds vs. an estimated 10,000 years)
- **2020**: **USTC Jiuzhang**, **photonic quantum supremacy** (Gaussian boson sampling)
- **2020**: the **Liquid tensor experiment** (Scholze + Lean) — the formal verification of a *complex mathematical proof* in Lean 4 (a direct successor to Foundations of Mathematics §Act 4 + Algebra §Act 4 perfectoid)
- **2021**: the **Razborov-Williams** follow-up — the algebrization barrier (a third barrier for P vs NP)
- **2023**: **AI for theorem proving** — DeepMind AlphaProof IMO silver medal (automated mathematical proof in earnest)
- **Present**: **post-quantum cryptography** (lattice, CSIDH, hash-based), **quantum fault-tolerance**, **TQC** (directly non-Abelian anyon)

→ Lean formal proof + AI proving + quantum-supremacy experiments proceed simultaneously. This field is the *integrating tool of the 4 fields mathematics + CS + AI + physics*.

**Decisive turn**: the Lean tensor experiment + quantum supremacy — the simultaneous verification that *mathematical proofs are computer-verified* and *the physical quantum computer exceeds the classical limit* → this field is the most future-facing dimension of mathematics.

### Core insight

The four acts of this field are the refinement of *formal limit = algorithmic limit*. Hilbert 1928 → Turing 1936 is the *abstract → physical* conversion. Cook-Levin 1971 formalized the *efficiency limit* as NP-complete. Shor 1994 + quantum supremacy verified that *physics determines computation*. Lean and AI proving complete the *mathematics ↔ computer* bridge in both directions.

The meta-mechanism of this field is the *direct successor* of Foundations of Mathematics §Act 3 — the Gödel formal limit ↔ the Turing algorithmic limit are *two expressions of the same meta-mechanism* (parallel to MM-06). Direct bridges to PHYSICS Quantum Information and non-Abelian anyon. **Computability is the physical realization of formal limits.**
