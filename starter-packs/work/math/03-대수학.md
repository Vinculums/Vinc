# Algebra

## One-line essence
The view that the essence lies not in *the objects themselves* but in *the structure of operations between objects* produced everything from the unsolvable quintic equation to the unifying language of every field.

## Intuitive picture

Algebra is *the discipline of operations*. The key is not numbers but *the structure of + and × between numbers*.

Three scenes:

- **The quintic equation — why it cannot be solved** (1832 Galois): the quadratic, cubic, and quartic have formulas for their roots. What about the quintic? Abel in 1824 proved that "there is generally no formula for the roots." But *why* not? — Galois in 1832 saw that the answer is *the symmetry group formed by the roots of the equation*. If the group is a *solvable group*, it can be solved; otherwise it cannot. Galois's answer, who died in a duel at 21, is *the starting point of algebra*.
- **Noether's abstraction** (1921): the *division algorithm* of the integers and the *division algorithm* of polynomials have the same structure. Both are *Euclidean rings*. Noether established *rings, ideals, fields, and modules* as an abstract formalism — algebra became the discipline of *all mathematical objects having operations*.
- **Category theory — the meta of algebra** (1945 Eilenberg-Mac Lane): groups, rings, fields, topological spaces, manifolds… all have the same form when viewed as *objects* and *the morphisms between them*. When categories are connected by *functors*, *a mathematical field itself becomes a morphism*. This is the starting point of algebraic geometry, homotopy-theoretic number theory, and ∞-categories.

The meta-insight of algebra: **the essence is not the objects but *the relations between objects***. This viewpoint is the bridge to the integration of all subsequent fields.

## Formal structure

### Group, Ring, Field

**Group** $(G, \cdot)$: the operation $\cdot$ satisfies associativity, an identity, and inverses.
- *Abelian group*: commutative $a \cdot b = b \cdot a$
- Examples: $(\mathbb{Z}, +)$, $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}, +)$, the symmetric group $S_n$, the general linear group $GL_n(\mathbb{R})$, the rotation group $SO(3)$

**Ring** $(R, +, \cdot)$: $(R, +)$ an Abelian group + $\cdot$ associative + distributivity.
- *Commutative ring*: $\cdot$ commutative
- *Integral domain*: $ab = 0 \Rightarrow a = 0$ or $b = 0$
- Examples: $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}[x]$, $M_n(\mathbb{R})$ (the matrix ring, noncommutative)

**Field** $(F, +, \cdot)$: every element except 0 has a multiplicative inverse. Examples: $\mathbb{Q}$, $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{C}$, $\mathbb{F}_p$ ($p$ prime).

### Galois theory (1832)

The *Galois group* of a field extension $K \subseteq L$: $\text{Gal}(L/K) = \{\sigma : L \to L \text{ automorphism}, \sigma|_K = \text{id}\}$.

**The Galois correspondence**:
$$\{\text{intermediate fields } K \subseteq M \subseteq L\} \xleftrightarrow{1:1} \{\text{subgroups } H \leq \text{Gal}(L/K)\}$$

$M = L^H$ (the fixed points of $H$), $H = \text{Gal}(L/M)$.

**The solvability-by-radicals theorem**:
$$f(x) \in K[x] \text{ solvable by radicals} \iff \text{Gal}(\text{splitting field}/K) \text{ a solvable group}$$

The general Galois group $S_5$ of the quintic is *not solvable* → there is no formula for the roots of the general quintic.

### Noether rings and ideals (1921)

**Ideal** $I \subseteq R$: $a, b \in I, r \in R \Rightarrow a + b \in I, ra \in I$.

**Noetherian ring**: every ideal is finitely generated. Example: $k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ (the Hilbert basis theorem).

**Unique factorization**:
- Integers: $\mathbb{Z}$ — $n = \pm p_1^{a_1} \cdots p_k^{a_k}$
- Polynomials: $k[x]$ — $f = c \prod (x - \alpha_i)^{m_i}$
- General: *UFD (Unique Factorization Domain)* — Noether's general definition

### Representation Theory

The *representation* of a group $G$: a homomorphism $\rho : G \to GL(V)$ — realizing an abstract group as *matrices*.

**Irreducible representation**: one whose only subrepresentations are trivial.

**Maschke's theorem** (1899): a representation over a finite group is a direct sum of irreducible representations.

**Schur's lemma**: a morphism between two irreducible representations is either 0 or an isomorphism.

**Peter-Weyl theorem**: every function on a compact group is approximated by a sum of matrix elements of irreducible representations.

→ PHYSICS: the representations of $SO(3)$ and $SU(2)$ are the direct starting point of the spin classification of Quantum Mechanics II.

### Introduction to Category Theory

**Category** $\mathcal{C}$: *objects* + *morphisms* + *composition* + *identity morphisms*.

**Functor** $F : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}$: objects → objects, morphisms → morphisms, preserving composition and identity.

**Natural transformation** $\eta : F \Rightarrow G$: a morphism between functors. $\eta_X : F(X) \to G(X)$ a naturally commuting diagram.

**Universal property**: defined by the property that a certain object *has a unique morphism from every other object satisfying a condition*. Examples: product, coproduct, free group, tensor product.

**Yoneda lemma**: every object $X$ is *completely determined* by the functor $\text{Hom}(-, X)$ — an object is determined by its own *relations*.

### Group representations ↔ physics

| Group | Physics |
|---|---|
| $SO(3)$ | 3-dimensional rotation — angular momentum |
| $SU(2)$ | the double cover of $SO(3)$ — spin 1/2 |
| Lorentz $SO(3,1)$ | spacetime — Wigner's particle classification |

## Why it matters

- **A unified language of mathematics**: every mathematical field is expressible in the form *objects + morphisms*. Topological spaces + continuous maps = $\mathbf{Top}$, manifolds + smooth maps = $\mathbf{Diff}$, sets + functions = $\mathbf{Set}$.
- **Direct application to PHYSICS**: group representation theory is the Standard Model of particle physics. Lie groups and Lie algebras are the foundation of gauge theory. The classification of $SU(N)$, $SO(N)$, $Sp(N)$, $E_8$ determines string theory and the Standard Model.
- **The foundation of algebraic geometry and number theory**: Grothendieck's schemes and étale cohomology begin from the *spectrum of a ring*. Wiles's FLT (1995) was solved via the *Galois representation* of an elliptic curve.
- **Quantum groups and algebraic topology**: Hopf algebras and Drinfeld's quantum groups are quantum invertibility + knot invariants. The Atiyah-Singer index theorem is based on K-theory.
- **Computability and cryptography**: the classification of finite groups (completed in the 1980s, the *classification of finite simple groups*), field extensions, discrete logarithms, elliptic-curve cryptography.
- **Meta-position**: this field is the foundation of the insight *algebra = the discipline of relations* — Foundations of Mathematics §Act 4 *the pluralization of foundations* is meta-integrated through the category theory of this field (the starting point of HoTT, ∞-topos, and motivic homotopy).

## Historical context — R-03 four acts (mathematics variant)

### Act 1 — Birth: Galois, Cayley, Dedekind (1830–1872)

- **1824**: **Abel**, *the proof of the absence of a formula for the roots of the quintic* (the precise theorem was published in 1826)
- **1832**: **Galois**, on the night before his duel, described *Galois theory* in a letter. Died at 21. Published by Liouville in 1843
- **1830s–50s**: **Cauchy, Cayley** began the formalism of groups (symmetric groups, permutation groups)
- **1854**: **Cayley**, *On the theory of groups* — the first definition of the abstract group axioms
- **1858**: **Möbius**, the *Möbius transformation* — the group action of projective geometry
- **1872**: **Klein**, the *Erlangen Programm* — *geometry = invariants under group actions* (unifying the classification of all geometries)
- **1872–1888**: **Dedekind**, introduction of the **ideal** — the foundation of algebraic number theory (the ring of integers of a field extension, the Dedekind domain)
- **1882**: **Kronecker**, the *formalism of field extensions*

→ Evolution from concrete instances (equations, geometry, integers) to abstract formalism (groups, rings, fields, ideals). Klein's Erlangen is the first meta-insight reducing geometry to *group theory*.

**Decisive turn**: Galois 1832 → Dedekind 1872 — *the symmetry of an equation* was abstracted into *the automorphisms of a field extension* → algebra set out as *the general theory of operational structures*.

### Act 2 — Formalization: Noether, Bourbaki, the emergence of category theory (1900–1945)

- **1893**: **Lie**, *Lie groups and Lie algebras* — continuous groups + infinitesimal transformations (the foundation of PHYSICS GR and gauge theory)
- **1899**: **Hilbert**, *Grundlagen der Geometrie* — axiomatic geometry (parallel to algebra)
- **1900**: **Hilbert**, the 23 problems — No. 5 *continuous groups = Lie groups*, No. 12 *the explicit form of field extensions*
- **1907**: **Killing-Cartan**, the *classification of simple Lie algebras* — $A_n, B_n, C_n, D_n + E_6, E_7, E_8, F_4, G_2$
- **1921**: **Noether**, *Idealtheorie in Ringbereichen* — the formalization of **ideal theory** and the *Noetherian ring*. She made the decisive leap of abstract algebra during a period when, just after her doctoral thesis, she was even being denied her Habilitation
- **1924**: **van der Waerden**, *Moderne Algebra* (compiling the Noether-Artin lectures, published 1930) — the first textbook of abstract algebra
- **1932–**: **Bourbaki** (a collective name, Cartan, Weil, Dieudonné, etc.), *Éléments de mathématique* — unifying algebra, analysis, and topology all in the form *set + structure*. The *Bourbaki style* became the standard of 20th-century mathematics
- **1945**: **Eilenberg-Mac Lane**, *General theory of natural equivalences* — the formalization of **categories, functors, natural transformations** (beginning in algebraic topology)

→ Noether's abstraction formalized algebra as *the discipline of operational structures*. Bourbaki unified all of mathematics as *set + structure*. Category theory appeared as the meta-formalism *above* it.

**Decisive turn**: Noether 1921 → Eilenberg-Mac Lane 1945 — *abstract algebra* was meta-integrated into *categories* → an era in which algebra is directly connected to other fields (topology, geometry, analysis) by *functors*.

### Act 3 — Verification/extension: Grothendieck, Atiyah, Wiles (1957–1995)

- **1955**: **Grothendieck**, the *Tôhoku paper* — general homological algebra (Abelian categories, sheaf cohomology)
- **1957–1962**: **Grothendieck**, *Éléments de géométrie algébrique* (EGA) — the introduction of the *scheme*, the refounding of algebraic geometry (Fields 1966)
- **1960–1973**: **Grothendieck**, *Séminaire de géométrie algébrique* (SGA) — **étale cohomology**, *topos*, motives — the answer to the Weil conjectures (Deligne, Fields 1974)
- **1963**: **Atiyah-Singer**, the **Index Theorem** — the index of a differential operator = a K-theory topological invariant
- **1967**: **Langlands**, the *automorphic representation* letter — the isomorphism conjecture *Galois representation ↔ automorphic form* (the **Langlands program**)
- **1980s**: **Atiyah-Bott, Witten**, *gauge theory* — topological field theory and Donaldson's 4-manifolds
- **1995**: **Wiles**, the **proof of Fermat's Last Theorem** — a partial proof of the Taniyama-Shimura conjecture (elliptic curve = modular form), with FLT as a corollary. Galois representations were the key tool (Wiles announced in 1993, completed the proof in 1995, Fields *honorific* 1998 — over the age-40 limit)

→ Grothendieck 1957–1973 integrated algebraic geometry, number theory, and category theory. Langlands 1967 was a *vision of the unification of mathematics*. Atiyah-Singer 1963 was the bridge of algebra, geometry, analysis, and topology.

**Decisive turn**: Grothendieck 1957 + Wiles 1995 — the *abstract category (scheme, étale) was verified as a tool that solves a 350-year-old open problem (FLT)* → demonstration of the *extreme effect* of abstract algebra.

### Act 4 — Reinterpretation/application: ∞-categories, motivic, HoTT, ∞-topos (1990–present)

- **1990s–**: **Voevodsky**, *motivic homotopy theory* — the union of algebraic geometry + topology (Fields 2002)
- **2000s**: **Lurie**, *Higher Topos Theory* (the 2009 book) — the formalization of **∞-topos** and ∞-category theory (HTT, 925 pp.)
- **2010s**: **Voevodsky**, *Univalent Foundations* (HoTT) — homotopy type theory appeared as an *alternative foundation of mathematics* (directly linked to Foundations of Mathematics §Act 4)
- **2010s–**: **Scholze**, the **perfectoid space** — Galois representations, *p-adic Hodge*, *condensed mathematics* (Fields 2018, at 30)
- **2020s**: the **Liquid tensor experiment** (Scholze) — the refinement of *condensed mathematics* via Lean 4 formal proof
- **Present**: the **Langlands program** — local, global, and geometric Langlands are all actively in progress (Fargues-Scholze 2021 local Langlands geometric)
- **Present**: **Connes spectral triple → Standard Model** (Connes-Chamseddine, the noncommutative geometry of the standard model)

→ This field matured into a tool that integrates every mathematical field + *physics*. ∞-categories and HoTT play two roles: *an alternative foundation + a unifying language of mathematics*.

**Decisive turn**: Lurie's ∞-topos + Voevodsky's HoTT — an era in which *algebra becomes the foundation* → the appearance of a foundation candidate *on par with* set-theoretic ZFC. Langlands and Scholze are advancing *the unification of mathematics in the p-adic dimension*.

### Core insight

The four acts of algebra are the evolution *from objects to relations*. Galois saw *the symmetry of an equation*, Noether abstracted *operational structures*, Eilenberg-Mac Lane meta-ized them into *categories*, and Lurie and Voevodsky made *∞-categories and HoTT* into the foundation itself. **An object is determined by its relations** (the Yoneda lemma).

This meta-insight is **complementary** to Analysis's *abstraction = expansion of application*: analysis is *abstract the limit → expand the application*, algebra is *abstract the relation → integrate the fields*. Together the two fields form the two meta-axes of mathematics (tool + language). The gauge groups of PHYSICS Quantum Field Theory and the Type III classification of Operator algebra in Quantum Gravity — Type III and modular flow are direct applications of this field.

## Self-identified open problems (U-14)

Self-identified by this note:

1. **A complete proof of the Langlands program** — local, global, and geometric Langlands all have only partial answers. The *physical* meaning of the *automorphic-Galois isomorphism* is open.
2. **A *simplified* proof of the classification of finite simple groups** — the proof completed in the 1980s is 10,000+ pages — a *second-generation proof project* (Aschbacher et al.) is in progress but unfinished.
3. **The equivalence of HoTT vs. ZFC** — a decade+ after Voevodsky/UF 2013 — a precise mapping of *which theorems are proved differently in the two foundations* is unfinished.
4. **The Hodge conjecture** — the conjecture algebraic cycle = Hodge class, a Millennium Problem, open.
5. **The BSD conjecture** — the *order of the zero of the L-function of an elliptic curve = the rank of the group* — a Millennium Problem, open, with Wiles a partial answer.
6. **The foundational status of ∞-categories** — whether Lurie's and Joyal's definition of an ∞-category *is defined over set theory or is a foundation in itself* is a meta-open-problem.
7. **The *geometric* nature of representation theory** — geometric Langlands is a partial answer to the *topological* meaning of representation theory — its *complete generalization* is unfinished.
8. **The *uniqueness* of the Connes spectral-triple Standard Model** — *why our universe has exactly the Connes spectral triple* is a meta-open-problem.
