# Geometry

## One-line essence
*The shape of a space* is completely captured by the *algebraic invariants* (homology, cohomology, index) defined on it, and the meeting of this algebra with differentiation unifies even quantum field theory, gravity, and computation.

## Intuitive picture

Geometry-topology is the discipline of *space*. But in two different dimensions:

- **Geometry**: *distance, angle, curvature* — quantitative shape (Riemann manifolds, differential geometry)
- **Topology**: *connectedness, holes, dimension* — qualitative shape (homotopy, homology theory)

Three scenes:

- **Riemann 1854 — generalizing curvature**: in a world where only Euclidean space existed, Riemann saw that "*distance can be defined in arbitrary dimensions*." A differentiable manifold + a Riemann metric + a curvature tensor. Sixty years later Einstein used this as *spacetime itself* (PHYSICS General Relativity).
- **Poincaré 1894 — the discipline of counting holes**: how to distinguish whether a surface is a torus or a sphere? *Connectedness and the number of holes*. Poincaré defined the *fundamental group* $\pi_1$ and *homology* $H_n$ — a new discipline that *classifies topology by algebraic invariants*. A hundred years later Perelman solved the Poincaré conjecture (2003).
- **Atiyah-Singer 1963 — the bridge between differentiation ↔ topology**: the dimension of the solutions (index) of an *elliptic differential operator* on a manifold = a *pure topological invariant*. Differentiation and topology meet exactly in the *index theorem* — quantum field theory anomalies, Yang-Mills moduli, Donaldson theory, and even string theory all flow from this theorem.

The meta-insight of this field: **the *shape* of a space is completely determined by the *algebra* on it**. This is the starting point of algebraic topology, algebraic geometry, motivic, and ∞-topos.

## Formal structure

### Riemann manifolds and curvature

**Manifold** $M$: locally homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^n$. Coordinate transitions are smooth.

**Riemann metric** $g_{ij}$: defines an *inner product* at each point (the $g_{\mu\nu}$ of PHYSICS GR).

**Levi-Civita connection** $\Gamma^k_{ij}$: the consistency of parallel transport and differentiation.

**Riemann curvature tensor**:
$$R^l_{ijk} = \partial_i \Gamma^l_{jk} - \partial_j \Gamma^l_{ik} + \Gamma^l_{im}\Gamma^m_{jk} - \Gamma^l_{jm}\Gamma^m_{ik}$$

Contractions: the **Ricci tensor** $R_{ij} = R^k_{ikj}$, the **scalar curvature** $R = g^{ij} R_{ij}$.

Direct PHYSICS application: the Einstein equation $R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2}R g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}$ (General Relativity).

### Homology and cohomology

**$n$-chain** $C_n$: a formal sum of $n$-dimensional simplices (points, lines, triangles, tetrahedra…).

**Boundary map** $\partial_n : C_n \to C_{n-1}$, $\partial_{n-1} \circ \partial_n = 0$ (boundary of boundary = 0).

**Homology group**:
$$H_n(X) = \ker(\partial_n) / \text{im}(\partial_{n+1})$$

Intuition: $H_n$ = *the number of $n$-dimensional holes*.

Examples:
- $H_0(S^1) = \mathbb{Z}$ (1 connected component)
- $H_1(S^1) = \mathbb{Z}$ (1 one-dimensional hole — the circumference of the circle)
- $H_2(S^2) = \mathbb{Z}$ (1 two-dimensional hole — the inside of the sphere)

**de Rham cohomology**: on differential forms $\Omega^n$, $d : \Omega^n \to \Omega^{n+1}$, $d^2 = 0$.
$$H^n_{dR}(M) = \ker(d) / \text{im}(d) = \frac{\text{closed forms}}{\text{exact forms}}$$

**de Rham theorem** (1931): $H^n_{dR}(M) \cong H^n(M; \mathbb{R})$ — differential-form cohomology = topological cohomology (real coefficients).

### Homotopy groups

**Fundamental group** $\pi_1(X, x_0)$: the homotopy equivalence classes of closed paths with start and end point $x_0$, a group under composition.

**Higher homotopy groups** $\pi_n(X)$: the homotopy equivalence classes of $S^n \to X$.

Examples: $\pi_n(S^n) = \mathbb{Z}$ (the Brouwer degree). $\pi_3(S^2) = \mathbb{Z}$ (the Hopf fibration — a key of string theory and gauge theory).

### Atiyah-Singer index theorem (1963)

For an *elliptic differential operator* $D : \Gamma(E) \to \Gamma(F)$ on a compact manifold $M$:
$$\text{ind}(D) = \dim \ker(D) - \dim \text{coker}(D) = \int_M \hat{A}(M) \cdot \text{ch}(\sigma(D))$$

Left side: the analytic index (the dimension of the PDE solutions — analysis).
Right side: the topological index (the integral of characteristic classes — topology).

**An exact bridge, analysis ↔ topology.**

Applications:
- **Riemann-Roch** (Hirzebruch's generalization 1954)
- **Gauss-Bonnet** (Chern-Gauss-Bonnet)
- **Hirzebruch signature**
- **Yang-Mills anomaly** (Adler-Bell-Jackiw → the Atiyah-Singer generalization)
- **The quantum Hall effect** (Thouless, the TKNN formula → a direct successor to 30 topological qubits)

### Characteristic Classes

**Chern class** $c_i(E)$: a topological invariant of a complex vector bundle. Example: $c_1(L) \in H^2(M; \mathbb{Z})$ — the quantization of magnetic flux.

**Pontryagin class** $p_i(E)$: real vector bundles.

**Stiefel-Whitney class** $w_i(E)$: $\mathbb{F}_2$ coefficients — orientability, spin structure.

**Euler class** $e(E)$: orientable bundles.

PHYSICS: the Chern-Simons action $\int c_2 = \int F \wedge F$ is a key of topological field theory (non-Abelian anyon).

## Why it matters

- **A direct foundation of GR**: Riemann manifold + curvature tensor = the Einstein equation. *Gravity = spacetime curvature*.
- **Gauge theory**: principal bundle + connection = gauge field. The $F = dA + A \wedge A$ of Yang-Mills is curvature.
- **Quantum field theory anomalies**: the chiral anomaly = the Atiyah-Singer index theorem. The exact prediction of the experimental $\pi^0 \to \gamma\gamma$.
- **Topological condensed matter**: the TKNN formula = the Chern number. The quantum Hall effect, topological insulators, the Majorana fermion (non-Abelian anyon).
- **String theory**: Calabi-Yau manifolds, moduli spaces, mirror symmetry — all direct applications of this field (String Theory).
- **Mathematical integration**: algebraic geometry (Grothendieck) + differential geometry + topology = motivic homotopy. This field is the integration bridge with Algebra §Act 4.
- **Modern 4-manifolds**: Donaldson 1983 and Seiberg-Witten 1994 discovered that *4 dimensions differ from all other dimensions*. Infinitely many exotic $\mathbb{R}^4$ (Freedman, Donaldson, Taubes).
- **Computational applications**: persistent homology (TDA, topological data analysis) applies the tools of this field to ML, biology, and neuroscience.

## Historical context — R-03 four acts (mathematics variant)

### Act 1 — Birth: Lobachevsky, Riemann, Poincaré (1830–1900)

- **1829–1832**: **Lobachevsky, Bolyai**, the independent discovery of *non-Euclidean geometry* (hyperbolic geometry) — the parallel postulate can be denied
- **1854**: **Riemann**, *Über die Hypothesen welche der Geometrie zu Grunde liegen* (inaugural lecture) — *arbitrary-dimensional manifolds* + *metric* + *curvature* (a 60-year advance input to PHYSICS GR)
- **1872**: **Klein**, the *Erlangen Programm* — geometry = invariants under group actions (the exact same citation as Algebra §Act 1)
- **1888–1890**: **Killing-Cartan**, the classification of Lie algebras — the simple classification of continuous groups (5 exceptions $E_6, E_7, E_8, F_4, G_2$)
- **1894**: **Poincaré**, *Analysis Situs* — the first use of the name **topology**. The definition of the *fundamental group $\pi_1$* and *homology*
- **1895–1904**: Poincaré, the *5 supplementary papers* — the formalization of topology
- **1900**: **Hilbert**, the 23 problems — No. 5 *continuous groups = Lie groups*

→ The flow non-Euclidean geometry → Riemann manifolds → Klein group actions → Poincaré topology. Geometry and topology begin *separate* → *integrated*.

**Decisive turn**: Riemann 1854 + Poincaré 1894 — the recognition that *a space can have arbitrary shape, and that shape is classified by algebraic invariants* → the beginning of the integrated discipline of geometry-topology.

### Act 2 — Formalization: Cartan, de Rham, Hodge, Eilenberg-Steenrod (1900–1952)

- **1899–1908**: **Élie Cartan**, the *exterior differential form* — the general theory of differential forms
- **1915**: **Einstein**, GR — the first *physical* application of Riemann manifolds (General Relativity)
- **1925–30s**: **Schouten, Levi-Civita**, *connection theory*
- **1931**: **de Rham**, the **de Rham theorem** — differential-form cohomology = topological cohomology (real coefficients). The first exact bridge between differentiation and topology
- **1934**: **Cartan-Eilenberg**, *Homological algebra* — the abstraction of homology
- **1941**: **Hodge**, **Hodge theory** — Riemann manifold + metric = *harmonic forms* represent cohomology (the Hodge decomposition on Kähler manifolds)
- **1945**: **Eilenberg-Mac Lane**, *General theory of natural equivalences* — category theory (Algebra §Act 2)
- **1945**: **Eilenberg-Steenrod**, *axiomatic homology* — the axiomatic definition of homology (homotopy invariance, exactness, excision, dimension)
- **1948**: **Whitney**, the *embedding theorem* — every $n$-manifold embeds in $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$
- **1952**: **Thom**, *cobordism theory* — the classification of manifolds, Fields 1958
- **1953**: **Serre**, *spectral sequences and sheaf cohomology* — Fields 1954, at 27 (the youngest Fields ever)

→ Differential forms + homology + cohomology are integrated. de Rham + Hodge is the first bridge *analysis ↔ topology*. Eilenberg-Steenrod made topology an axiomatic discipline.

**Decisive turn**: de Rham 1931 + Eilenberg-Steenrod 1945 — *differential forms carry topological information* + *topology is an axiomatic discipline* → geometry-topology can be precisely treated with algebraic tools (algebraic topology).

### Act 3 — Verification/extension: Atiyah-Singer, Milnor, Smale, Donaldson (1956–1995)

- **1956**: **Milnor**, **exotic $S^7$** — the existence of 28 *nonstandard* differentiable structures on the 7-sphere (Fields 1962). The first instance of manifolds *topologically the same but differentiably different*
- **1961**: **Smale**, the proof of the *generalized Poincaré conjecture* $n \geq 5$ — h-cobordism (Fields 1966)
- **1962**: **Atiyah-Hirzebruch**, **K-theory** — the classification of vector bundles
- **1963**: **Atiyah-Singer**, the **index theorem** — the exact bridge analysis ↔ topology (Atiyah, Fields 1966)
- **1976**: **Yau**, the *proof of the Calabi conjecture* — the existence of Calabi-Yau manifolds (Fields 1982, a direct string-theory application)
- **1976**: **Thurston**, the *geometrization conjecture* — the classification of 3-manifolds (Fields 1982)
- **1983**: **Donaldson**, *Donaldson polynomials* — the gauge theory of 4-manifolds (Fields 1986)
- **1980s**: **Witten**, *topological field theory* (TQFT) — Atiyah's axioms 1988. Chern-Simons, the Donaldson invariant, and Seiberg-Witten are all this field (Fields 1990 — the first physicist)
- **1994**: **Seiberg-Witten**, the *monopole equations* — a simplification of Donaldson theory
- **1995**: **Wiles**, FLT (cited in Algebra §Act 3 — an integrated application of algebraic geometry + this field)

→ The index theorem is the *ultimate tool* of this field. The discovery of the singularity of 4-manifolds (Donaldson, exotic $\mathbb{R}^4$). An era in which physics (Witten gauge, TQFT) becomes a tool of this field.

**Decisive turn**: Atiyah-Singer 1963 + Witten 1980s — *analysis, topology, and physics are unified in a single theorem* → this field evolved into an integrating tool for quantum field theory, string theory, and condensed matter.

### Act 4 — Reinterpretation/application: Perelman, motivic, ∞-topos, TDA (1995–present)

- **1995**: **Floer homology**, infinite-dimensional Morse theory — symplectic manifolds
- **2002–2003**: **Perelman**, the **proof of the Poincaré conjecture** — the completion of the classification of 3-manifolds (Ricci flow + entropy). Declined the Fields 2006, declined the Clay Millennium $1M — an emblem of the *pure* pursuit of mathematical truth
- **2002**: **Voevodsky**, *motivic homotopy theory* (Fields 2002) — the integration of algebraic geometry + topology (cited in Algebra §Act 4)
- **2009**: **Lurie**, *Higher Topos Theory* (HTT, 925 pp.) — the formalization of **∞-topos**. All homotopy theory is integrated into ∞-categories
- **2010s**: **persistent homology** — TDA measures *the topology of data* (an explosion of applications in biology, ML, neuroscience)
- **2010s**: **mirror symmetry** (Strominger-Yau-Zaslow, Kontsevich homological MS) — the discovery of a *symmetry* between Calabi-Yau manifolds in string theory
- **Present**: **Geometric Langlands** (Drinfeld, Beilinson, Gaitsgory, Fargues-Scholze) — the *topological-geometric* dimension of the Langlands program
- **Present**: **Witten, string theory**, *quantum gravity ↔ this field* (String Theory, Swampland)

→ At the same time as Perelman solved a 100-year-old open problem, Voevodsky and Lurie evolved this field into a *foundation candidate for mathematics*. TDA brought the tools of this field to *data analysis*.

**Decisive turn**: Perelman 2003 + Lurie's ∞-topos — *the resolution of a classical topological conjecture* + *the foundationalization of ∞-categories* simultaneously → this field reached three dimensions at once: *classical open problems + a foundation of mathematics + an applied tool*.

### Core insight

The four acts of geometry-topology are the refinement of the recognition that *a space is both shape and algebra*. Riemann and Poincaré *defined space*, de Rham, Hodge, and Eilenberg-Steenrod *classified it by algebraic invariants*, Atiyah-Singer made *the exact bridge of differentiation and topology*, and Perelman and Lurie achieved *resolving classical open problems + foundationalization*.

This field is the place where the two meta-axes of Analysis (tool) + Algebra (relation) meet in the concrete object *space*. PHYSICS GR (General Relativity), QFT gauge (Quantum Field Theory), string theory (String Theory), and topological condensed matter (non-Abelian anyon) are all direct applications of this field. **The *shape* of a space is determined by *algebra*, and that *algebra* returns to *physics*.**
