# Foundations of Mathematics

## One-line essence
Every mathematical object is reducible to sets, yet this reduction itself formally proves its own self-limitation — namely, *what is decidable*.

## Intuitive picture

The foundational problem of mathematics is a single question — **what is mathematics? Where does it begin?**

Until the late 19th century mathematicians worked intuitively. "Set," "infinity," and "proof" seemed to have clear meaning. Then *infinity* broke.

The essence is captured in three scenes:

- **Cantor's discovery of infinity** (1874): the count of the natural numbers ℕ and the count of the real numbers ℝ are *not equal*. Both are infinite, but ℝ is *larger*. Even infinity has grades — countable infinity ℵ₀ < uncountable infinity 2^ℵ₀. This was the first event to shake mathematical intuition.
- **Russell's paradox** (1901): "the set of all sets that do not contain themselves as an element" R = {x : x ∉ x}. But is R ∈ R? — If yes, then R ∉ R; if no, then R ∈ R. Contradiction. The intuitive definition of a set *produces a contradiction*. Mathematics must prove its own foundation.
- **Gödel's response** (1931): if a formal system contains arithmetic, then *there must exist within it a statement that is true but unprovable*. The very attempt to fully formalize the foundation was proven *impossible within the formalism*.

These three scenes form the four-act flow of this note — Cantor opened the foundational problem by *defining infinity*, Russell, Frege, and Hilbert responded with *formalization*, Gödel proved *the limits of the formalism*, and ZFC + Cohen forcing created *a way of accepting undecidability*.

Mathematics is not "the discipline that can decide everything" but "the discipline that can self-prove *how far decidability reaches*."

## Formal structure

### The definition of set theory (Cantor)

The *cardinality* $|A|$ of a set $A$:
$$|A| = |B| \iff \exists\, f : A \to B \text{ bijective}$$

The separation of countable and uncountable infinity — Cantor's diagonal argument (1891):
$$|\mathbb{N}| = \aleph_0 < 2^{\aleph_0} = |\mathbb{R}|$$

**Core of the proof**: if there were a surjection $f : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}$, one could construct a new real $r$ by changing the $n$-th digit of $f(n)$ — but $r$ differs from every $f(n)$, a contradiction. ∴ $\mathbb{R}$ is not countable.

The continuum hypothesis (CH): is there *another infinity* between $\aleph_0$ and $2^{\aleph_0}$?
$$\text{CH} : 2^{\aleph_0} = \aleph_1$$

→ Problem 1 of Hilbert's 23 problems of 1900. As discussed below, it turned out to be *undecidable*.

### Russell's paradox and the ZFC axiomatization (Zermelo, Fraenkel)

The naive comprehension of sets ${x : P(x)}$ produces a contradiction → the *axiom of restricted comprehension* (Aussonderung):
$$\forall A\, \exists B\, \forall x\, (x \in B \iff x \in A \land P(x))$$

The 9 ZFC axioms (Extensionality, Pairing, Union, Power Set, Infinity, Replacement, Foundation, Choice, Aussonderung):

**Axiom of Choice (AC)**: from an infinite collection of nonempty sets, one can select one element from each.
$$\forall \mathcal{F}\, \left(\emptyset \notin \mathcal{F} \implies \exists f : \mathcal{F} \to \bigcup \mathcal{F},\, \forall A \in \mathcal{F}\, f(A) \in A\right)$$

→ Equivalent forms of AC: Zorn's lemma, the well-ordering principle, Tychonoff's theorem.

### Gödel's incompleteness theorems (1931)

**First incompleteness**: any consistent, *recursively enumerable* formal system $T$ that contains arithmetic has a statement $G_T$ that can be neither proved nor refuted within $T$.

**Second incompleteness**: such a $T$ cannot prove its own consistency $\text{Con}(T)$ *within itself*.

**Core of the proof (Gödel numbering + diagonalization)**:
1. Code every formula and proof of the formal system $T$ as a natural number ⟦φ⟧ — the *Gödel number*
2. Construct a self-referential statement $G$: $G \iff \neg \text{Prov}_T(\ulcorner G \urcorner)$
3. If $T \vdash G$, then $G$ is false ⊥, ∴ $T$ is contradictory
4. If $T \vdash \neg G$, then $G$ is true → $T \vdash G$, again a contradiction, ∴ $T \nvdash \neg G$
5. ∴ if $T$ is consistent, $G$ is true but $T$ cannot prove $G$.

### Cohen forcing (1963) — the independence of CH

**Theorem** (Cohen 1963): if ZFC is consistent, then ZFC + ¬CH is also consistent. In 1940 Gödel had proved the consistency of ZFC + CH via the *constructible universe* $L$. Combining the two results → **CH is independent of ZFC**.

**Core of forcing**:
- In a model $M \vDash \text{ZFC}$, add a *generic filter* $G$ → the extension $M[G] \vDash \text{ZFC}$
- $G$ is a filter meeting every *dense set* of the partial order $P$
- With an appropriate forcing notion, add $\aleph_2$ new reals → $2^{\aleph_0} \geq \aleph_2$ → ¬CH
- Thereby CH is *undecidable* in ZFC — a *concrete natural instance* of Gödel's 1931 *undecidable* statement.

## Why it matters

- **A unified foundation for mathematics**: every mathematical object (numbers, functions, spaces, structures) is reducible to ZFC sets. Every mathematical theorem is provable over ZFC. Mathematics is the child of ZFC.
- **Self-proof of formal limits**: the Gödel and Cohen results are the event of mathematics *formally proving its own limits*. No other discipline proves the limits of its own foundation this way — the summit of mathematics' meta-level self-awareness.
- **Direct connection to computability theory**: Church-Turing undecidability (1936) = the *computational-dimension expression* of Gödel incompleteness. The undecidability of the Halting Problem is the same mechanism.
- **The direction of modern mathematics**: simultaneous recognition of ZFC's *sufficiency* and *insufficiency*. The starting point for exploring *alternative foundations* — CH, AC, large-cardinal axioms (measurable, supercompact), HoTT (Univalent Foundations), and so on.
- **The essence that differs from PHYSICS and other disciplines**: no dependence on external experiment — verification is by *the self-consistency of abstract formal evolution*. Yet *undecidability* is self-proven as an intrinsic limit — this is the P3 bridging limit of learning mathematics.

## Historical context — R-03 four acts (mathematics variant)

### Act 1 — Birth: the emergence of Cantor's set theory (1874–1900)

The foundational problem of mathematics began with *infinity*.

- **1874**: **Cantor**, *Über eine Eigenschaft des Inbegriffs aller reellen algebraischen Zahlen* — the first proof of $|\mathbb{R}| > |\mathbb{N}|$
- **1878**: Cantor, dimension-independence — $|\mathbb{R}| = |\mathbb{R}^2|$ ("I see it, but I do not believe it")
- **1883**: Cantor, *Grundlagen* — the definition of *ordinals*, transfinite arithmetic
- **1891**: Cantor, the *diagonal argument* — $|A| < |2^A|$ (Cantor's theorem)
- **1895–1897**: Cantor, *Beiträge* — establishment of the cardinal/ordinal system, introduction of the alephs $\aleph_\alpha$
- **1899**: Cantor, self-discovery of the *set of all sets* paradox (in a letter to Dedekind)
- **1900**: **Hilbert**, the 23 problems at the Paris International Congress of Mathematicians — No. 1 CH, No. 2 the consistency of arithmetic, No. 10 the decidability of Diophantine equations

→ As Cantor stratified *infinity*, the limits of the intuitive notion of a set were exposed. CH, the continuum hypothesis, was unresolved from the moment it appeared.

**Decisive turn**: Cantor's self-recognition (1899) of ${x : \text{all sets for which } x \in x \text{ can be denied}}$ — *the definition of a set itself produces a paradox* → the starting point of the demand to formalize the foundation of mathematics.

### Act 2 — Formalization: the three great schools of logicism, formalism, and intuitionism (1879–1928)

Three formalization attempts that answered the foundational problem.

- **1879**: **Frege**, *Begriffsschrift* — the first proper formal system of *predicate logic*, first-order logic + natural deduction
- **1884–1903**: Frege, *Grundlagen / Grundgesetze* — *logicism*: arithmetic is derivable from pure logic (number = the equivalence class of equinumerous sets)
- **1901**: **Russell**, the paradox letter to Frege — *the contradiction of Frege's system itself is proven*
- **1908**: **Zermelo**, *Investigations in the foundations of set theory* — the 7 Z axioms + explicit AC (the separation axiom = Aussonderung)
- **1910–1913**: **Whitehead-Russell**, *Principia Mathematica* — avoids the paradox with *type theory*, formal derivation of arithmetic (the proof of 1+1=2 dozens of pages in)
- **1922**: **Fraenkel, Skolem**, refinement of ZF — addition of the *Replacement axiom* → completion of ZFC
- **1924**: **Tarski-Banach**, the AC paradox — cut a sphere into 5 pieces to make 2 spheres of the same size ("AC violates intuition")
- **1907**: **Brouwer**, *intuitionism* — rejection of the law of the excluded middle, admitting only *constructive* mathematics
- **1925**: **Hilbert**, *Über das Unendliche* — the *formalist program*: prove the consistency of arithmetic by *finitary methods*
- **1928**: **Hilbert-Ackermann**, explicit statement of the *decision problem* (Entscheidungsproblem) — *does there exist an algorithm that decides the truth of every mathematical statement?*

→ All three schools sought to formalize the foundation *completely*. Hilbert's confidence: "*Wir müssen wissen. Wir werden wissen.*" (We must know. We will know.)

**Decisive turn**: Hilbert's program's demand for a *self-consistency proof* was formally specified (1928) → the meta-question *can a formal system prove itself* was posed formally. The answer would come in the next act.

### Act 3 — Verification/extension: the limit proofs of Gödel, Church, Turing (1928–1936)

The period in which the *impossibility* of Hilbert's program was formally proven.

- **1929**: **Gödel**, *Vollständigkeit* — the *completeness* theorem of first-order logic (*formal derivability = semantic truth*)
- **1931**: **Gödel**, *Über formal unentscheidbare Sätze* — announcement of the **incompleteness theorems**
  - First: a consistent, arithmetic-containing formal system has an undecidable statement
  - Second: that system cannot prove its own consistency within itself
- **1934**: Gödel, Herbrand, Kleene, the definition of *recursive functions* (formalization of *computable*)
- **1935**: **Church**, *λ-calculus* — another model of computation
- **1936**: **Turing**, *On Computable Numbers* — the introduction of the *Turing machine*, the proof of the undecidability of the Halting Problem
- **1936**: the **Church-Turing** thesis — the equivalence *λ-calculus = Turing machine = recursive functions* (the intuitive definition of computability)
- **1936**: **Church**, the proof of the undecidability of the *Entscheidungsproblem* — *No* to Hilbert's 1928 question

→ The meta-unity of the Gödel, Church, and Turing results: *the limits of a formal system = the limits of algorithms*. Two expressions of the same mechanism (parallel to MM-06).

**Decisive turn**: the transition Gödel 1931 → Turing 1936 — *the limits of abstract formalism* were transformed into *physically realizable computational limits* → the starting points of computer science (CS) and AI were conceived at the same time (an advance signal that the next act of R-03's four acts is *cross-domain integration*).

### Act 4 — Reinterpretation/application: natural instances of undecidability + pluralization of foundations (1938–present)

The period in which it was seriously explored what *natural statements* undecidability appears as over ZFC.

- **1938**: **Gödel**, introduction of the *constructible universe* $L$ — proof of the consistency of ZFC + CH + GCH + AC (using model theory)
- **1940**: Gödel, the book *Consistency of CH* — a precise analysis of $L$
- **1963**: **Cohen**, *Independence of CH* — the introduction of **forcing**, the construction of a model of ZFC + ¬CH. Proof of the independence of CH. Awarded *the mathematics Nobel = the Fields Medal* in 1966
- **1960s–70s**: *large-cardinal axioms* in earnest — measurable, supercompact, Woodin, etc. Development of the *axiom of determinacy* (AD)
- **1970**: **Matiyasevich**, the undecidability of Hilbert's 10th — the absence of a general algorithm for solving Diophantine equations (Davis-Putnam-Robinson + Matiyasevich)
- **1980s**: **Shelah**, PCF theory — refinement of the singular cardinal hypothesis
- **1986**: **Friedman**, *reverse mathematics* — classification of theorems by their *proof strength* (RCA₀, WKL₀, ACA₀, ATR₀, Π¹₁-CA)
- **2000s–**: **Woodin**, the *Ω-conjecture* — the search for new axiom candidates that make CH decidable (Woodin "absoluteness")
- **2012–**: **Voevodsky**, *Univalent Foundations* (HoTT) — an alternative foundation, a homotopy-theoretic approach
- **2010s–**: **Lurie**, *∞-category theory* — the generalization of the categorical foundation (∞-topos, motivic homotopy theory)

→ *Undecidability* is reinterpreted not as a *formal limit* but as *degrees of freedom in the choice of axioms*. Mathematics evolves not toward *a single correct answer* but into a structure of *axiom diversity + consistency verification*.

**Decisive turn**: Cohen's 1963 forcing → the subsequent *large cardinals + AD + Univalent Foundations + ∞-categories* — entry into an era of *pluralized* foundations. The dream of a single foundation that Gödel 1931 closed off was transformed into *the search for diverse consistent foundations*.

### Core insight

The foundation of mathematics is not *a discipline that finds a complete correct answer* but *a discipline that self-proves how far decidability reaches*. The four acts — Cantor's discovery of infinity → the Russell/Frege paradox → Hilbert's formalism → the limit proofs of Gödel and Cohen — are all *one meta-mechanism*: **mathematics proving its own limits within its own formalism**.

This places mathematics in a position no other discipline holds — *the formal provability of its own limits*. PHYSICS discovers limits through external experiment, but mathematics proves limits *within the formalism itself*. This structure is the engine of evolution for all subsequent fields (category theory, homotopy theory, HoTT).

## Self-identified open problems (U-14)

Open problems this note itself identified:

1. **Does a true answer to CH exist** — after Cohen's 1963 ZFC independence, various answer candidates such as *Woodin's Ω-conjecture* / *large-cardinal axioms* / *forcing axioms*. But none has consensus as a *natural axiom*. **The very justification of axiom choice remains open**.
2. **The status of AC** — *violation of intuition* (Banach-Tarski) vs. *practical necessity for mathematics* (used in nearly all of algebra and analysis). A quantitative mapping of *which results collapse* if AC is rejected is incomplete.
3. **HoTT/Univalent Foundations vs. ZFC** — are they equivalent or a *genuine alternative*? A precise comparison of *which theorems are proved differently in the two foundations* is unfinished.
4. **The foundational status of ∞-categories** — Lurie's and Joyal's ∞-categories are defined over ZFC, but *whether the ∞-category itself is a foundation* is a meta-question.
5. **The depth of the equivalence computability = formal limit** — the *physical* realization of the Church-Turing thesis (*whether quantum computing breaks the Turing limit*) is unresolved.
6. **The completeness of reverse mathematics** — can every mathematical theorem be classified by *proof strength*? Most have been classified into the 5 levels RCA₀–Π¹₁-CA, but the *large-cardinal* region is still undetermined.
7. **The nature of mathematical intuition** — Gödel and Cohen: *we accept the unresolved as undecidable, yet mathematicians have intuitive answers*. Is this *intuition* something outside the formalism?
