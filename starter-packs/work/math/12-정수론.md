# Modern Number Theory

## One-line essence
The two questions — *where the primes are* (analytic) and *what structure the solutions of equations have* (algebraic) — converge into *a single discipline* through modular forms, p-adic numbers, and L-functions — and the completed blueprint of that convergence is precisely the Langlands program.

## Intuitive picture

Modern number theory is the field that treats *the oldest objects* (primes, integer equations) with *the most modern tools* (complex analysis, algebraic geometry, p-adic analysis).

Three scenes:

- **Riemann's 8 pages (1859)**: the prime-counting function $\pi(x)$ expressed completely through the *zeros* of the complex function $\zeta(s)$. The one-sentence conjecture "all nontrivial zeros lie on $\text{Re}(s) = \tfrac{1}{2}$" (the Riemann hypothesis) has been open for over 165 years — the *error term* of the prime distribution hangs on this single sentence.
- **Wiles's 7 years in the attic (1986–1995)**: Fermat's Last Theorem ($x^n + y^n = z^n$, no integer solutions for $n \geq 3$, 1637) was reduced (Frey-Ribet) to a theorem in an entirely different language — *the modularity of elliptic curves* — and Wiles proved semistable modularity, closing the 350-year-old open problem. **The equivalence of an algebraic object (the Galois representation) and an analytic object (the modular form)** was the proof engine.
- **The p-adic third eye (Hensel 1897 → Iwasawa 1950s)**: the reals $\mathbb{R}$ are not the only completion of $\mathbb{Q}$ — for each prime $p$ there is $\mathbb{Q}_p$ (Ostrowski 1916: that is all of them). The local-global viewpoint, seeing number-theoretic objects *at all $p$ simultaneously*, became the standard grammar of modern number theory.

The meta-insight of this field: **analytic number theory (prime distribution, $\zeta$, L-functions) and algebraic number theory (number fields, Galois, elliptic curves) are not a division of labor but *two readings of the same L-function*.** If Number Theory was the foundational narrative, this note is the *modern working structure* above it — and the Langlands program is the extension claiming the equivalence of these two readings *in every dimension*.

## Formal structure

### The prime number theorem (PNT) and the Riemann zeta

**Prime number theorem** (Hadamard, de la Vallée Poussin 1896, independently and simultaneously):
$$\pi(x) \sim \frac{x}{\log x} \quad (x \to \infty)$$

Core of the proof: $\zeta(s) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty n^{-s} = \prod_p (1 - p^{-s})^{-1}$ (the Euler product = the analytic form of the fundamental theorem of arithmetic) has no zeros on $\text{Re}(s) = 1$.

**Riemann hypothesis (RH, 1859)**: the nontrivial zeros of $\zeta(s)$ all lie on $\text{Re}(s) = \tfrac{1}{2}$.

The error-term statement equivalent to RH (von Koch 1901):
$$\pi(x) = \operatorname{Li}(x) + O(\sqrt{x} \log x)$$

→ RH is the final quantification of *how regularly the primes are distributed*. Hilbert's 8th, a Millennium Problem. Extended by the generalized RH of the Langlands program (all automorphic L-functions).

### Modular forms and elliptic curves — the modularity theorem

**Modular form**: a holomorphic function $f$ on the upper half-plane $\mathbb{H}$ satisfying the weight-$k$ transformation law:
$$f\!\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right) = (cz+d)^k f(z), \quad \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \in SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$$

The L-function of an **elliptic curve** $E/\mathbb{Q}: y^2 = x^3 + ax + b$:
$$L(E, s) = \prod_p L_p(E, s)^{-1}, \quad a_p = p + 1 - \#E(\mathbb{F}_p)$$

**Modularity theorem** (the Taniyama-Shimura-Weil conjecture 1955–57 → Wiles 1995 semistable → Breuil-Conrad-Diamond-Taylor 2001 complete): for every $E/\mathbb{Q}$ there exists a weight-2 newform $f$ such that
$$L(E, s) = L(f, s)$$

**The FLT proof chain**: assume $a^p + b^p = c^p$ → the Frey curve $y^2 = x(x - a^p)(x + b^p)$ (1985) → Ribet 1986: this curve cannot be modular → Wiles 1995: every semistable curve is modular → contradiction ∴ FLT.

→ The first grand verification of the equivalence *algebra (Galois representations) = analysis (modular forms)* = a partial proof of the Langlands program. For the physical face of modular forms, see Modular Forms ↔ String Theory.

### An overview of p-adic numbers and Iwasawa theory

**p-adic absolute value**: $|x|_p = p^{-v_p(x)}$ ($v_p$ = the $p$-adic valuation). The completion $\mathbb{Q}_p$, the ring of integers $\mathbb{Z}_p = \varprojlim \mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$.

**Ostrowski's theorem** (1916): the nontrivial absolute values on $\mathbb{Q}$ are exactly $|\cdot|_\infty$ and the $|\cdot|_p$ — *the complete list of local places*.

**Iwasawa theory** (Iwasawa 1950s–60s): how the $p$-part of the class group grows along the $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension tower $\mathbb{Q} \subset \mathbb{Q}_1 \subset \mathbb{Q}_2 \subset \cdots$:
$$\#A_n = p^{\mu p^n + \lambda n + \nu} \quad (n \gg 0)$$

**The Iwasawa main conjecture** (proved by Mazur-Wiles 1984, over $\mathbb{Q}$): the algebraic invariant (the characteristic ideal) coincides with the analytic object (the $p$-adic L-function, Kubota-Leopoldt).

→ The prototype of *the p-adic-dimensional integration of the analytic/algebraic division of labor*. The key tools of Wiles's FLT proof (Euler systems, Iwasawa theory) came from here.

### Analytic vs. algebraic number theory — the division of labor

| | Analytic number theory | Algebraic number theory |
|---|---|---|
| Objects | prime distribution, asymptotic formulas | number fields, ideals, Galois groups |
| Tools | $\zeta$, L-functions, the circle method, sieves | rings of integers, class groups, étale cohomology |
| Representative results | PNT 1896, Vinogradov 1937, Zhang 2013 | Kummer, Hilbert, class field theory 1927 |
| Question form | "how many" | "what structure" |

**The integration point**: the output of both camps is encoded in *L-functions* — the Dirichlet L-function (1837, primes in arithmetic progressions) is an analytic tool and at the same time the L-function of a Galois character. From the Langlands viewpoint, the division of labor is *a difference of local readings of the same automorphic object*.

### The BSD conjecture (Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer, 1965)

The Mordell-Weil group of $E/\mathbb{Q}$: $E(\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^r \oplus E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}$ (Mordell 1922).

**The BSD conjecture**:
$$\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L(E, s) = r = \operatorname{rank} E(\mathbb{Q})$$

The precise form claims that the leading coefficient is expressed through the regulator, the Tate-Shafarevich group $\Sha$, and the Tamagawa numbers.

**Partial results**: Coates-Wiles 1977 (CM curves, $L(E,1) \neq 0 \Rightarrow r = 0$) → Gross-Zagier 1986 (Heegner points, $L'(E,1)$) → Kolyvagin 1989 (Euler systems): if the analytic rank is 0 or 1, the rank part of BSD holds.

→ The frontline open problem of the integration of the division of labor — *the analytic object (the order of the L-function zero) = the algebraic object (the rank of the group of rational points)*. A Millennium Problem.

## Why it matters

- **The modern inheritance of the oldest question in mathematics**: the *2300-year continuity* from Euclid's infinitude of primes (300 BC) and Fermat (1637) to $\zeta$, Galois representations, and the perfectoid — the structure unique to number theory in which the questions are elementary and the tools are cutting-edge.
- **The mother of Langlands**: the Langlands program is the generalization of this note's class field theory, modular forms, and L-functions to the *entire non-abelian dimension* — **Langlands is the extension of number theory**, and Geometric Langlands is its geometric dimension. Without this note, Langlands floats in midair.
- **A direct PHYSICS bridge**: the $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ symmetry of modular forms = the modular invariance of string theory (Modular Forms ↔ String Theory) — partition functions, Monstrous Moonshine, and BPS-state counting are number-theoretic objects.
- **The foundation of cryptography**: RSA (integer factorization), ECC (the elliptic-curve discrete logarithm), post-quantum (lattices, isogenies) — all of modern security stands on the *computational asymmetry* of number-theoretic hard problems.
- **The point of contact with undecidability**: the undecidability of Hilbert's 10th (an algorithm for Diophantine equations) (Matiyasevich 1970) — directly linked to Foundations of Mathematics §Act 4. Number theory is a field where *concrete undecidability* lives.

## Historical context — R-03 four acts (mathematics variant)

### Act 1 — Birth: the introduction of analysis (1837–1896)

- **1837**: **Dirichlet**, infinitely many primes in the arithmetic progression $a + nd$ ($\gcd(a,d)=1$) — *the first injection of analytic methods (L-functions) into number theory*
- **1859**: **Riemann**, *Über die Anzahl der Primzahlen* — 8 pages. The analytic continuation of $\zeta(s)$, the functional equation, and the statement of the RH
- **1896**: **Hadamard, de la Vallée Poussin**, the proof of the PNT (independently, simultaneously) — $\zeta(1 + it) \neq 0$ is the key
- **1896–**: **Hilbert, Minkowski**, the *Zahlbericht* — the systematization of the theory of algebraic number fields

→ The *discrete* object of primes began to be conquered by the *continuous* tool of complex functions. The division-of-labor structure between the analytic camp and the algebraic camp formed.

**Decisive turn**: Riemann 1859 — the problem of the distribution of primes was completely translated into *a problem of the location of complex zeros*. All subsequent analytic number theory operates on this translation.

### Act 2 — Formalization: class field theory and modular forms (1897–1937)

- **1897**: **Hensel**, the introduction of p-adic numbers — the birth of the local viewpoint
- **1916**: **Ostrowski**, the classification of the absolute values on $\mathbb{Q}$ — the complete list of local places
- **1920–1927**: **Takagi, Artin**, the completion of class field theory — abelian Galois ↔ idele class (the starting point of Langlands program §Act 1)
- **1916–1920**: **Ramanujan, Mordell, Hecke**, $\tau(n)$ and Hecke operators — the formalization of the arithmetic structure of modular forms
- **1922**: **Mordell**, the finite-generation theorem for $E(\mathbb{Q})$
- **1937**: **Vinogradov**, the asymptotic resolution of odd Goldbach (the circle method)

→ The algebraic camp (class field theory) and the analytic camp (the circle method, Hecke theory) each completed their summit tools. Still separated from each other.

**Decisive turn**: Artin's 1927 reciprocity — the completion of the abelian dimension of *Galois information = analytic information*. The question of the non-abelian generalization was conceived (exploding 40 years later in Langlands's 1967 letter).

### Act 3 — Verification/extension: modularity and Iwasawa (1950s–1995)

- **1955–57**: **Taniyama-Shimura-Weil**, the conjecture that every elliptic curve is modular
- **1950s–60s**: **Iwasawa**, the theory of $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extensions — the growth law of the p-adic dimension
- **1965**: **Birch, Swinnerton-Dyer**, the BSD conjecture proposed on the basis of computer calculations (EDSAC computations — *a conjecture born of the computer*)
- **1977**: **Coates-Wiles**, the first partial result on BSD (CM curves)
- **1984**: **Mazur-Wiles**, the proof of the Iwasawa main conjecture (over $\mathbb{Q}$)
- **1985–86**: **Frey, Ribet**, the reduction FLT → modularity
- **1986**: **Gross-Zagier**, the Heegner-point formula
- **1989**: **Kolyvagin**, Euler systems — BSD for rank ≤ 1
- **1993–95**: **Wiles (with Taylor)**, semistable modularity = **the proof of FLT**

→ The tools of the three camps — algebraic, analytic, p-adic — were *fully mobilized within a single proof* (Wiles: Galois representations + the Hecke algebra + Iwasawa/Euler systems). The period in which the division of labor turned into collaboration.

**Decisive turn**: Wiles 1995 — the closing of the 350-year problem, and the proof that *the Galois ↔ automorphic equivalence works in practice*. Number theory was confirmed as the proving ground of the Langlands program.

### Act 4 — Reinterpretation/application: p-adic geometry and prime gaps (2001–present)

- **2001**: **Breuil-Conrad-Diamond-Taylor**, the complete modularity theorem
- **2004**: **Green-Tao**, arithmetic progressions of arbitrary length within the primes — the fusion with additive combinatorics (directly Modern Combinatorics)
- **2009**: **Khare-Wintenberger**, the complete proof of the Serre conjecture
- **2012**: **Scholze**, the perfectoid space — the reconstruction of p-adic geometry (Algebraic geometry §Act 4)
- **2013**: **Zhang Yitang**, the first proof of a bounded prime gap ($< 7 \times 10^7$) → **Maynard, Polymath 8** narrowed it to 246
- **2014**: **Bhargava**, bounds on the average rank of elliptic curves (Fields 2014) — the statistical approach to BSD
- **2018**: **Scholze**, the Fields Medal — p-adic Hodge, arithmetic Langlands
- **2021**: **Fargues-Scholze**, geometric local Langlands (Langlands program §Act 4)
- **2023–**: **AI for math** — the Lean 4 FLT formalization project (Buzzard) in progress

→ Number theory expands in every direction — *combinatorics (Green-Tao), geometry (perfectoid), physics (modular/string), AI (formal proof)*. From the era of the division of labor to the era of the *universal crossroads*.

**Decisive turn**: Scholze's perfectoid 2012 — the tilting equivalence between the p-adic world and the characteristic-$p$ world → the local theory of number theory is *completely recast as geometry*, leading directly to Fargues-Scholze 2021.

### Core insight

The four acts of modern number theory are the flow *the invasion of analysis (Riemann) → the formalization of algebra (Artin) → the collaboration of the two camps (Wiles) → the diffusion into geometry, combinatorics, and physics (Scholze, Green-Tao)*. The meta-mechanism: **every deepening of number theory is the discovery of an equivalence — "two different languages were saying the same thing"** — the PNT ($\pi(x) \leftrightarrow \zeta$ zeros), modularity ($E \leftrightarrow f$), the Iwasawa main conjecture (class group ↔ p-adic L), BSD (rank ↔ zero order). What *generalizes and organizes* this equivalence-discovery machine is precisely the Langlands program.

## Self-identified open problems (U-14)

Self-identified by this note:

1. **The Riemann hypothesis** — open since 1859. More than 40% of zeros verified on the critical line (Conrey 1989), the finite-field analogue proved (Weil, Deligne 1974) — the original remains unfinished
2. **The BSD conjecture** — the rank ≥ 2 cases entirely open. Even the finiteness of $\Sha$ is unproven in general
3. **The twin prime conjecture** — Zhang-Maynard reached 246, but 2 is unfinished. The parity problem is the essential barrier of the sieve
4. **The Goldbach conjecture** — the even version open (the odd version completed by Helfgott 2013)
5. **The ABC conjecture** — the community-verification dispute over Mochizuki's IUT proof claim (2012–2021 publication) is unresolved (consistent with Number Theory U-14)
6. **The generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH)** — the all-L-functions dimension. Mutually dependent with Langlands functoriality
7. **p-adic BSD and p-adic RH** — the complete proofs of the Iwasawa-theoretic analogues are unfinished
8. **A deterministic polynomial generation of primes** — the absence of an efficient deterministic formula generating primes (AKS 2002 solved only testing)
9. **A complete proof of Langlands** — the higher open problem subsuming all the equivalences of this note (Langlands program U-14 §1)
