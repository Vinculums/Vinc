## Mathematical structure

**AI production function (Aghion-Jones-Jones 2017)**:
$$Y = A \cdot K^{\alpha} \cdot L^{\beta} \cdot \text{AI}^{\gamma}, \quad \alpha + \beta + \gamma > 1?$$
- AI is *a new factor, neither capital nor labor*. If $\gamma$ is positive, *endogenously accelerating growth* (though hard to measure)
- Threshold hypothesis: when $\gamma$ reaches a threshold, *singularity-like* growth

**Network Effect (Metcalfe-Reed-Tilly)**:
- Metcalfe: $V \propto n^2$ (network value)
- Reed: $V \propto 2^n$ (when group formation is possible)
- Tilly: $V \propto n \log n$ (fits the empirics)
- **Winner-takes-all**: $V$ accelerates → market concentration is automatic

**Tirole two-sided markets (2003)**:
$$\pi = (p_1 - c_1) D_1(p_1, D_2) + (p_2 - c_2) D_2(p_2, D_1)$$
- indirect network effects → one side free·the other side expensive. The foundation of the Big Tech model.

**How Bitcoin works (Nakamoto 2008)**:
- Hash chain + PoW + cost of a 51% attack > reward = immutable consensus
- Inflation: $\text{halving every 4 years}$, total issuance capped at 21M

**Social cost of carbon (Nordhaus DICE·Stern Review)**:
$$\text{SCC} = \int_0^\infty e^{-\delta t} \cdot \frac{\partial \text{Damage}}{\partial \text{Emissions}_0} dt$$
- $\delta$ (discount rate): Stern 1.5% vs Nordhaus 3% → SCC $40 vs $200/tCO₂ (a 5x gap)
- the *philosophical limit* of policy quantification (intergenerational justice)

**Measuring generative-AI value** (Mollick·Brynjolfsson 2024):
- average labor productivity +30~40% (knowledge work)
- the gap for junior employees *narrows* (AI as an equalizing tool)
- the gap for experts *widens* (skilled AI users gain more)

**Technology adoption curve (Rogers 1962 + Bass 1969)**:
$$\frac{dN}{dt} = (p + q \cdot \frac{N}{M})(M - N)$$
- $p$ innovators·$q$ imitators·$M$ market size
- AI adoption: ChatGPT reached 100 million users in 2 months — *the fastest adoption curve in history*

### Act 1 — Emergence of information economics (1960~1990)

All 3 won the 2001 Nobel.

**Romer** (1990) — knowledge externalities + endogenous growth (consistent with 10 Schumpeter §2).

**Technological determinism vs. social constructivism** (Bijker·Pinch *SCOT* 1987) — the debate over whether technology determines society vs. society determines technology.

> **External-variable 5 axes (Act 1)**:
> - **World currents**: WWII·the Cold War·the information revolution (semiconductors 1947)·the birth of the internet (ARPANET 1969)
> - **Great figures**: Stigler·Akerlof·Spence·Stiglitz·Romer·Shannon (information theory)
> - **Technological revolution**: semiconductors·mainframes·minicomputers·UNIX·TCP/IP (1983)
