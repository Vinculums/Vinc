# game theory

## One-line essence
It is the formal theory of the *strategic interaction* of multiple agents — situations in which each one's decision depends on *each other's decisions* — and the *concept of equilibrium* is at its center.

## Intuitive picture
Economics asks "how does one person decide *about* the market." Game theory asks "how does one person decide about *another person*." A market is *anonymous*, but a game *names the opponent*. This one-word difference split the whole discipline.

- **The prisoner's dilemma**: Two prisoners are interrogated separately. If *both* stay silent, a light sentence. If *I* confess and the *opponent* stays silent, I go free and the opponent gets a heavy sentence. If both confess, both get a heavy sentence. The rational choice is *both confess* — both people reach *the outcome they do not want*. Individual rationality ≠ collective rationality. The *archetypal case* of game theory.
- **Nash equilibrium**: A state in which everyone, even knowing the opponents' strategies, has *no reason to change their own strategy*. In the prisoner's dilemma, (confess, confess) is Nash. Not an *optimality* but a *stability* concept.
- **The Schelling point**: "Where in New York shall we meet tomorrow at noon?" without an arrangement — most people say *in front of the Grand Central clock*. There is no reason, but *everyone guesses that everyone else will guess so*. A *focal point* growing out of *common knowledge*. Market prices, wartime ceasefire lines, currencies, and languages are all Schelling points.
- **Evolutionary games**: Animals cannot perform rational calculations either. But *evolution* naturally selects the *equilibrium strategy*. ESS (Evolutionarily Stable Strategy) — a strategy that *cannot be invaded* by an invading strategy. The equilibrium concept can work without a rationality assumption.
- **Mechanism design**: One designs the *rules of the game themselves* to make the desired outcome an *equilibrium*. Auction design, radio-frequency allocation, marriage matching, kidney exchange — all *reverse-engineered games*.

Game theory applies *everywhere there is strategic interaction*. Economics + politics + military + biology + computer science + AI alignment. If the micro (01) is a *market model* and behavioral (03) an *individual-cognition model*, then game theory is an *interaction model*. It grows at the *point of integration* of the three disciplines.

## Mathematical structure

**Strategic game (normal form)**: $G = (N, \{S_i\}, \{u_i\})$
- $N = \{1, \dots, n\}$: the set of players
- $S_i$: the strategy set of player $i$
- $u_i: S_1 \times \dots \times S_n \to \mathbb{R}$: the payoff function of $i$

**Nash equilibrium (1950, 1951)**:
$$\sigma_i^* \in \arg\max_{\sigma_i \in \Delta(S_i)} u_i(\sigma_i, \sigma_{-i}^*) \quad \forall i \in N$$
- $\Delta(S_i)$: a probability distribution over $S_i$ (*mixed strategy*)
- **Existence theorem** (Nash 1951, the Kakutani fixed-point theorem): a finite game always has a (mixed) Nash equilibrium

**Subgame Perfect Equilibrium (Selten 1965)**:
- A Nash equilibrium in every subgame — removing the *non-credible threat*
- *Backward induction*: solving from the last stage backward

**Bayesian Nash (Harsanyi 1967)**:
- A game of information asymmetry (incomplete information). Each player has a *type* $\theta_i$ — an equilibrium over the prior distribution $p(\theta)$
$$\sigma_i^*(\theta_i) \in \arg\max_{\sigma_i} \mathbb{E}_{\theta_{-i}}[u_i(\sigma_i, \sigma_{-i}^*; \theta)]$$
- The 1994 Nash-Harsanyi-Selten Nobel — the golden trio of game theory

**Evolutionarily Stable Strategy (Maynard Smith 1973)**:
$I$ is an ESS ↔ for every mutant $J \neq I$
$$u(I, I) > u(J, I) \quad \text{or} \quad u(I, I) = u(J, I) \land u(I, J) > u(J, J)$$
- No rationality assumption; *evolutionary dynamics* produce the equilibrium. Applied to biology, cultural evolution, and language evolution.

**Mechanism Design — the core axioms**:
- IC (Incentive Compatible): *truthful reporting* is the optimal strategy for each agent
- IR (Individual Rational): the payoff from participation ≥ the payoff from non-participation
- Budget Balance: no deficit for the mechanism operator
- **The VCG mechanism (Vickrey-Clarke-Groves)**: IC + efficient — but BB is not guaranteed. The core tool of auctions and public-goods decisions.
- **The Revelation Principle**: an outcome achievable by any mechanism is also achievable by a *direct truthful-reporting mechanism*

**Cooperative Game (Shapley 1953)**:
- The value function $v: 2^N \to \mathbb{R}$ — the total value of coalition $S$
- **Shapley value**: $\phi_i(v) = \sum_{S \not\ni i} \frac{|S|!(n-|S|-1)!}{n!} [v(S \cup \{i\}) - v(S)]$
- The formal definition of "fair distribution." Directly applied to ML-model interpretation (SHAP).

## Why it matters

Game theory evolved from *a tool of economics* to *the common language of all social, biological, and information sciences*.

Deeper positioning:
- **The point of integration of the micro (01), macro (02), and behavioral (03)**: The micro market is an *anonymous game*, macro policy is a *Fed vs. market game*, and behavioral is *level-k thinking* (game + cognitive limits). The four disciplines meet in game theory.
- **Overcoming the limits against real markets**: The *alternative* to the perfect-competition assumption of the micro (01) — oligopoly (Cournot, Bertrand), information asymmetry (Spence, Akerlof, Stiglitz), contracts (Hart-Holmström), auctions (Vickrey, Milgrom).
- **The policy applications of mechanism design**:
  - The U.S. FCC radio-frequency auction (Milgrom design, from 1994)
  - Kidney exchange (Roth, Shapley 2012 Nobel)
  - Medical-school matching (NRMP, Roth-Sotomayor 1990)
  - School assignment (Boston, NYC, Abdulkadiroğlu-Pathak)
  - Google and Facebook ad auctions (GSP, VCG)
- **The cross-domain application of evolutionary games**: Biology (Hamilton kin selection, Trivers reciprocal altruism), cultural evolution (Boyd-Richerson), language (Nowak), AI multi-agent.
- **Modern AI alignment**: Artificial-intelligence safety = *the mechanism design of the AI-vs.-human game*. RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) is a combination of *behavioral economics + game theory*. The core formal tool of AGI alignment.

Where game theory collapses (*the unrealism of the common-knowledge assumption*, *the multiple-equilibrium selection problem*, *the gap with bounded rationality*, *the emergent equilibria of AI multi-agents*), the next discipline springs up.

## Historical context (the R-03 economic-variant four acts + integration of the five external-variable axes)

### Act 1 — Emergence of theory (1838–1944)

**Cournot** (*Recherches*, 1838) — the *quantity decisions* of two firms in an oligopoly market. The archetype of the Cournot-Nash equilibrium. But it was merely *used as a mathematical tool*, not a general theory.

**Bertrand** (1883) — a critique of Cournot, a price-competition model. **The Edgeworth box** (1881) — the *contract curve* of a two-agent exchange.

**Borel** (1921) — the first formalization of mixed strategies. **von Neumann** (1928) — the proof of the *minimax theorem*. The *complete solution* of two-person zero-sum games.

**von Neumann-Morgenstern** (*Theory of Games and Economic Behavior*, 1944) — the *founding of the discipline* of game theory. Zero-sum games + cooperative games + the utility axioms. A 700-page monumental work — but it left the *general noncooperative game* unsolved.

> **The five external-variable axes**:
> - **Global currents**: World War II + the advent of nuclear weapons → an urgent need for a *quantitative tool of strategic decision-making*. The 1944 TGEB was published during the war (von Neumann advised the Manhattan Project).
> - **Great figures**: von Neumann (of Hungarian origin, a multidisciplinary giant of computer science, quantum mechanics, and game theory), Morgenstern (an Austrian economist). The decisive case of the *European → American scholarly exile*.
> - **Technological revolution**: Mathematical logic (Hilbert), set theory, topology (the Brouwer fixed point), probability theory — the *mathematical foundation* of game theory. The absence of computational tools (1944).
> - **National cultures**: Hungary (von Neumann, Harsanyi as a successor), Austria (Morgenstern), the United States (publication, dissemination). **The Hungarian genius group** was key (Wigner, Teller, Szilard, von Neumann).
> - **Social change**: Totalitarianism and the nuclear threat → an explosion of the social importance of *large-scale strategic decisions*.

**Decisive transition**: The 1944 TGEB left the *equilibrium concept of the general noncooperative game* unsolved — the core problem Nash would solve six years later.

### Act 2 — Formalization (1944–1980)

**Nash** (1950 *PNAS*, 1951 *Annals of Math*) — the general definition + existence proof of the *equilibrium of the general noncooperative game*. A 28-page PhD thesis founded the whole discipline. 1994 Nobel.

**The RAND Corporation** (from 1948) — funded by the U.S. military, it developed game theory as a *nuclear-strategy* tool. Nash, Shapley, Schelling, Bellman, and Dantzig were RAND researchers simultaneously. **The Cold War was the incubator of game theory**.

**Schelling** (*Strategy of Conflict*, 1960) — *focal point, credible commitment, tacit cooperation, deterrence*. The formal foundation of nuclear-deterrence theory. 2005 Nobel. He established the scholarly format of *light mathematics + heavy insight*.

**Selten** (1965) — *subgame perfect equilibrium*. Removing non-credible threats. 1994 Nobel.

**Harsanyi** (1967–68) — *Bayesian Nash*, games of information asymmetry. The concept of the *type space*. 1994 Nobel.

**Aumann** (1976) — the formal definition of *common knowledge*. 2005 Nobel. **The decisive contribution of the Israeli school** (parallel to Tversky and Kahneman).

**Cooperative games**: The Shapley value (1953), the core (Gillies 1953), the nucleolus (Schmeidler 1969).

> **The five external-variable axes**:
> - **Global currents**: The Cold War (1947–91), the Cuban Missile Crisis (1962), the Vietnam War. **Nuclear-deterrence theory** was the *national-security application* of game theory.
> - **Great figures**: Nash, Schelling, Selten, Harsanyi, Aumann (4 Nobels), Shapley, Bellman, Arrow. **The RAND network** was the *physical center* of the discipline.
> - **Technological revolution**: The advent of the computer → dynamic programming (Bellman 1957), linear programming (Dantzig 1947). The emergence of the *computational tools* of game theory.
> - **National cultures**: **U.S. RAND was the center of the discipline** (Nash, Schelling, Shapley), Israel (Aumann), Germany (Selten), of Hungarian origin (Harsanyi). A multinational network of scholars.
> - **Social change**: The nuclear threat + the arms race *standardized strategic thinking in society*. The *rational-actor* assumption penetrated politics and diplomacy.

**Decisive transition**: In 1972 Maynard Smith introduced game theory into *biology* → ESS — the possibility of abandoning the rationality assumption. The *explosion of cross-domain application* of the next act.

### Act 3 — Market verification and the explosion of application (1980–2000)

**Evolutionary games (Maynard Smith, Hamilton, Trivers)**: ESS, kin selection, reciprocal altruism. After the 1980s sociobiology wars (the Wilson critique), *gene-culture coevolution* (Boyd-Richerson) took hold.

**Axelrod** (*Evolution of Cooperation*, 1984) — an iterated prisoner's-dilemma tournament. *Tit-for-tat* won. Quantitative proof of the *evolutionary naturalness* of cooperation. A cross-domain influence on political science, biology, and CS.

**Industrial organization (Tirole)**: 1988 *Theory of Industrial Organization*. Applied game theory in earnest to *oligopoly, contracts, and regulation*. 2014 Nobel.

**Auction theory**:
- **Vickrey** (1961, 1996 Nobel): the second-price auction — IC + efficient
- **Milgrom-Weber** (1982): information-asymmetric auctions, common value
- **Milgrom design** (1994 FCC radio frequency): the discipline as a *direct policy outcome*. 2020 Nobel.

**Mechanism design (Hurwicz, Maskin, Myerson)**: formed from the 1970s, 2007 Nobel. *Revelation principle, implementation theory, optimal auction*. The discipline's *reverse-engineering* capability in earnest.

**Contract theory (Hart, Holmström)**: 1980s incomplete contracts, moral hazard, adverse selection. 2016 Nobel. The integration of game theory + information economics.

> **The five external-variable axes**:
> - **Global currents**: The end of the Cold War (1991), neoliberalism, the spread of the market economy. The application of game theory shifted from *military → market*.
> - **Great figures**: Maynard Smith, Hamilton, Axelrod (evolution), Tirole (industry), Milgrom, Vickrey (auctions), Hurwicz, Maskin, Myerson (mechanisms), Hart, Holmström (contracts). **A bumper age of Nobels** — game theory became the scholarly standard.
> - **Technological revolution**: The personal computer, simulation, the internet. The possibility of the *large-scale computational verification* of game theory.
> - **National cultures**: The United States (Stanford, Harvard, MIT as the center of mechanism design), Britain (LSE, Oxford industrial organization), France (Tirole, TSE), Israel (the heirs of Aumann). The *quantitative dispersion* of schools.
> - **Social change**: Market design became a *direct government-policy tool* (FCC radio frequency, kidney exchange, school assignment). The role of *scholar as policy designer* was standardized.

**Decisive transition**: The 1990s advent of the internet → the possibility of *algorithmic game theory* → the explosion of the CS-game-theory integration of the next act.

### Act 4 — Reinterpretation, expansion, and the AI era (2000–present)

**Algorithmic game theory** (Nisan-Roughgarden-Tardos-Vazirani 2007 *Algorithmic Game Theory*):
- The *complexity* of equilibrium computation (PPAD-complete, Daskalakis 2009)
- *Price of Anarchy* (Koutsoupias-Papadimitriou 1999): how much the equilibrium differs from the social optimum
- Algorithmic mechanism design (Nisan-Ronen 1999)

**ML + game theory**:
- **Multi-agent RL** (Littman 1994, MADDPG 2017, Lowe et al.)
- **AlphaGo, AlphaZero** (DeepMind from 2016) — Nash-equilibrium approximation (counterfactual regret minimization)
- **Pluribus** (2019) — superhuman multiplayer poker, the MCCFR algorithm
- **OpenAI Five, AlphaStar** (from 2018) — the conquest of multi-agent RL games

**The expansion of mechanism-design application**:
- **Roth-Shapley 2012 Nobel** — matching markets (medical school, school, kidney exchange)
- **Milgrom 2020 Nobel** — a new auction format (the incentive auction, FCC 2017)
- **Blockchain game theory** — Bitcoin Nakamoto consensus, Ethereum gas auction, MEV, DeFi

**The LLM and AI-alignment era** (from 2020):
- **RLHF** (Christiano et al. 2017, OpenAI ChatGPT) — the game between human preferences and the LLM. A combination of behavioral (03) + game theory.
- **Multi-LLM negotiation** (Park et al. 2023, Generative Agents) — LLMs participate as game players. The equilibrium is *emergent*.
- **Constitutional AI** (Anthropic 2022) — the LLM application of mechanism design. A core AI-safety tool.
- **AGI alignment** — formalized as *the mechanism design of the AI-vs.-human multi-agent game* (Russell, Hadfield-Menell, Christiano).

**The refinement of evolutionary and cultural games**:
- **Henrich** (*Secret of Our Success*, 2015) — a game model of culture-gene coevolution
- **Bowles-Gintis** *A Cooperative Species* (2011) — quantifying the evolutionary mechanism of cooperation
- **Nowak** (*Evolutionary Dynamics*, 2006) — the integration of game theory + population dynamics

> **The five external-variable axes**:
> - **Global currents**: The internet, the platform economy, blockchain, COVID, the advent of AI. *Algorithms as the infrastructure of social decision-making* — game theory as the *formal system of digital-society operation*.
> - **Great figures**: Roth, Milgrom, Tirole (traditional), Nisan, Roughgarden, Tardos (algorithmic), DeepMind, OpenAI, Anthropic researchers (AI). **The age of CS and AI integration**.
> - **Technological revolution**: Multi-agent RL, LLMs, blockchain, automated mechanism design (auto-bidding). The *real-time automated operation* of game theory becomes possible.
> - **National cultures**: The United States (the home of CS-game-theory, OpenAI, DeepMind, Stanford), China (Tencent and Alibaba mechanism-design application, a game-theoretic critique of the national social-credit system), the EU (AI-regulation mechanism design). **The dependence of AI governance on game theory**.
> - **Social change**: *Algorithmic governance* (automated pricing, matching, content recommendation, credit scores) became a social standard. *AI alignment* became a core question of humanity's future. Game theory as a *tool for operating civilization*.

**Decisive transition**: The imminent advent of AGI (from 2023) → *the mechanism design of the AI-vs.-human multi-agent game* rose as the *historical duty* of the discipline. Game theory evolved from an *economic tool* into a *civilizational tool*.
