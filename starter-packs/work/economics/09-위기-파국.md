# financial crisis

## One-line essence
*Stability breeds instability* (Minsky) — a crisis is not an external shock but the *internal dynamics* of capitalism, and its quantitative modeling is the frontier domain of 21c macro-finance.

## Intuitive picture
All 4 cycles (05) ended in crisis. *Why a crisis every time* is a question that none of the disciplines of Acts 1–7 could answer — this note is the *ongoing progress* toward that answer.

- **Minsky (1919~96)** — a lifelong *maverick*. The true heir of Keynes, yet *peripheral* in both the Keynesian-consensus and the neoliberal eras. His 1986 *Stabilizing an Unstable Economy* was recognized posthumously. **During the 2008 crisis the term *Minsky moment*** became common.

  Core thesis: **financial stability *itself* breeds instability**. Stable period → confidence ↑ → debt ↑ → leverage ↑ → collapse even under a small shock. The *internal dynamics* of capitalism.

- **3 stages of debt (Minsky)**:
  1. **Hedge**: cash flow repays both principal and interest
  2. **Speculative**: cash flow repays interest only (principal is rolled over)
  3. **Ponzi**: cash flow cannot even repay interest (relies solely on rising asset prices)

  In a stable period *all agents simultaneously* move 1→2→3. At stage 3 a small shock → cascading defaults → crisis.

- **Reinhart-Rogoff (2009)** — 800 years of crisis data. **"This time is different"** (*This Time is Different*) — the exact same words are repeated right before every crisis. A crisis is an *invariant pattern*: asset bubble → credit explosion → external borrowing → crisis → debt deflation → 6~8 year recovery.

- **Taleb *Black Swan* (2007)** — discards the Gaussian-distribution assumption. Financial returns are *fat-tailed* (tail events overwhelmingly outweigh the mean distribution). *Antifragility* — systems that grow *stronger* under shock vs. systems that grow *weaker*.

- **Schularick-Taylor (2012)** — 140 years of credit data for 14 countries. A *credit explosion* is the most powerful leading signal of a crisis. Money supply is irrelevant; the *credit ratio* is the essence.

- **2008 full disciplinary absorption**: Bernanke (a Great Depression PhD) → Fed chair → quantitative easing + systemic-risk tools. **Adrian-Shin (NY Fed)** — liquidity·funding spirals. **Geanakoplos** leverage cycle. **HANK** (Kaplan-Moll-Violante) — integrates distributional dynamics into crisis models.

This note is *academic-tracking mode* in earnest — 21c crisis scholarship is *ongoing progress*. The *completion point* of major root 5/6.

## Mathematical structure

**Minsky debt classification**:
$$\text{Hedge: } CF_t \geq P_t + I_t \quad \forall t$$
$$\text{Speculative: } CF_t \geq I_t \text{ but } CF_t < P_t + I_t$$
$$\text{Ponzi: } CF_t < I_t$$

**Financial accelerator (Bernanke-Gertler-Gilchrist 1999)** — collateralized lending:
$$\text{borrow}_t = \min(B_t, \theta \cdot \text{collateral}_t)$$
- asset price ↓ → collateral value ↓ → borrowing limit ↓ → asset sales → price ↓ (self-reinforcing spiral)
- *real shock → financial shock → real shock* amplification mechanism

**Leverage Cycle (Geanakoplos 2009)**:
$$\text{Leverage} = \frac{\text{Asset}}{\text{Equity}}$$
- leverage ↑ in stable times → margin calls in a crisis → forced sales → price ↓ → more margin calls
- the 2008 LTCM·Bear Stearns·Lehman bankruptcy dynamics

**Reinhart-Rogoff standard post-crisis pattern**:
- house prices average -35% (6 years)
- equities -55% (3.5 years)
- unemployment rate +7%p (persisting 5 years)
- GDP -9% (2 years)
- government debt +86% (after 3 years)

**Schularick-Taylor (2012)** — crisis prediction:
$$P(\text{crisis}_{t+5}) = f(\Delta \text{credit/GDP}_t, \text{leverage}_t)$$
- the change in the credit ratio 5 years earlier is the *most powerful leading signal*. *Money supply is irrelevant*.

**Taleb fat-tailed distribution**:
$$P(X > x) \sim x^{-\alpha} \quad (\alpha < \infty)$$
- Gaussian: $\alpha \to \infty$ → tail events can be ignored
- power law: $\alpha = 2~4$ → tail events overwhelm the mean
- **Mandelbrot** (1963) cotton prices → generalized by Taleb

**Antifragility quantification** (Taleb):
- $f''(x) > 0$ — the function's second derivative positive = *favorable* under shock (antifragile)
- $f''(x) < 0$ — *unfavorable* under shock (fragile)
- options·businesses·biological systems·cities are antifragile, giant banks·bureaucracies are fragile

**HANK crisis dynamics** (Kaplan-Moll-Violante 2018):
- macro dependent on the household wealth distribution — *differences in the marginal propensity of the wealthy·middle class·poor* are the essence of crisis dynamics
- in a crisis the *wealth-poverty asymmetry* accelerates (wealth concentration → accelerated further by the crisis)

### Act 1 — Formation of a maverick discipline (1986~2007)

**Minsky** *Stabilizing an Unstable Economy* (1986) — the peak of a lifetime's work. Yet as a *Keynesian left + heterodox* figure, peripheral to academia.

**Mandelbrot** *Fractals and Scaling in Finance* (1997) — a *non-Gaussian finance hypothesis* ever since cotton prices (1963). Exposes EMH·CAPM as *wrong assumptions*.

**Bernanke-Gertler** (1989·1999 *Handbook of Macroeconomics*) — the financial accelerator. Yet only *partly integrated* into the standard DSGE model.

**Reinhart-Rogoff** (1999·2008 — *This Time is Different* academic paper) — collected 800 years of crisis data. Yet recognized only *right before the crisis*.

**Taleb** *Fooled by Randomness* (2001)·*Black Swan* (2007) — from outside academia (trader + philosopher) → a shock to academia.

**1987 Black Monday·1997 Asia·1998 LTCM·2000 dot-com** — 4 crises + academia *surprised* every time. The *Great Moderation* (1985~2007 declining volatility) hypothesis masked the incompetence on crises.
