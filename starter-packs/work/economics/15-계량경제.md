# Econometrics

## One-line essence
Empirical economics is the fight over "how to extract causation from correlation" — the *potential-outcomes framework + natural experiments (IV·DID·RDD)* was established as the answer and sealed by the 2021 Nobel (Card·Angrist·Imbens).

## Intuitive picture
If 01~14 are *theory*, this 15 is *the courtroom that decides with data whether that theory is true*. The core problem is a single one — we cannot observe *the same person's treated world and untreated world* simultaneously (the fundamental problem of causal inference).

- **Potential Outcomes (Rubin)**: for individual $i$, the outcome under treatment $Y_i(1)$ and under no treatment $Y_i(0)$ *both exist* but only one is observed. The causal effect = $Y_i(1) - Y_i(0)$ — restoring the unobservable counterfactual by *design* is the whole of econometrics.
- **The randomized experiment is the gold standard**: an RCT assigns treatment by a coin flip → the two groups are on average identical → the difference = causation. The problem is that economics' big questions (minimum wage·education·immigration) cannot be experimented on.
- **Natural Experiments**: find the quasi-random assignment the world *accidentally* created. A minimum-wage hike applied only to New Jersey (Card-Krueger 1994)·the Vietnam draft lottery (Angrist 1990) — the *credibility revolution*.
- **The 3 great design tools**:
  - **IV (instrumental variable)**: a variable correlated with the treatment and linked to the outcome only through the treatment, bypassing endogeneity
  - **DID (difference-in-differences)**: the *difference of differences* of before-after × treatment·control groups — the common-trend assumption
  - **RDD (regression discontinuity)**: near a threshold (cutoff) it is quasi-random — a local causal effect
- **Time series**: macro data are *non-stationary* — ignoring unit roots·cointegration gives *spurious regression*. VAR estimates multivariate dynamics with minimal theoretical assumptions.
- **The structural vs. reduced-form debate**: the reduced form (natural experiments) offers *credibility*, structural models offer *counterfactual extrapolation·policy simulation*. The methodological war of Angrist-Pischke vs Heckman·Rust — now a complementary convergence.
- **Convergence with ML**: machine learning does prediction, econometrics does causation — DML (Double ML)·causal forest combine the two. The new standard of the high-dimensional control-variable era.

## Mathematical structure

**Potential outcomes and the fundamental problem** (Neyman 1923·Rubin 1974):
$$\tau_i = Y_i(1) - Y_i(0), \quad \text{observed: } Y_i = D_i Y_i(1) + (1-D_i) Y_i(0)$$
- the individual effect $\tau_i$ is unobservable → identify the average treatment effect $\text{ATE} = \mathbb{E}[Y(1) - Y(0)]$
- the bias of a naive comparison: $\mathbb{E}[Y|D{=}1] - \mathbb{E}[Y|D{=}0] = \text{ATE} + \underbrace{\text{selection bias}}_{\mathbb{E}[Y(0)|D=1]-\mathbb{E}[Y(0)|D=0]}$

**Instrumental variable (IV)** (Wright 1928·Angrist-Imbens-Rubin 1996):
$$\hat{\beta}_{IV} = \frac{\text{Cov}(Z, Y)}{\text{Cov}(Z, D)}$$
- conditions: relevance $\text{Cov}(Z,D) \neq 0$ + exclusion restriction ($Z$ affects $Y$ only through $D$)
- Imbens-Angrist (1994): under heterogeneous effects, IV identifies only the **LATE** (the local effect on those who comply with the instrument) — the theoretical core of the 2021 Nobel

**Difference-in-differences (DID)**:
$$\hat{\tau}_{DID} = (\bar{Y}_{T,post} - \bar{Y}_{T,pre}) - (\bar{Y}_{C,post} - \bar{Y}_{C,pre})$$
- identifying assumption: *parallel trends* (absent treatment, the two groups' trends are identical)
- 21c refinement: staggered-adoption bias (Goodman-Bacon 2021·Callaway-Sant'Anna 2021) — a re-founding of difference-in-differences itself

**Regression discontinuity (RDD)** (Thistlethwaite-Campbell 1960·Hahn-Todd-van der Klaauw 2001):
$$\tau_{RDD} = \lim_{x \downarrow c} \mathbb{E}[Y|X=x] - \lim_{x \uparrow c} \mathbb{E}[Y|X=x]$$
- quasi-random locally at the threshold $c$ — local polynomial regression + optimal bandwidth (Imbens-Kalyanaraman 2012)

**Unit root·cointegration** (Dickey-Fuller 1979·Engle-Granger 1987):
$$y_t = \rho y_{t-1} + \varepsilon_t, \quad \rho = 1 \Rightarrow \text{unit root (permanent shock)}$$
- regression between $I(1)$ variables is spurious regression (Granger-Newbold 1974) — but if a linear combination is $I(0)$, then **cointegration** = a long-run equilibrium relation exists (Engle-Granger, 2003 Nobel)

**VAR** (Sims 1980):
$$\mathbf{y}_t = A_1 \mathbf{y}_{t-1} + \cdots + A_p \mathbf{y}_{t-p} + \mathbf{u}_t$$
- estimates the dynamics of a vector of macro variables with *minimal a priori restrictions* — impulse-response functions·variance decomposition. Sims 2011 Nobel

**Panel fixed effects**:
$$y_{it} = \beta x_{it} + \alpha_i + \gamma_t + \varepsilon_{it}$$
- the entity fixed effect $\alpha_i$ removes *time-invariant unobserved heterogeneity* — the basic weapon of panel data

**Double Machine Learning (DML)** (Chernozhukov et al. 2018):
$$Y - \hat{\mathbb{E}}[Y|X] = \tau \,(D - \hat{\mathbb{E}}[D|X]) + \nu$$
- estimate nuisance functions with ML + Neyman orthogonalization + cross-fitting → ML's regularization bias does not enter the $\tau$ estimate to first order — a formal marriage of ML and econometrics

## Why it matters

Econometrics is *the quality-control department of all of economics*. 01 micro's demand elasticity·02 macro's multiplier·03 behavioral's bias magnitude·14 financial engineering's volatility — none have numbers without econometrics.

Deeper position:
- **The disciplinary-historical meaning of the credibility revolution**: the 1980s critique "econometrics can produce any conclusion" (Leamer 1983 *Let's Take the Con Out of Econometrics*) → natural-experiment design was the answer → the completing axis of economics' *becoming an empirical science*
- **Integration with 12 behavioral economics advanced**: the antidote to the replication crisis is precisely econometric precision — preregistration·p-hacking detection (p-curve)·multiple-testing correction
- **Integration with 14 financial engineering**: Financial Engineering's volatility·risk models have as their empirical counterpart time-series econometrics (ARCH·GARCH, Engle 2003 Nobel). The rough-volatility debate too is ultimately an econometric identification problem
- **Integration with 02 macro**: the Lucas critique (1976) — reduced-form relations collapse when policy changes → the root of the structural (DSGE) vs VAR debate
- **The core of real-world policy**:
  - minimum wage (Card-Krueger 1994 → the 21c re-verification battle): the flagship battlefield of DID
  - returns to education (Angrist-Krueger 1991 quarter-of-birth IV)
  - immigration effects (Card 1990 Mariel boatlift)
  - development-policy RCTs (Banerjee-Duflo-Kremer 2019 Nobel — the expansion of the experimentable domain)
- **Limits vs. reality**:
  - *external validity*: LATE is a complier local effect — not extrapolable to other contexts (the Deaton-Heckman critique)
  - *weak instruments*: weak IV explodes the bias (Staiger-Stock 1997)
  - *parallel trends unverifiable*: the DID assumption is fundamentally counterfactual
  - *p-hacking·publication bias*: distortion of the significance threshold (Brodeur et al.)
  - *big data ≠ causation*: a larger sample does not make selection bias disappear (Meng 2018)

## Historical context (R-03 economics-variant 4 acts + external-variable 5 axes integrated)

### Act 1 — Founding: Cowles and structural equations (1928~1970)

**Wright** (1928) — the first use of instrumental variables (father-and-son Philip·Sewall Wright, path analysis). **Frisch·Tinbergen** — the founding of econometrics, Econometrica (1933)·the first economics Nobel (1969). **Haavelmo** *The Probability Approach in Econometrics* (1944) — the paradigm of viewing economic data as a *probability sample* (1989 Nobel). **The Cowles Commission** (1940s~50s) — structural estimation of simultaneous equations·formalizing the identification problem (Koopmans). **Klein** macro-econometric models (1980 Nobel).

> **External-variable 5 axes (Act 1)**:
> - **World currents**: the Great Depression → demand for economic forecasting·WWII planned economy
> - **Great figures**: Frisch·Tinbergen·Haavelmo·Koopmans·Klein·Wright
> - **Technological revolution**: axiomatization of probability (Kolmogorov 1933)·early computers·national-accounts statistics
> - **National culture**: Norway·the Netherlands → the move of the US Cowles (Chicago→Yale)
> - **Social change**: Keynesian policy → government demand for macro models

**Decisive turn**: 1970s stagflation + the **Lucas critique** (1976) — estimated relations without structure collapse under a policy change → trust in giant macro models plummeted. The entrance to the next act's two branches (VAR vs micro empirics).

### Act 2 — The time-series revolution + the credibility crisis (1970~1990)

**Granger-Newbold** (1974) spurious regression — exposing the fictitiousness of non-stationary time-series regression. **Dickey-Fuller** (1979) the unit-root test. **Sims** (1980) *Macroeconomics and Reality* — the VAR that discards structural restrictions. **Engle** (1982) ARCH — quantifying volatility clustering. **Engle-Granger** (1987) cointegration — long-run equilibrium of non-stationary variables (Engle·Granger 2003 Nobel). **Leamer** (1983) — the critique of specification arbitrariness, bringing the credibility crisis into public debate. **Heckman** (1979) sample-selection correction (2000 Nobel).

> **External-variable 5 axes (Act 2)**:
> - **World currents**: stagflation·the Volcker disinflation·financial liberalization
> - **Great figures**: Granger·Engle·Sims·Dickey·Fuller·Leamer·Heckman·Lucas
> - **Technological revolution**: mainframe→PC econometric software (TSP·RATS)
> - **National culture**: the US (the UCSD time-series cluster·the Minnesota VAR)·the UK (LSE Hendry methodology)
> - **Social change**: demand for policy evaluation (welfare·education program evaluation)

**Decisive turn**: the Leamer critique + the mass availability of micro data → "design creates credibility" — the entrance to the natural-experiment revolution.

### Act 3 — The credibility revolution: the 3 great natural-experiment tools (1990~2010)

**Angrist** (1990) the Vietnam draft-lottery IV. **Angrist-Krueger** (1991) the quarter-of-birth IV — returns to education. **Card** (1990) Mariel boatlift·**Card-Krueger** (1994) the New Jersey-Pennsylvania minimum-wage DID — the shock that *the minimum wage did not reduce employment*. **Imbens-Angrist** (1994) the LATE theorem — the precise definition of what IV identifies. **Angrist-Imbens-Rubin** (1996) the potential-outcomes formalization of IV. The revival of RDD (Hahn-Todd-van der Klaauw 2001·Lee 2008). **Angrist-Pischke** *Mostly Harmless Econometrics* (2009) — the school textbook. On the opposite side, **Heckman**·**Deaton** — the structural·external-validity counterattack. **Pearl** — do-calculus·DAGs (the computer-science-born causal revolution, in tension·convergence with econometrics).

> **External-variable 5 axes (Act 3)**:
> - **World currents**: the end of the Cold War·globalization·welfare reform (US 1996) evaluation demand
> - **Great figures**: Angrist·Card·Krueger·Imbens·Rubin·Heckman·Deaton·Pearl
> - **Technological revolution**: administrative data·micro-census·Stata standardization
> - **National culture**: the US (MIT·Princeton labor-economics cluster) dominant·development-economics RCTs (India·Kenya field)
> - **Social change**: the evidence-based-policy movement·the minimum-wage political debate

**Decisive turn**: the spread of RCTs into development economics (Duflo·Banerjee·Kremer, J-PAL 2003) + the emergence of big data → the triple convergence of experiments·quasi-experiments·ML. The entrance to the next act.

### Act 4 — The Nobel seal + ML convergence (2010~present)

**2019 Nobel** Banerjee-Duflo-Kremer — development-economics RCTs. **2021 Nobel** **Card** (natural-experiment empirics) + **Angrist-Imbens** (LATE methodology) — the *official seal of the credibility revolution*. **The DID re-founding**: Goodman-Bacon (2021)·Callaway-Sant'Anna (2021)·Sun-Abraham (2021) — a boom in correcting staggered-DID bias. **DML** (Chernozhukov et al. 2018)·**causal forest** (Wager-Athey 2018) — heterogeneous treatment effects via ML. **Athey·Imbens** — a synthesis review of econometrics+ML. **Synthetic control** (Abadie 2010·2021) — the counterfactual for a single treated unit. The LLM era: text as data (Gentzkow-Shapiro)·the LLM-simulated-subject debate (connecting to note 12).

> **External-variable 5 axes (Act 4)**:
> - **World currents**: post-financial-crisis inequality debate·COVID's mass generation of natural experiments·AI
> - **Great figures**: Card·Angrist·Imbens·Athey·Chernozhukov·Abadie·Duflo
> - **Technological revolution**: big data·ML·administrative-data linkage (register data)·cloud computation
> - **National culture**: the US·the Nordics (the whole-population register-data advantage)·the development-economics Global South field
> - **Social change**: institutionalization of evidence-based policy·the reproducibility movement (preregistration)·data-privacy regulation

**Decisive turn** (ongoing): DML + heterogeneous-effect ML + LLM text data = *econometrics 3.0*. The causal questions stay the same, while the counterfactual-estimation engine is being replaced by ML.

### Core insight

The essence of the 4 acts of econometrics is the gradual victory of the principle that *identification precedes estimation*. Act 1 Cowles by structure, Act 2 time series by probabilistic structure, Act 3 natural experiments by *design*, Act 4 ML by *orthogonalization* — each act rediscovered, with a different tool, the same lesson that "not the data but the *assumptions* buy causation." **The 2021 Nobel is a prize given to method itself** — the moment methodology became coequal with theory in economics, showing that for a complex-systems discipline dealing with *un-experimentable objects*, the refinement of design is the only path.
