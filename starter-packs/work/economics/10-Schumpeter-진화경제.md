# Schumpeter Evolutionary Economics

## One-line essence
*Creative destruction* — the essence of capitalism is not equilibrium but a *storm of innovation*, and 80 years later it is directly inherited by *technological paradigms·endogenous growth·platforms·AI*.

## Intuitive picture
Micro (01)·macro (02) ask about *equilibrium*. Schumpeter answered that *not equilibrium but *innovation* is the essence*. In 1942. And for 80 years he was *shunned by the mainstream* — only the 21c AI·platform era *revived* him.

- **Creative destruction**: new technology → destroys old industries → creates new industries. 1900 carriages → 1920 automobiles → 1990 aviation·1995 the internet·2010 smartphones·2022 ChatGPT. *Equilibrium is momentary + destruction is the essence*.
- **Entrepreneur**: not merely a capitalist. One who makes *new combinations*. 5 combinations: new product·new method·new market·new raw material·new organization.
- **The self-negation of capitalism**: Schumpeter's pessimistic prediction — capitalism's success → bureaucratization of big firms → entrepreneurship disappears → transition to socialism. Only the *bureaucratization of big firms* part was correct; socialism was not.
- **Nelson-Winter (1982)**: biological evolution → economics. *The firm = genes (routine)*, *the market = natural selection*, *innovation = mutation*. An *evolutionary model* of productivity·technological change.
- **Aghion-Howitt (1992)**: *mathematized* creative destruction. An endogenous growth model — the growth rate is a function of *R&D investment + market structure*. Discards the neoclassical Solow's exogenous-technology assumption.
- **Christensen (1997 *Innovator's Dilemma*)**: *disruptive innovation* — a small startup bypasses a giant firm in the *low-end market*. The death of Kodak·Blackberry·Nokia·Blockbuster.
- **21c revival**: the AI·platform era = a *Schumpeterian supercycle*. The Big Tech 5 (MAANG) create a new industry + destroy an old industry every decade. Amazon (retail)·Uber (taxis)·Netflix (film)·OpenAI (knowledge)·Anthropic·Tesla (automobiles).

This discipline is the natural successor to *crisis·catastrophe (09)* — a crisis is not an *external shock* but a *dynamic byproduct of creative destruction*. The successor to environment·culture (08) — the disciplinary foundation for the transformation of platform·AI industries.

## Mathematical structure

**Aghion-Howitt 1992 endogenous growth**:
$$g = \lambda \cdot \mu(n, \phi) \cdot (\gamma - 1)$$
- $g$: growth rate, $\lambda$: innovation arrival rate, $\mu$: research intensity, $\gamma$: innovation size
- $n$: R&D labor, $\phi$: market-structure parameter
- Core: the growth rate is *endogenous* — policy·market structure·R&D affect it

**Romer 1990 endogenous growth (varieties)**:
$$Y = A \cdot K^\alpha \cdot L^{1-\alpha}, \quad \dot{A} = \delta \cdot A \cdot H_A$$
- $H_A$: R&D human capital. Technological progress $A$ is *non-excludable + non-rival* through *knowledge externalities*. 2018 Nobel.

**Nelson-Winter routines**:
$$\text{firm}_i = \{R_i^1, R_i^2, \dots\}, \quad R_i^j = \text{routine}$$
- A firm operates as a set of *fixed routines*. Environmental change → some routines unfit → *firm elimination* or *mutation*.
- Firm = gene pool, market = natural selection, innovation = mutation.

**Christensen disruptive innovation**:
- *Sustaining innovation*: existing market + improvement along the existing value dimension → advantage to giant firms
- *Disruptive innovation*: new market + new value dimension → advantage to startups
- Giant firms ignore disruption through *rational decision-making* → consequently are eliminated

**Quality ladder model (Grossman-Helpman)**:
$$P(\text{innovate at } t) = \lambda(R_t)$$
- at each moment a new technology appears with probability $\lambda$ → *complete replacement* of incumbent firms. A *Poisson arrival* model.

**Schumpeterian market structure + R&D**:
- monopoly → stable R&D possible (Schumpeter mark II)
- competition → creative pressure (Schumpeter mark I)
- the two effects trade off → optimal market structure is *non-competitive + non-monopolistic* (inverted-U, Aghion et al.)

## Historical context (R-03 economics-variant 4 acts + external-variable 5 axes integrated)

### Act 1 — Schumpeter's emergence + being shunned (1942~1980)

**Schumpeter** (1883~1950) — of Austrian origin·émigré at Harvard (1932~). 1911 *Theory of Economic Development* (in German)·1939 *Business Cycles*·**1942 *Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy***. Among the 8 giants (06), *the only giant who dealt in earnest with dynamics rather than equilibrium*.

**Core thesis** (1942):
1. capitalism = creative destruction
2. the entrepreneur is the essence
3. capitalism's success → self-negation (bureaucratization of big firms → socialism)
4. socialism + democracy are compatible (though less efficient than capitalism)

**Problem**: Keynes·neoclassicals standardized the discipline around the *equilibrium model* → Schumpeter was *peripheral* (even more shunned after his death in 1950).

**Galbraith** (*New Industrial State*, 1967) — a Schumpeter mark II heir (big firms = the agents of innovation).

**First appearance of the evolutionary hypothesis**: **Alchian** (1950 *Uncertainty, Evolution, and Economic Theory*) — a firm survives if it is *fit for survival* rather than *rational*. A direct precursor of Nelson-Winter.

> **External-variable 5 axes (Act 1)**:
> - **World currents**: WWII·the postwar golden age·the Cold War·capitalist confidence (1940s~70s)
> - **Great figures**: Schumpeter (a lonely giant)·Galbraith·Alchian·Hayek (a spiritual ally of Schumpeter — innovation·dispersion of knowledge)
> - **Technological revolution**: the late second industrial revolution·the emergence of semiconductors (1947)·space development·mainframe computers. **Innovation exploded outside the discipline**, yet the discipline dealt only with equilibrium.
> - **National culture**: US (creative capitalism)·Japan (1950s~ developmental state + the Toyota production system as an evolutionary case)·USSR (planned economy — partly matching Schumpeter's precise prediction)
> - **Social change**: mass production + mass consumption + the middle class — Schumpeter's pessimistic prediction (bureaucratization of big firms) partly came true

**Decisive turn**: 1970s stagflation → the limits of neoclassicism + the 1980s explosion of innovation (Silicon Valley·semiconductors·the PC) → the *entrance to Schumpeter's revival*.

### Act 2 — Formalization of evolutionary economics + technological paradigms (1980~2000)

**Nelson-Winter** *Evolutionary Theory of Economic Change* (1982) — the founding of evolutionary economics. Biological evolution → economics. 5 core concepts:
1. **Routines** — the firm's genes
2. **Search** — the mutation mechanism
3. **Selection** — market natural selection
4. **Mutation** — innovation
5. **Retention** — preservation of successful routines

**Dosi** *Technological Paradigms and Technological Trajectories* (1982 *Research Policy*) → *Technical Change and Economic Theory* (Dosi-Freeman-Nelson-Silverberg-Soete 1988):
- *Technological paradigm*: an integrated model of the *knowledge + behavior patterns* of a specific technological area
- *Trajectory*: the *path of progress* within a paradigm
- A paradigm *shift* is the essence of creative destruction (e.g., carriages → automobiles·analog → digital·classical SW → AI)

**Freeman** *Long Waves in the World Economy* (1983·1988) — reinterprets the Kondratieff 50-year cycle as a *cycle of technological revolutions*:
- 1st (1780s textiles·steam)
- 2nd (1840s steel·railways)
- 3rd (1890s electricity·chemistry)
- 4th (1940s oil·automobiles·plastics)
- 5th (1980s ICT)
- 6th (2010s? bio·nano·AI?)

**Romer** *Endogenous Technological Change* (1990 *JPE*) — integrates R&D + knowledge externalities into the neoclassical framework. **Aghion-Howitt** *A Model of Growth Through Creative Destruction* (1992 *Econometrica*) — the first formalization of the Schumpeter model. Both scholars won the Nobel (Romer 2018, Aghion not yet awarded).

> **External-variable 5 axes (Act 2)**:
> - **World currents**: neoliberalism·Japan's rise (1980s)·the collapse of the USSR·China's reform and opening·the 1990s US ICT explosion
> - **Great figures**: Nelson·Winter·Dosi·Freeman·Romer·Aghion·Howitt·Galbraith·Hayek (90s revival)
> - **Technological revolution**: the spread of the PC (IBM 1981·Mac 1984)·standardization of the internet (TCP/IP 1983·the web 1991)·semiconductor Moore's law
> - **National culture**: US Silicon Valley (a full-fledged testing ground for Schumpeter)·Japan's Toyota·Korea's chaebol·SUSTAINING Europe + neoliberal Britain
> - **Social change**: the rise of white-collar labor + technical labor·mass layoffs (1980s rust belt) + new-industry jobs = the first large-scale verification of creative destruction

**Decisive turn**: the 1990s internet explosion → Schumpeter scholarship enters as a *mainstream candidate*. Yet a *school divergence* between DSGE·rational-expectations orthodoxy and evolutionary economics.

### Act 3 — Standardization of endogenous growth + disruptive innovation (2000~2015)

**Christensen** *Innovator's Dilemma* (1997) → 2000s MBA·practitioner standard. The *rational death* of Kodak (film)·Blackberry·Nokia (feature phones)·Blockbuster (video rental). Giant firms die *because they are rational*.

**Acemoglu-Aghion-Zilibotti** (2006) — the optimal policy differs by distance from the technology frontier (innovation stage = market freedom·catch-up stage = industrial policy).

**Clusters + the geography of innovation** (Saxenian *Regional Advantage* 1994·Glaeser·Florida): Silicon Valley vs. Boston Route 128 — same technology·different evolution. The disciplinization of the *evolutionary dynamics* of geographic clusters.

**Long Tail (Anderson 2006)**·**Power Law in Innovation** (Henrich·Boyd): innovation is *power-law distributed* — a few *blockbuster innovations* overwhelm the many ordinary ones.

**Platform evolution** (Eisenmann-Parker-Van Alstyne 2006·Tirole-Rochet 2003): two-sided markets + evolutionary dynamics. An evolutionary model of *winner-takes-all* competition.
