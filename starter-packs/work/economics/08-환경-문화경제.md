# ecological economics

## One-line essence
The *nature, culture, and digital commons* that mainstream economics had left *outside* have entered as the *essence* of the 21st-century economy, and the three axes of *Ostrom commons + ecological limits + platform power* are its integrating tools.

## Intuitive picture
The micro (01) through game theory (04), economic history (05), political economy (06), and development (07) all dealt only with the two spheres of *market + state*. Yet the most enormous domain of the real economy had been *externalized*:

- **Nature**: air, water, climate, soil, biodiversity — *no price → no measurement → no policy → crisis*
- **Culture**: language, custom, art, religion — *transmitted free across generations → its value exposed in the digital age*
- **The commons**: fisheries, forests, groundwater, knowledge, software — *a third governance that is neither market nor state*
- **Digital platforms**: Google, Meta, Amazon, TikTok — *a new power that is neither market nor state*

These four domains are *connected* — Ostrom's commons (1990) broke the *state vs. market* dichotomy, digital platforms operate on top of a *data commons*, and the climate crisis is the integrated crisis of the *global commons*. The *integrating tool* of 21st-century economics is the essence of this note.

- **Ostrom's discovery**: Hardin (1968), "the tragedy of the commons," assumed *an absence of governance over the commons* — real fishing and forest communities are capable of *self-management for centuries*. The 8 design principles. The 2009 Nobel (the first economics Nobel for a woman).

- **Ecological limits**: Infinite GDP growth vs. the Earth's finite resources. Climate 1.5°C, an 80% decline in biodiversity, ocean acidification — *externalization* is no longer possible. The three candidates of *degrowth*, *green growth*, or the *circular economy*.

- **Culture and the creative industries**: Film, games, music, design, software = 14% of 21st-century U.S. GDP, the Korean Wave 5%+ of Korea's GDP. But value measurement, copyright, and AI-generated art are all unsolved.

- **Platforms and data**: Google and Meta are *the largest markets in the world*. But they are *not a market but private governance*. The user = *a new category that is neither commodity nor worker*. Varoufakis's *techno-feudalism* (06).

The integration of these four domains is *the essence of 21st-century economics* — this note is great root 4/6.

## Mathematical structure

**Ostrom's 8 design principles (the commons)**:
1. Clear boundaries (who may use it)
2. Rules congruent with local conditions
3. Collective choice (users decide the rules)
4. Monitoring
5. Graduated sanctions
6. Conflict-resolution mechanisms
7. Recognition of self-organization by external authority
8. Multi-layer governance

→ An *institution-analysis tool* rather than a quantitative model. But the game-theory core (repeated games, reputation mechanisms) applies.

**The Pigouvian tax (internalizing the externality)**:
$$\tau^* = \text{MEC} \quad (\text{marginal external cost})$$
- The carbon tax = $40–$200/tCO₂ (a 5-fold gap between the Stern and Nordhaus estimates)

**The Coase theorem (1960)**: Zero transaction costs + clear property rights → the externality is resolved efficiently by *private bargaining*. But *transaction costs > 0* is the essence → government intervention is needed.

**The Nordhaus DICE model**:
$$\max \sum_t \beta^t U(C_t, T_t), \quad T_{t+1} = T_t + f(\text{emissions}_t)$$
- Utility = consumption + climate. The optimal carbon tax = the social rate of time preference ($\delta$) + the damage function.
- *The Stern (1.5%) vs. Nordhaus (3%) discount-rate gap* → a 10-fold gap in policy recommendations.

**The platform two-sided market (Rochet-Tirole 2003)**:
$$\max_{p_1, p_2} \pi(p_1, p_2) = (p_1 - c_1) D_1(p_1, D_2) + (p_2 - c_2) D_2(p_2, D_1)$$
- *Indirect network effects*: more users on one side → higher value on the other → price asymmetry (free on one side, expensive on the other)
- The scholarly foundation of the Google and Meta advertising models and Apple's App Store 30% fee.

**Measuring creative-industry value (Throsby 2001)**:
$$V = V_{\text{economic}} + V_{\text{cultural}}$$
- $V_{\text{cultural}}$: the six dimensions of aesthetic, spiritual, social, historical, symbolic value, and authenticity. The *quantitative measurement of non-economic value*.

## Historical context (the R-03 economic-variant four acts + integration of the five external-variable axes)

### Act 1 — The emergence of ecology and the commons (1968–1990)

**Boulding** (1966 *Spaceship Earth*) — the earnest recognition of the Earth's finitude. **Hardin** (1968 *The Tragedy of the Commons*) — the commons-tragedy hypothesis. But a *state vs. private ownership* dichotomy — the entryway to Ostrom's critique.

**The Club of Rome** (1972 *The Limits to Growth*) — computer simulation, the limits of resources, population, and environment. A simple model, but *verified with sufficient time* (accurate 50 years later).

**Georgescu-Roegen** (1971 *The Entropy Law and the Economic Process*) — the economy = a process of increasing entropy. *The integration of physics and economics*. **Daly** (1977 *Steady-State Economics*) — the steady-state economy. The formation of the ecological-economics school.

**Pigou** (1920) → **Coase** (1960 *The Problem of Social Cost*) — externality theory in earnest. The foundation of the **Stern Review** (2006).

**Ostrom** *Governing the Commons* (1990) — the 8 design principles. *The commons is not a tragedy but a governance problem*. 2009 Nobel.

> **The five external-variable axes (Act 1)**:
> - **Global currents**: The environmental movement (Earth Day 1970), the 1973 oil shock (the recognition of resource limits), Soviet environmental disasters
> - **Great figures**: Boulding, Hardin, the Club of Rome, Georgescu-Roegen, Daly, Ostrom, Pigou, Coase, Carson (*Silent Spring* 1962)
> - **Technological revolution**: Computer simulation, environmental-measurement technology
> - **National cultures**: The U.S. and Western Europe (the environmental movement), Japan (overcoming industrial pollution), the USSR (environmental disasters)
> - **Social change**: The environmental movement, the antinuclear movement, second-wave feminism (ecofeminism), the formation of green parties (Germany 1980)

**Decisive transition**: 1990 Ostrom + the IPCC (founded 1988) → *the commons and climate became scholarly agendas in earnest*. The next act's environmental economics + policy in earnest.

### Act 2 — Environmental economics + cultural economics in earnest (1990–2008)

**Environmental economics**: The **Stern Review** (2006) — the quantitative report of climate economics. The cost of action, 1% of GDP, vs. the cost of inaction, 5–20% of GDP. The **Nordhaus DICE** model took hold. 2018 Nobel. But the Stern-Nordhaus discount-rate gap (1.5% vs. 3%) → a 10-fold gap in policy recommendations.

**Carbon markets**: The **Kyoto Protocol** (1997) → the EU ETS (from 2005) — the quantitative tool of carbon trading. But the limits of price volatility + carbon leakage.

**Nicholas Stern** (2006 *Stern Review*) → the foundation of UK and EU policy. **Acid rain and the ozone layer (Montreal 1987)** — *success cases of international environmental policy*.

**Cultural economics**: **David Throsby** *Economics and Culture* (2001) — the six dimensions of cultural value. **Richard Florida** *The Rise of the Creative Class* (2002) — the creative class is the essence of urban development. **Caves** *Creative Industries* (2000) — analysis of the film, music, game, and design industries.

**Creative-industry policy**: The UK's *Creative Industries Mapping* (1998) → Korea's *Korean Wave* (from the 2000s) → China's *cultural-industry promotion* (from 2009) — *the creative industries became a standard of national economic policy*.

> **The five external-variable axes (Act 2)**:

> - **Social change**: The mainstreaming of environmental consciousness, creative-industry labor + youth migration to cities, the explosion of digital content

**Decisive transition**: The 2008 financial crisis + the 2007 *iPhone* → *the platform economy in earnest*. The entryway to the next act's digital and platform scholarship.
