# political economy

## One-line essence
The economy is not a *market mechanism* but a *political construct in which power + values + institutions are entangled*, and 250 years of intellectual history are the answers of eight giants to *how to interpret* this essence.

## Intuitive picture
Economics is not the discipline of the *economy* but the discipline of the *political economy*. Smith was a *moral philosopher*, Marx a *philosopher and revolutionary*, Keynes a *diplomat and treasury adviser*, Friedman a *monetarist politician*, Hayek a *critic of totalitarianism*, Sen a *development ethicist*, Stiglitz a *critic of globalization*, Piketty a *political scientist of distribution*. Not one of them was *a person who did only economics*.

- **Smith (1723–90)**: *The Theory of Moral Sentiments* (1759) + *The Wealth of Nations* (1776). The invisible hand assumes that *sympathy + moral restraint* make the market work. The Smith cited by neoliberalism is a *half Smith*.
- **Marx (1818–83)**: *Das Kapital* (1867). Capital = a social relation, labor value = socially necessary labor time, crisis = the internal contradiction of capital dynamics. Not a mere critic but *the most precise analyst of capitalism*.
- **Keynes (1883–1946)**: *The General Theory* (1936). Micro rationality is macro irrationality. The designer of the *grand compromise* of government + market. The architect of Bretton Woods (1944).
- **Friedman (1912–2006)**: *Capitalism and Freedom* (1962). The money supply + the free market = political freedom. Adviser to the Chilean military government (from 1973) — the critique of *separating economic freedom from political freedom*.
- **Hayek (1899–1992)**: *The Road to Serfdom* (1944). Central planning = the road to totalitarianism. Price = the *integrating signal of dispersed knowledge*. The market ≠ efficiency; the market = *a procedure of knowledge discovery*.
- **Sen (1933–)**: *Development as Freedom* (1999). Development = not GDP but *capability*. Poverty = *the deprivation of freedom*. 1998 Nobel.
- **Stiglitz (1943–)**: *Globalization and Its Discontents* (2002). Information asymmetry collapses the market itself. A critique of the neoliberal policies of the IMF and the World Bank. 2001 Nobel.
- **Piketty (1971–)**: *Capital in the 21st Century* (2014). $r > g$ → the concentration of wealth is automatic. He recommends a progressive capital tax. *Distribution is the essence of the macro*.

These eight gave *different answers* but all asked *the same question* — *what is the market, who defines it, and for whom is it*. Political economy is *a discipline whose essence is not the answer but the question*.

## Mathematical structure

Political economy centers on *conceptual structure* more than equations. But the core quantitative pieces:

**Marx's value equation**:
$$W = c + v + s$$
- $c$: constant capital (machines, raw materials), $v$: variable capital (wages), $s$: surplus value
- The rate of profit $r = s/(c+v)$, the rate of surplus value $s' = s/v$
- *The law of the tendential fall of the rate of profit*: the organic composition of capital ($c/v$) rises → $r$ falls → crisis

**Hayek's price signal**:
$$p^* \in \arg\min_{(\text{coordination cost})} \sum_i \mathbb{E}[\text{misallocation} | \text{decentralized info}]$$
- The price is the *optimal signal* that integrates *dispersed knowledge*. For central planning the cost of information integration is infinite.

**Sen's capability**:
$$\text{Wellbeing} = f(\text{functionings}, \text{capabilities})$$
- functioning = the *actual act* (eating, reading, moving)
- capability = the *possibility of acting* (freedom)
- HDI (Human Development Index, UNDP 1990) — Sen's influence, combining GDP, education, and life expectancy

**Piketty's capital dynamics**:
$$\beta = K/Y = s/g, \quad \alpha = r \cdot \beta$$
- $r > g$ → $\beta$ rises → the capital share $\alpha$ rises → the concentration of wealth
- In both the 19th and 21st centuries $\beta \approx 6$; the mid-20th century was the *exception* (wars, taxes)

**Stiglitz's information asymmetry** (adverse selection + moral hazard):
$$\text{Adverse Selection: } E[Q | p] < E[Q] \text{ (lemons market)}$$
$$\text{Moral Hazard: } \text{Effort}(\text{contract}) < \text{Effort}^{\text{first best}}$$
- The *intrinsic limit* of the market itself. A violation of the neoliberal assumptions.

## Why it matters

Political economy is *the political unconscious of economics*. Every micro (01), macro (02), behavioral (03), and game (04) model presupposes *implicit value assumptions* — political economy is the discipline that *exposes and criticizes* those assumptions.

Deeper positioning:
- **The political foundation of the four theoretical disciplines (01–04)**: Utility maximization = *the 18th-century liberal image of the human*, general equilibrium = *Pareto efficiency (indifferent to distribution)*, rational expectations = *the assumption of information equality*, game theory = *the assumption of player equality*. All are the quantitative codification of *political assumptions*
- **The intellectual foundation of real policy**: Reagan-Thatcher = Friedman and Hayek / the New Deal and the welfare state = Keynes and Polanyi / the developmental state = Sen and List / Brexit and Trump = the critique of neoliberalism + protectionism / Lula and Sanders = Piketty's distribution politics / China's "common prosperity" = a Marx + developmental-state mixture. **Every political movement is a direct heir of political-economic thought**
- **The limits against real markets**:
  - *Western-centeredness (the externalization of the colonial)*: after Marx, the critique of colonialism comes in earnest in development economics (07)
  - *White-male-centeredness (the externalization of feminism)*: feminist economics emerged in the late 20th century
  - *Human-centeredness (the externalization of ecology)*: ecological economics emerged in the 21st century
  - *The externalization of intergenerational justice*: the climate crisis and youth politics in earnest in the 21st century
- **The emerging political economy**:
  - **Acemoglu-Robinson** *Why Nations Fail* (2012) — institutions (extractive vs. inclusive) are the essence of development
  - **Mazzucato** *The Entrepreneurial State* (2013) — innovation is made by the state (every core technology of the iPhone was state-funded)
  - **Raworth** *Doughnut Economics* (2017) — the safe zone between the social foundation and the ecological ceiling
  - **Varoufakis** *Technofeudalism* (2023) — the AI era is not capitalism but *techno-feudalism*

## Historical context (the R-03 economic-variant four acts + integration of the five external-variable axes)

### Act 1 — Classical political economy (1776–1900)

**Smith** (1776 *The Wealth of Nations*): free trade, the division of labor, the invisible hand. But *The Theory of Moral Sentiments* (1759) — moral restraint is the market's precondition. **Ricardo** (1817 *Principles*): comparative advantage, the labor theory of value, the theory of rent. **Mill** (1848 *Principles*): the apex of classical economics + a synthesis of social reform. **Marx** (1867 *Das Kapital*): capital dynamics, labor value, alienation, crisis theory. *Not a critique but the most precise analysis*.

> **The five external-variable axes (Act 1)**:
> - **Global currents**: The British Industrial Revolution, colonial trade, the 1848 European revolutions, the American Civil War, German unification
> - **Great figures**: Smith, Ricardo, Mill, Marx + Mill's father (James Mill of the East India Company) + Friedrich Engels (Marx's patron and a capitalist)
> - **Technological revolution**: The First Industrial Revolution (steam, spinning, railways), entry into the Second (electricity, chemicals)
> - **National cultures**: British free trade, Germany's List protectionism, America's Hamilton vs. Jefferson, Russian economic backwardness + populism
> - **Social change**: The formation of the working class, the Chartist movement, the 1848 revolutions, the International Workingmen's Association (1864), first-wave feminism (Wollstonecraft 1792)

**Decisive transition**: Marx's 1867 *Das Kapital* was *the deepest critique of classical liberalism*. Capitalism = social relations + internal contradictions + crisis dynamics. The entryway to the next act's *political responses* (the socialist movement + social-reform liberalism).

### Act 2 — Neoclassicism + Keynes + the critics (1900–1973)

**The neoclassical formation**: Marshall (1890), Pigou (1920), Walras → in earnest in the micro (01). Political economy narrowed into *economics* — the *political* part was split off.

**Keynes** (1936 *General Theory*): micro rationality ≠ macro rationality. The grand compromise of government + market. The architect of Bretton Woods.

**Polanyi** (1944 *The Great Transformation*): The market economy is not a *historical product* but a *political imposition*. Labor, land, and money are *fictitious commodities* — marketize them and society collapses. A critique parallel to Keynes.

**Hayek** (1944 *The Road to Serfdom*): Central planning = the road to totalitarianism. Founded the *Mont Pelerin Society* (1947) — the incubator of neoliberal thought. **Mises**, Hayek, Friedman, Stigler, and Buchanan were all members.

**Schumpeter** (1942 *Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy*): *Creative destruction* — the essence of capitalism is not equilibrium but *the storm of innovation*. But he predicted socialism *would replace* capitalism (while pessimistic about that outcome).

**Galbraith** (1958 *The Affluent Society*) — the affluent society's *artificial wants created by advertising + public poverty*. A synthesis of Keynes + institutionalism.

> **The five external-variable axes (Act 2)**:
> - **Global currents**: World Wars I and II, the Russian Revolution, the Great Depression, the Cold War, colonial liberation, the antiwar movement
> - **Great figures**: Keynes, Polanyi, Hayek, Schumpeter, Galbraith + Mises, Friedman, Buchanan, Robinson (post-Cambridge) + Veblen (institutionalism)
> - **Technological revolution**: Electricity, the automobile, aviation, nuclear power, television, semiconductors (the first half)
> - **National cultures**: The Anglo-American Keynesian consensus, the Western European social market, the Japanese developmental state, Soviet planning, the Chinese communist revolution, Latin American dependency-theory candidates
> - **Social change**: The formation of the welfare state, women's labor, the civil-rights movement, second-wave feminism, the antiwar movement, the New Left (1968)

**Decisive transition**: The 1973 oil shock → cracks in the Keynesian consensus → Mont Pelerin thought rose as a *policy-standard candidate*. The Hayek 1974 and Friedman 1976 Nobels simultaneously provided *scholarly legitimation*.

### Act 3 — The return to the market + the information and development critiques (1973–2008)

**Friedman** (the 1980 *Free to Choose* TV series) → the policy foundation of **Reagan-Thatcher**. The proposition monetarism + the free market = political freedom was turned into a political tool.

**The Hayek revival**: Hayek's 1974 Nobel → the republication of *The Road to Serfdom* → after the collapse of the Eastern bloc (1989–91), *the end of history* (Fukuyama 1992).

**Sen** (1981 *Poverty and Famines* + 1985 *Commodities and Capabilities* + 1999 *Development as Freedom*): the critique of GDP + the capability approach. With **Mahbub ul Haq** he developed the *HDI* (UNDP 1990). **Development ethics** took hold.

**Stiglitz** (1970s information economics, 1990s chief economist of the World Bank, 2002 *Globalization and Its Discontents*): the market limits of information asymmetry + the critique of IMF and World Bank neoliberalism. 2001 Nobel.

**Acemoglu-Robinson** (2006 *Economic Origins of Dictatorship and Democracy*, 2012 *Why Nations Fail*): *Institutions determine development*. Inclusive vs. extractive institutions. 2024 Nobel (together with Robinson and Johnson).

**Mariana Mazzucato** (2013 *The Entrepreneurial State*): Innovation is made by the state — exposing that Mont Pelerin neoliberalism *contradicts the actual history*.

> **The five external-variable axes (Act 3)**:
> - **Global currents**: The oil shock, Reagan-Thatcher, the Soviet collapse, the rise of China, neoliberal globalization, 9/11, the Iraq War, the financial crisis
> - **Great figures**: Friedman and Hayek (orthodoxy), Sen, Stiglitz, Acemoglu, Robinson, Mazzucato (critique), Krugman (both sides) + Lula and Chavez (the Latin American left), Buchanan (public choice), Coase (social cost)
> - **Technological revolution**: The PC, the internet, the web, biotechnology, financial engineering, the digitalization explosion
> - **National cultures**: Anglo-American neoliberalism + Chinese market socialism, India's 1991 liberalization, the Latin American *Pink Tide*, Islamic finance + the settling of the Southeast Asian developmental state
> - **Social change**: Middle-class stagnation, the revival of inequality (Piketty's 1980 threshold), globalization's losers, third-wave feminism, LGBT, the environmental movement + the IPCC

**Decisive transition**: The 2008 financial crisis → the *critical political economy* of Sen, Stiglitz, and Acemoglu rose as an orthodoxy candidate. But *the policy change was quantitative easing + austerity + bailouts*, a *preservation of existing power* — the beginning of the *crisis of democracy* between scholarly critique and political powerlessness.

### Act 4 — Distribution, ecology, technology (2008–present)

**Piketty** (2014 *Capital* + 2020 *Capital and Ideology*): $r > g$ + *intellectual history*. He recommends a progressive capital tax and an international wealth tax. *Distribution returns as the essence of political economy*.

**Raworth** *Doughnut Economics* (2017): the safe zone between the social foundation and the ecological ceiling. Adopted as official policy in Amsterdam and Copenhagen. An heir of **Daly**'s ecological economics (from the 1990s).

**Varoufakis** (2023 *Technofeudalism*): the AI and platform era is not capitalism but *techno-feudalism* — Amazon, Google, and Microsoft are the *lords*, users the *serfs*. An heir of **Wallerstein**'s world-systems.

**Mazzucato** (2018 *The Value of Everything*, 2021 *Mission Economy*): separating value creation vs. value capture. She recommends mission-oriented policy (Apollo, the New Deal, climate). Influencing EU and UK policy.

**Climate political economy**: **Pollin** (eco-socialism), the **Stern Review** (2006 climate economics), **Nordhaus**'s DICE (the social cost of carbon), **Daly**'s steady-state economics. The debate of the *growth imperative* vs. *resource limits* in earnest.

**Decolonial political economy** (Bhambra, Rodney, Chibber): capitalism grew on top of *colonies + slaves + women's unpaid labor* — a critique of the externalization in economic history.

**Feminist political economy** (Folbre, Waring, Federici): the GDP externalization of unpaid housework + care labor — the politics of economic measurement itself.

**AI political economy** (Acemoglu-Restrepo, Brynjolfsson, Korinek): AI as *labor replacement + productivity distribution* — a new political economy forming in earnest.

> **The five external-variable axes (Act 4)**:
> - **Global currents**: The 2008 crisis, European debt, the Arab Spring, Brexit, Trump, COVID, the Russia-Ukraine war, climate, AI
> - **Great figures**: Piketty, Mazzucato, Raworth, Varoufakis, Acemoglu, Sen (tradition → distribution, ecology, technology heirs) + Bukele and Milei (left and right populism)
> - **Technological revolution**: AI, blockchain, climate technology, gene editing, quantum. **AI *transforms political economy itself*** (large-scale automation + algorithmic governance)
> - **National cultures**: The U.S., the EU, China, India + the Latin American leftward return + Islam + Africa + the AI giants + the Bitcoin nation (El Salvador)
> - **Social change**: The peak of inequality, the intergenerational, climate justice, digital labor, AI jobs, Gen MZ, aging, fourth-wave feminism

**Decisive transition** (present): AI + climate + multipolarity simultaneously → *the absence of a ninth giant* (Smith, Marx, Keynes, Friedman, Hayek, Sen, Stiglitz, Piketty — all eight are *thinkers of an already-passed political economy*). 21st-century political economy is the era of the *absence of a learned giant* — the domain where this note enters academic tracking mode.
